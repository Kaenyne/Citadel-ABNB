\PageTitle{Systematic macro-to-equity waterfall}{A causal chain with explicit information clocks}

\Callout{ICNavy}{The unit of work is an event packet: one target event, one offset-aware cutoff, one eligible evidence set, four linked forecasts, and a later immutable resolution.}

\vspace{3pt}
\Step{0}{Event clock}{Fix event, target metric, guided period, unit, and $as\_of\_utc$. Evidence must have verified first availability strictly before cutoff; equality fails. Retain every rejection and reason.}
\Step{1}{Macro regime}{Classify growth, inflation, FX, rates, employment, and consumer balance-sheet conditions using vintages actually observable at cutoff. State regime uncertainty, not a single narrative.}
\Step{2}{Travel system}{Translate macro conditions into travel \textbf{demand}, carrier/room \textbf{capacity}, market \textbf{pricing}, and regulatory \textbf{supply}. Align geography, frequency, lag, and booking-versus-stay timing.}
\Step{3}{ABNB operating state}{Nowcast nights and experiences booked, ADR, gross booking value, take rate, revenue, and margins. Produce an operating P10/P50/P90; do not jump directly from macro to guidance.}
\Step{4}{Management policy}{Model how management maps the operating distribution into the formal range: conservatism, range width, seasonality, known headwinds, prior cadence, and source-bound language.}
\Step{5}{Market expectations}{Freeze contemporaneous consensus, dispersion, bank expectations, implied move, and relevant positioning. A later-restated estimate is not point-in-time consensus.}
\Step{6}{Surprise vector}{Construct joint actual and guidance surprises for comparable metrics and periods: $\mathbf{s}_t=[s^{\mathrm{actual}}_t,\ s^{\mathrm{guide}}_t]$. Raw sequential guidance change is not a surprise.}
\Step{7}{Market state}{Condition on valuation, factor exposures, crowding/positioning, implied volatility, macro regime, and recent price path. These variables mediate how the same surprise is priced.}
\Step{8}{Reaction distribution}{Estimate downside/base/upside return distributions and decision thresholds, net of the implied move and benchmark. Keep the reaction forecast separate from the guidance forecast.}
\Step{9}{Resolve and learn}{Attach reviewed actuals, issued guidance, consensus surprises, and price outcomes without changing the frozen forecast. Attribute error to economic, policy, expectations, or reaction layers.}

% PAGE 6
\PageTitle{Where the investable edge must come from}{Forecast the residual, define the surprise, then price the state}

\Section{1. Remove the easy seasonal level}
\begin{align*}
G_t &= \widehat{G}^{\mathrm{outside}}_t + \Delta^{\mathrm{policy}}_t + \varepsilon_t,\\[-2pt]
\text{research target} \quad R^G_t &= G_t-\widehat{G}^{\mathrm{outside}}_t.
\end{align*}
The outside view includes same-guided-quarter seasonality, prior guidance, historical range width, eligible macro/travel inputs, and source-bound policy priors. Candidate features must improve the out-of-sample distribution of $R^G_t$, not merely track raw $G_t$.

\Section{2. Compare like-for-like with contemporaneous expectations}
\begin{equation*}
s^{\mathrm{guide}}_t = \frac{\widehat{G}_t-C^{\mathrm{guide}}_t}{\mathrm{scale}(C_t)},
\qquad
s^{\mathrm{actual}}_t = \frac{A_t-C^{\mathrm{actual}}_t}{\mathrm{scale}(C_t)}.
\end{equation*}
Consensus $C_t$ must match metric, guided period, currency basis, and publication cutoff. Estimate dispersion and revision path matter because identical midpoint surprises can carry different information content.

\Section{3. Price the joint surprise vector, not a single headline}
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{s}_t=
\begin{bmatrix}s^{\mathrm{actual}}_t & s^{\mathrm{guide}}_t\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
r^{\mathrm{excess}}_{t+1}\sim
f\!\left(\mathbf{s}_t,\ \mathrm{positioning}_t,\ \mathrm{implied\ move}_t,\
\mathrm{valuation}_t,\ \mathrm{macro\ regime}_t\right).
\end{equation*}
This structure permits beat/raise, beat/cut, miss/raise, and miss/cut states. It also acknowledges that a ``good'' guide can underperform when already priced or when valuation and macro beta dominate.

\Section{4. Keep decisions downstream of distributions}
