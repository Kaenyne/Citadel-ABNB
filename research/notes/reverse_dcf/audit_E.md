Auditor: Claude (independent), 13 Sep 2026. Scope: `analysis/src/reverse_dcf/E_positioning_card.py`, the four CSVs under `data/processed/reverse_dcf/E/`, and section 10 of `research/notes/2026-09-13_market-implied-model.md`. Inputs re-read from `16_consensus_at_print_merged.csv`, `02_kpi_panel_quarterly.csv`, `30_quarterly_pnl.csv`, `29_q4_2026_bridge.csv`, `02_guidance_ledger.csv`, `16_consensus_additions.csv`, `04_consensus_sources.csv`, `market_implied_params.json`, `C_coefficients_used.csv`, and the 10 Sep nights-baseline reconciliation note. Findings CSV: `data/processed/reverse_dcf/audit/audit_E_findings.csv`. Auditor script (not committed): scratchpad `audit_E_checks.py`.

## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES

No blockers. The script re-runs and all four CSVs reproduce byte for byte; the joint-solve prices, the sign-rule coefficients, the consensus arithmetic and the sign counts all check by hand. The direction of the card's conclusion ("on nights, yes; on revenue and valuation, no") survives. Seven material findings, all about what the numbers mean rather than how they were computed:

1. The "price-implied NTM equals the team's bridge to within $2m" headline is an artefact. The script compounds the team's nights, ADR, FX and residual terms multiplicatively; the team's own P&L file adds them. Against the team's actual revenue path ($14,818m) the price-implied NTM ($14,860m) is $43m higher, the uplift is +0.32pt of nights, and the price-implied 3Q26 reads 10.61%, one basis point over the dead band into "acceleration" rather than "flat".
2. The "team base" the card scales is WS10's 10.29 / 9.94 nights path, which the team retired on 10 Sep in favour of 9.9 / 8.9. The team's revenue path was built on the retired nights. Across the four defensible conventions the price-implied 3Q26 nights runs 9.9 to 10.6%, which straddles the dead band. The sign at the price is not identified and should be labelled JUDGEMENT.
3. The Street's nights bar carries management's guide direction in 16 of 16 prints where a guide exists. The three prior "accelerating bars" were three accelerating guides, all met. The card presents the bar as Street positioning; it is the guide. The team's call is a bet on the first downside miss of a management nights guide in 16 resolved guides.
4. The Street NTM row uses the management-delivered FY26 as the denominator for the 1H27 share. On the Street's own FY26 base the Street row reads 13.4% (not 13.0%), implies 3Q26 nights of 10.75% ("acceleration", not "flat"), and reprices +1.4% (not +0.3%).
5. The "ex-NA lap" ladder row does not put 4Q26 at 8.1%. The script shifts every NTM quarter by -0.39pt (4Q26 lands at 9.55%, 1H27 at 7.8%). Correct constructions give -0.4% to -1.7% (or -3.9% if the lap also hits 1H27 one for one) against the script's -1.1%.
6. The fundamental column's level depends on a units choice (the +1.37pp proxy bias, which A found unstable) and the "combined" column adds a total-return sign rule to a fundamental term (double counting the estimate component) using a one-sided rule rather than a simple sum.
7. The sign-history table carries 3Q21 as "flat" because its comparator is missing (a NaN comparison falls through to "flat"); the flat bucket is 1 print (3Q25, +0.6%), not 2 (+6.8%); the scored sample is 16 with a comparator (17 rows), not 15.

Eight minor findings follow. Every number quoted below is MEASURED from the files named unless marked.

The card's table shows "flat (3Q21, 3Q25): 2 prints, mean +6.8%". 3Q21 has no prior-quarter growth rate (`prev_growth` is NaN), and in the script `street_g > prev + 0.25` and `street_g < prev - 0.25` are both False against NaN, so the row falls into "flat" by default, and the same happens to its `printed` column. SYNTHESIS.md line 34 says "the fourth time in 16 prints", so the two documents already disagree. Dead-band sensitivity: the three accelerating bars are the same at 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0pt bands; the count is not band-dependent.

**Vendor and confidence.** The merged file's `cons_revenue_vendor` and `confidence` columns describe the revenue and next-quarter consensus, not the nights bar. From `04_consensus_sources.csv` and `16_consensus_additions.csv` the nights series is: StreetAccount via CNBC for 3Q21 to 4Q25 (13 prints, plus FactSet for the dropped 1Q21), LSEG via CNBC for 1Q26, and a Zacks seven-analyst average for 2Q26 (145.44m, filled in by WS16, whose own note says "the vendor is Zacks, not StreetAccount, so it is not strictly the same series as the other 17 nights rows"). The 3Q26E and 4Q26E bars are Bloomberg FA (4 Sep). So the "Street bar" series is spliced across four vendors and the card does not say so; C's 2Q26 case study labels the 145.44m "StreetAccount", which is wrong. None of the three accelerating-bar prints is low-confidence on the nights number: 4Q21's "medium" flag is about its unattributed next-quarter revenue consensus (the notes column says so). 4Q21 is, however, the reopening comp (a +61.9% bar on the 4Q20 trough) and sits outside C's pre-stated 3Q22-on sample; on the post-3Q22 sample the count is 2 accelerating bars in 13.

**Is the Bloomberg 148.9m like for like?** No, and it matters at the margin. A 3Q26 bar below 147.75m (0.77% under Bloomberg's) would read "flat" by the card's own rule. The historical nights surprise column has a mean absolute value of about 1.5%, so a vendor-to-vendor gap of 0.8% is inside the noise of the series. No StreetAccount, LSEG or Zacks 3Q26 nights bar was retrievable (`04_current_consensus.csv`, 6 Sep: "NO published Q3-2026 ... nights consensus was retrievable"). The "fourth accelerating bar" therefore rests on one vendor with 0.86pt of cushion over the dead band. It is MEASURED on the Bloomberg number and JUDGEMENT that it is the same quantity the history scores.

**Alternative comparator 1: the bar vs the prior-year same-quarter growth rate.** Scoreable for 13 prints (4Q22 on). The bar accelerates on 2 (1Q26, 2Q26; both printed acceleration, mean day-1 +7.4%) and decelerates on 11. The 3Q26E bar (+11.45 vs 3Q25's +8.79) accelerates on this comparator too, and so does the 4Q26E bar (+10.09 vs +9.82). So the "rare" label survives on a count, but on this comparator the last three bars in a row are accelerating, which is a different story from "the first since 4Q24".

**Alternative comparator 2: the bar vs the company's own guide direction.** This is the one that undoes the framing. From `02_guidance_ledger.csv`, management gave a next-quarter nights guide at every print from 2Q21 on. Matching each bar's sign to the guide's direction: 16 of 16 agree. The three accelerating bars follow the three accelerating guides ("approximate Q4 2019 levels" at 3Q21; "a modest sequential increase in the year-over-year growth rate" at 2Q23; "higher than Q3 2024" at 3Q24). Every decelerating bar follows a "moderate", "below", "stable" or bucket guide, and in the two bucket guides (4Q25 "mid-single-digit" 4 to 6, 1Q26 "high-single-digit" 7 to 9) the bar sat in the top of the bucket (5.95, 8.85). The 3Q26 bar (11.45) sits in the upper half of the "low double-digit" bucket (10 to 12, midpoint 11.0). On the bucket midpoint the guide itself is a +0.66pt acceleration; on the literal low end (10.0) it is a deceleration. So the Street has added about 0.45pt to the guide midpoint, consistent with its habit in the two prior bucket guides, both of which printed above the bucket top.

What this means for claim 1: the Street's bar contains no positioning information beyond management's guide. "The Street has implied acceleration three times, all three printed acceleration" is the same fact as "management has guided nights acceleration three times and delivered three times". More broadly, of 16 resolved nights guides in the ledger, none was missed to the downside (15 met, 2 bucket guides beaten, 1 directional guide missed upward at 1Q26 "slightly decelerate", which printed +10.34 vs +9.15). The team's decelerating base case is a bet on the first downside miss of a management nights guide. That is a legitimate bet (the 10 Sep bridge note says no pattern-based scenario produces a Q3 acceleration), but it is not the bet the card describes.

## 3. Price-implied 2H26 path (task 3)

**Inputs.** Confirmed from `30_quarterly_pnl.csv` base: 3Q26 nights_yoy 10.29, 4Q26 9.94, 1Q27 7.95, 2Q27 8.45; adr_exfx 3.0 in all four; fx_pp +3.00 / -0.43 / -1.03 / -0.80; resid_pp +0.22 / -0.51 / 0 / 0. Prior-year revenue bases 4,095 / 2,778 / 2,678 / 3,608 are exactly what the file's revenue_musd / (1 + rev_yoy/100) returns. Price-implied NTM: EV $92,010m, quadratic root g 12.930%, NTM $14,860.5m at 17.65x, price back-solves to $170.19. All as the card states.

**The bridge arithmetic.** The team's file is additive: rev_yoy = nights_yoy + adr_exfx + fx_pp + resid_pp (3Q26: 10.29 + 3.0 + 3.0 + 0.22 = 16.51, $4,771.1m). The script compounds the same terms: 4,095 x 1.1029 x 1.03 x 1.0322 = $4,801.7m. Per quarter:

| Quarter | Team file revenue | E's multiplicative rebuild | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3Q26 | 4,771.1 | 4,801.7 | +30.6 |
| 4Q26 | 3,110.8 | 3,116.2 | +5.4 |
| 1Q27 | 2,943.7 | 2,947.0 | +3.3 |
| 2Q27 | 3,992.3 | 3,998.0 | +5.8 |
| NTM | 14,817.8 | 14,862.8 | +45.0 |

So the "team base" NTM the card scales ($14,863m) is $45m above the team's own NTM ($14,818m, +12.61%). The price-implied $14,860m is $43m (0.29pp of NTM growth) above the team's path, not $2m below it. On the additive arithmetic the uniform nights uplift that closes the gap is +0.32pt (at $131.6m of NTM revenue per point), which puts the price-implied 3Q26 at 10.61%, one basis point over the 10.59 dead-band edge into "acceleration". On a 2H26-only uplift it is +0.61pt (10.90%). Under E's own multiplicative arithmetic the 2H26-only uplift (-0.03pt) vs uniform (-0.02pt) is immaterial, because the gap there is $2m. The 4Q26 price-implied revenue is $3,115 to 3,119m under every construction, below the Street's $3,154 to 3,200m; that part of claim 2 holds.

**Which is "the team base"?** The 10 Sep reconciliation note (`research/notes/2026-09-10_nights-baseline-reconciliation.md`, recommendation 1) adopted 3Q26 +9.9% / 4Q26 +8.9% as the team nights baseline and explicitly retired WS10's 10.29 / 9.94 to the top of the band. `30_quarterly_pnl.csv` base still carries 10.29 / 9.94 (nights_m 147.35 = 133.6 x 1.1029; 134.02 = 121.9 x 1.0994), and `29_q4_2026_bridge.csv` shows the $3,111m 4Q26 revenue built on the WS10 nights ("4Q26 base 9.9%"). So the brief's "4Q26 nights +8.9%, rev $3,111m" pairs the new nights baseline with a revenue bridge built on the old one, and the card inherits that. The card's row label ("+10.3% on the pnl bridge / +9.9% baseline") acknowledges the two numbers without resolving which the price is being compared with. The price-implied 3Q26 nights under the four defensible conventions:

| Convention | Uplift vs base | Price-implied 3Q26 | Sign vs 2Q26 (10.34, 0.25 band) |
|---|---|---|---|
| WS10 nights 10.29, multiplicative (the card) | -0.02 | 10.27 | flat |
| WS10 nights 10.29, additive (the team's file) | +0.32 | 10.61 | acceleration (marginal) |
| Baseline 9.9, same uplift as the card | -0.02 | 9.88 | deceleration |
| Baseline 9.9 / 8.9 in the additive bridge, other terms unchanged (team NTM $14,774m, +12.27%) | +0.66 | 10.56 | flat (at the edge) |

The range is 9.9 to 10.6: the sign of the price-implied 3Q26 print is inside the resolution of the method and depends on which team number and which arithmetic is used. The honest statement is "the price is consistent with a 3Q26 nights print anywhere from 9.9 to 10.6%, i.e. flat against 2Q26 within the method's resolution", and it is JUDGEMENT, not the MEASURED "flat" the card's table implies. The conclusion the card draws from it (the price does not carry the Street's +11.45%) survives in every row: even the most generous convention lands 0.85pt below the Street's bar.

**The Street NTM construction.** 4,744 + 3,177 + 15,745 x (6,286 / 14,231.3) = $14,875.6m reproduces. The denominator is the management-delivered FY26, so the row mixes the Street's FY27 with management's FY26. On the Street's own FY26 ($14,130m, brief) the 1H27 share is 0.4449 and the Street NTM is $14,925.5m (+13.42%): uplift +0.46pt, price-implied 3Q26 10.75% ("acceleration"), joint-solve price $172.58, fundamental +1.4%. On $14,160m: $14,910.6m, +1.1%. So the card's "Street: flat, +0.3%" is the least favourable of three reasonable constructions for the claim that the Street is positioned above the price; the consistent one says the Street NTM is $50 to 65m above the price-implied NTM, not $15m, and the Street path reprices +1.1 to +1.4% on fundamentals. This strengthens the "on nights, yes" half and weakens "the price pays for the Street" in section 1 of the market note by about a point of NTM growth.

## 4. Repricing ladder (task 4)

**Prices.** Closed form price = ((a + b g) x LTM revenue x (1 + g/100) x LTM margin + net cash) / shares with a 12.533, b 0.3955, LTM revenue 13,159, margin 0.35086, net cash 9,593, shares 597: Street g 13.045 -> $170.745; delivered g 14.023 -> $175.493; team (E's NTM) g 12.948 -> $170.276; ex-NA (E's construction) g 12.545 -> $168.338. All four reproduce to the cent. One point of NTM growth is worth $4.85 at the price; 1.37pp is $6.66.

**Team path on the team's own arithmetic.** With the team's NTM of $14,818m (g 12.61) the joint-solve price is $168.63 and the fundamental repricing -0.9%, not +0.05%. On the reconciled nights baseline in the additive bridge ($14,774m) it is $167.02, -1.9%. The card's "fundamental ~0" for the team path is the multiplicative artefact from section 3.

**Realised-units column and the choice of primary.** `joint_solve_price_if_path_is_realised_units_usd` = price(g + 1.37) adds $6.66 to every row (Street $177.41, delivered $182.24, team $176.94). The +1.37pp is A's mean proxy bias over 12 vintages, which audit A found to be unstable (overstates by 1.5 to 4.8pp in 2022-24, understates by 1 to 3pp in 2025; RMSE 2.9). So the level of the fundamental column is uncertain by about plus or minus $7 to 15 in either direction, and presenting the no-bias version as primary is a choice, not a measurement. Audit A asked for the realised-units reading to be carried alongside at every price; the card carries it as one sentence under the table. That is partly consistent. What survives the units choice is the ordering and the differences between rows (Street minus team +0.3%, delivered minus team +3.1%, on E's arithmetic; +1.3% and +4.0% on the team's), because the offset is common to all rows. Fix: present the fundamental column as differences against the team path, or as a range across the two units, and label the level JUDGEMENT.

**Sign-rule coefficients.** `C_coefficients_used.csv`: S1_post2022 c -0.452, b_sign 5.6573 (n 14); S1_ex_reopening c -0.665, b_sign 3.3547 (n 16). Reactions: accelerating +5.205 / +2.690, decelerating -6.109 / -4.020. Match the ladder and the C note.

**"Combined" is not a simple sum.** The script uses `min(r_fund, 0) + sign` when the sign is negative and `max(r_fund, 0) + sign` when positive: the fundamental term is included only when it has the same sign as the sign rule. In three rows this equals the simple sum; in the team row it drops the +0.05% (simple sum -6.06 / -3.97 vs the CSV's -6.11 / -4.02). Numerically immaterial here, but the rule is undocumented and always makes the combined figure more extreme in the direction of the sign rule. The bigger conceptual point: the sign-rule coefficients are total day-1 returns on prints that also carried estimate revisions (WS12: about 29% of the absolute print move is estimate change on average), so adding a fundamental (estimate) term to them double counts. It matters most for the delivered row (+5.8 to +8.3% adds a +3.1% estimate repricing to a sign-rule mean that already contains the estimate revisions of past accelerating prints). Fix: quote the sign rule and the fundamental term side by side and do not add them, or present the fundamental term as the "estimate" component and the sign rule as the "multiple" component with WS12's 71 / 29 split as the bridge between them.

**Ex-NA lap row.** The label says "3Q26 nights 9.9% and 4Q26 8.1%". The script sets d = 9.9 - 10.29 = -0.39 and applies it to all four NTM quarters, so the construction has 4Q26 at 9.55% and 1H27 at 7.8%, and the `- py_rev["4Q26"] * 0` term does nothing. That is not a representation of a 4Q26 8.1% path. Alternatives, all with 3Q26 at 9.9% and E's multiplicative bridge elsewhere:

| Construction | 4Q26 revenue | NTM | Joint-solve price | Fundamental |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Script (uniform -0.39pt; 4Q26 at 9.55%) | 3,104 | 14,810 | $168.34 | -1.1% |
| 4Q26 at 8.1% on the bridge's own one-for-one nights term | 3,064 | 14,794 | $167.75 | -1.4% |
| 4Q26 at C's repair mapping $3,102m (team elasticity 0.38%/pt) | 3,102 | 14,832 | $169.14 | -0.6% |
| 4Q26 $3,102m and the lap also -0.8pt in 1H27 | 3,102 | 14,780 | $167.27 | -1.7% |
| 4Q26 at 8.1% one for one and the lap -1.84pt in 1H27 | 3,064 | 14,676 | $163.48 | -3.9% |

The script's -1.1% sits inside the range but for the wrong reason. Two notes on C's $3,102m: the 0.38%/pt "team elasticity" C derived by comparing the brief's 8.9% / $3,111m with the delivered 10.5% / $3,130m is an artefact of the base mismatch in section 3 (the $3,111m was built at 9.94% nights, not 8.9%), so on the team's own bridge a 4Q26 at 8.1% is $3,064m and the C-repair number is too high. And whether the NA lap persists into 1H27 is the whole content of the team's FY27 thesis (the lap note calls FY27 the trade), so a row labelled "ex-NA lap" that leaves 1H27 at the base path understates what that scenario means for NTM. Fix: relabel the row to what it computes, or rebuild it quarter by quarter and show the 1H27 assumption explicitly.
