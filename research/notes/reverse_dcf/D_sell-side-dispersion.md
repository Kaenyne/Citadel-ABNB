## 1. Answer

The sell-side tape is mostly a multiple call on management's delivered case, not an operating forecast that differs from it. On the 12 Sep tape (32 live targets, mean $181.8, median $182.5), the mean and median targets imply 17.1x EV / FY27 delivered EBITDA on the brief's spot basis (16.1x on the FY27-end share count and net cash), which is between the team's mid (16.5x) and high (18.5x) exit multiples. The p25 target ($165) at 16.5x needs revenue of only $14,886m (+4.6%, nights +2.2%), which no analyst forecasts: it is the delivered case at 15.3x. The p75 ($200) at 16.5x needs revenue +29.2%, which no analyst forecasts either: it is the delivered case at 18.9x. The operating dispersion sits in the estimates, not the targets: the Zacks FY27 revenue range ($14.99-16.29bn, 13 estimates) maps to nights +2.9% to +11.8% on the delivered FY26 base under the decomposition below, and the consensus $15.73bn to nights +8.0% (delivered base) or +9.0% (Zacks FY26 base), one to two points below management's delivered +10.0%, half of that gap being the FY26 base. The whole estimate range priced at 16.5x is worth $166-179; estimate dispersion explains at most a quarter of the $125-220 target spread.

## 2. Method

Tape construction (MEASURED, `D_live_targets_2026-09-12.csv`): `Ticker('ABNB').upgrades_downgrades` pulled 12 Sep 2026, 469 actions since Dec 2020 (466 in the 6 Sep pull; 3 new, 0 removed). Live tape = latest action per firm with an action in the last 365 days and a target above zero: 32 firms (31 on 6 Sep; Raymond James is new), plus Keybanc, Needham and BTIG with ratings but no target. Rating buckets: Buy = Buy / Outperform / Overweight / Positive / Market Outperform; Hold = Neutral / Hold / Equal-Weight / Market Perform / Sector Weight; Sell = Sell / Underweight / Reduce. The tape is 32 of a 40-46 analyst universe (MarketBeat 40, S&P Global 46); the feed's gaps are quantified in caveat 2 and flagged per firm in the file (`last_action_chain_break`, `chain_breaks_since_sep2025`).

Implied multiple per target: EV = target x 597.0m diluted shares less $9,593m net cash ex float (ANCHOR, spot basis, matches the brief's $92.0bn EV at $170.19 and what workstream A uses), divided by management delivered FY27 adj. EBITDA $5,801m. Sensitivity: FY27-end basis, 570.7m diluted and $10,609m net cash (ANCHOR, delivered case), which is arguably the right basis for a 12-month target and runs 0.75-1.2 turns lower. The old file `12_analyst_targets.csv` used $5.83bn EBITDA, $12.4bn net cash (including float) and 567m shares, so its multiples are 1.0-1.5 turns lower than the spot-basis numbers here; the ranking is unchanged.

Required operating numbers per target at a held multiple m: EBITDA = EV / m; revenue = EBITDA / 36.2% (delivered margin held, ANCHOR); revenue growth on the delivered FY26 base $14,231m (ANCHOR); nights growth = (1 + revenue growth) / ((1 + 3.0%)(1 - 0.6%)(1 + 0)) - 1 (log-additive decomposition; ADR ex-FX +3%, FX -0.6pp, take rate flat are JUDGEMENT, set in the task, not in the brief; management's delivered case has FY27 ADR +3.1% in USD and take rate -0.7% (13.42% to 13.33%) with no FX line, so the terms are close to management's but not management's). EPS from a linear map fitted on the three management cases (EPS = -2.301 + 1.445 per $bn EBITDA; delivered check 6.08; JUDGEMENT, it carries the management model's tax, interest and buyback structure and nothing else). Reported at 13.5 / 16.5 / 18.5x (`D_target_implied.csv`, all 32 rows).

"At each analyst's own implied multiple": by construction, every target at its own implied multiple reproduces the delivered case exactly, so the informative number is the multiple itself (column 3 of the headline table and `D_target_implied.csv`). Where a firm has published its own estimate or multiple, `D_analyst_own_multiples.csv` re-runs the arithmetic on that (section 4).

Revision pattern (MEASURED, `D_02_revisions_positioning.py`): a daily panel of the mean live target (365-day window) and rating shares (24-month window) from Dec 2020 to 11 Sep 2026; for all 23 prints, the mean target on the session before the reaction day and at +1, +5, +20, +40 sessions; every target change in [-5, +25] sessions (counts are lower bounds, caveat 2); the chase ratio = change in mean target / day-1 price move; a regression of the 21-session log change in the mean target on the contemporaneous and two lagged 21-session log price changes, on overlapping daily data with Newey-West (21 lags) and on non-overlapping 21-session blocks at every one of the 21 possible block offsets (mean and range reported, not one offset); the same regression with falls and rises as separate regressors; and the mean-target change over the 42 sessions after any 21-session price move of 15% or more, with and without a print in the window.

Web quotes: `D_thesis_quotes.csv`, 19 rows, each with URL and access date. Most are vendor summaries (Investing.com, TipRanks/TheFly, MarketScreener/MT Newswires) of the notes, not the notes themselves; where the wording is the analyst's own it is marked so. Benzinga and Skift returned 403.

## 8. Caveats

1. Targets are 12-month, not FY27-year-end, and analysts do not use one metric. A 12-month target set in August or September 2026 is a September 2027 price, which most houses set on a blend of FY27 EV/EBITDA, FY27 P/E and DCF, sometimes on FY28 numbers (Bernstein's 25.5x is a P/E on core EPS of $8.51, which is not FY27 on any published basis). Holding every target to EV / FY27 delivered EBITDA on one share count and one net cash figure is a common yardstick, not a reconstruction of anyone's model. The spot basis (597.0m, $9,593m) and the FY27-end basis (570.7m, $10,609m) differ by 0.75-1.2 turns; both are reported. The old `12_analyst_targets.csv` basis (float-inclusive net cash, 567m shares, $5.83bn EBITDA) is 1.0-1.5 turns lower again. Quote the ranking and the range, not the second decimal.

2. Feed coverage and accuracy (MEASURED, `D_feed_chain_breaks.csv`, `D_live_targets_2026-09-12.csv`). The yfinance / Benzinga feed carries 32 live targets against 40 on MarketBeat and 46 on S&P Global. Chaining each firm's rows, the stated prior target differs from the feed's own previous target for that firm in 96 of 440 target actions, 17 of them since Sep 2025 on live-tape firms (Wedbush: feed $130 in Aug 2025, then "from $152" to $200; Evercore $155 then "from $190"; Piper $132 then "from $145"; BofA $146 then "from $160"; B. Riley $170 then "from $180"; Bernstein $162 then "from $168"; Mizuho, Jefferies, Cantor, BMO, Baird, Truist, Goldman, Morgan Stanley). Gordon Haskett (Hold, $180 from $146 in Aug 2026 per MarketScreener, a run of at least nine 2025-26 target changes) has no feed row since Jan 2023. The feed also duplicates Truist's 12 Jun 2026 row and carries a spurious Morgan Stanley 13 Jan 2026 row. So the feed drops whole firms and roughly a third of intermediate actions, not just the two Goldman raises. Consequences: the daily mean-target panel is stepwise wrong between prints; every raise and cut count in section 5 is a lower bound; the tape is 32 of a 40-46 universe. The ranking, the percentiles and the 2Q26 path are robust to this because post-print raises are the actions the feed does carry; the regression's dependent variable is noisier than its standard errors assume. Vendor means differ by $3 (S&P $178.96 on 3 Sep, MarketBeat $179.97 on 11 Sep, yfinance $182.13 on 12 Sep, this file $181.81) because each has a different universe and staleness window (this file drops targets older than 365 days; four of the 32 pre-date the 2Q26 print).

3. The decomposition and the EPS map are held-constant arithmetic, and the decomposition terms are JUDGEMENT. Nights growth is derived from revenue growth by subtracting ADR +3.0%, FX -0.6pp and a flat take rate, none of which is in the brief; if FY27 ADR is +1% or take rate rises 20bp (management's ambition case), every nights number moves by that amount. The EPS-to-EBITDA map is a straight line through the three management cases and assumes the management model's tax rate, interest income and buyback path; it cannot tell a margin estimate from a growth estimate, which matters most for the Zacks EPS high ($6.80). Bull and bear "what they must believe" numbers are therefore statements about the multiple given management's operating case, except for Bernstein, and section 4 says so on each line.

4. Web sourcing. Every quote comes from a vendor summary (Investing.com, TipRanks/TheFly, MarketScreener/MT Newswires, TIKR, StreetInsider, a blog) rather than the note itself, except where the analyst is quoted directly (DA Davidson's "We affirm our BUY ...", Morgan Stanley's 2022 "Supply deep dive ..."). Benzinga and Skift returned 403. No Truist reasoning for the 10 Sep raise exists in public reporting; Morgan Stanley's 2026 reasoning is a paraphrase, not a quote; Goldman's 7 Aug rating is by continuity from the verified 20 Jul headline. Nothing is fabricated; where a number is missing the row says "not published".

5. The revision regressions are small samples with a mechanical component. The block regression has 65-66 observations per offset (45 from 2023) and its results vary across the 21 offsets (contemporaneous beta 0.035-0.111, R2 0.19-0.34), which is why the range and the daily Newey-West estimate are quoted rather than a single offset. The print table has six prints a side at the 5% threshold; the same-day revisions on print days are scheduled note-writing rather than a reaction to the price, so print-day chase ratios overstate how fast the tape follows the price on ordinary days. Leave-one-print-out was not run. The non-print drawdown statistic counts days, not independent episodes (16 episodes of falls, 14 of rises).
