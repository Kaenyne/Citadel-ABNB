## Bottom line

GitHub holds a lot of tooling and very little committed data for this problem. Of 1,773 unique repositories catalogued, 1,102 are acquisition methods (API clients, scrapers, parsers), 351 are stored datasets, 194 point to an external host and 126 are both; of the 739 that were reviewed, only 204 earned a `sample` verdict and 59 were actually pulled (213.7 MB of heads and manifests; 58 with `sampled=true`, one empty). The strongest things that exist are: (1) a public DoltHub database (`post-no-preference/earnings`) with weekly point-in-time revenue and EPS consensus snapshots for ABNB from 7 Feb 2021 to 13 Sep 2026 (1,160 rows) and for BKNG and EXPE back to 2017, the closest thing to a free second consensus vendor for the L0 vintage register (gap 4; licence unstated, API fragile); (2) EUROCONTROL's `euctrl-pru/daio`, daily flight counts for 40 European countries from 1 Jan 2019 to the previous day, auto-committed, a fresh EMEA travel-volume input that leads the 150-day-lagged Eurostat series (gap 10); (3) pre-2023 Inside Airbnb archives that are off the Inside Airbnb CDN: ChicagoBooth's 30-city 2015 mirror, `estherkim99/cs1951a-final` with 244 raw monthly listings dumps for 8 US cities 2015 to 2020 (about 5 GB), `montera34/airbnb.barcelona` with 34 dated Barcelona snapshots Apr 2015 to Mar 2019, a complete NYC scrape dated 1 Jan 2015 (`JoeyDeJager/inside-airbnb-data`, 27,361 listings, CC0), Berlin and Vienna summary dumps from May and Sep 2017 (`tmasjc/airbnb-market-data`, 20,576 and 9,201 listings), a Hugging Face mirror of 116 markets' reviews files from the Mar to May 2023 dumps (NYC 6 Mar 2023, before Local Law 18 enforcement), and a cleaned NYC listings snapshot dated 5 Jan 2024 inside the missing LL18 window (gap 1); (4) Booking.com's own Hugging Face releases, 1.6M 2023 reviews carrying guest_type and room_nights and 1.55M 2016 reservations with check-in and check-out dates, the only competitor-side party-size and length-of-stay tables found (gaps 9 and 20); and (5) true vintage series that did not exist in our holdings, a daily git-scraped Toronto STR registry (917 commits since 20 Feb 2024) and immutable daily FX snapshots since 2 Mar 2024 (gaps 4 and 12). Four of the 20 gaps found nothing usable: gap 2 (realised STR ADR or occupancy from a free source; AirDNA, Key Data and Transparent are paywalled and no leaked export or replication package exists), gap 3 (STR booking lead-time and cancellation distributions; every hit is a re-analysis of the Portugal hotel dataset we already hold), gap 18 (cruise advance ticket sales; only the EDGAR XBRL route via NCLH's ContractWithCustomerLiability) and gap 19 (AI-referral traffic; one single-snapshot bot directory, no time series). Several others are only half-filled: no app-download history (gap 5, method only), no non-US card-spend panel (gap 6), no STR/CoStar or Nevada/Florida lodging series (gap 8, Texas only), nothing for Vrbo, Sonder, Vacasa, Tujia or Xiaozhu (gap 9), no DAC7 statistics (gap 12), no official LatAm or APAC tourism-agency mirrors (gap 7, clients only), and no Airbnb-specific Reddit or Community Center corpus (gap 14).

## What ran

Pipeline (three stages, ground truth on disk, `analysis/src/github_altdata/state.py` reports the state):

1. Scouts (Sonnet, effort medium): 123 themes x 2 modalities (`gh` = GitHub CLI search; `web` = WebSearch with WebFetch verification) plus 16 wildcard persona lenses = 262 agents, each writing `scouts/scout_NNN.json` with candidates, queries run and free-text notes. 262 files are on disk (four, 213, 232, 258 and 261, were recovered from the run journal after the agent returned without writing). 2,344 raw candidate hits collapsed to 1,773 unique repositories by URL key; 1,416 repos were seen by one scout, 259 by two, 98 by three or more (maximum 15, `tomslee/airbnb-data-collection`). Five scouts returned zero candidates, all in the web modality: 043 (Hostelworld, Sonder, Vacasa and similar), 095 (consumer sentiment surveys), 131 (tourist and city taxes), 135 (analyst estimates and consensus history) and 153 (geocoding and boundaries).
2. Review: originally Opus at 5 repos per agent with per-repo verification; after the scope cut, a metadata triage at 25 repos per agent, at most 6 WebFetches per batch, no `gh api`, effort medium, Opus for the high tier and Sonnet for the medium tier. Verdict fields: `verdict` (sample, catalog_only, discard), `usefulness`, `priority` (1 to 3), `source_family`, `tos_or_license_flag`, `sample_plan`.
3. Sampling (Fable, one agent per job): pull at most 25 MB per source (prefer 5 MB), heads of large tables, never clone without `--depth 1 --filter=blob:none`, never touch airbnb.com, never log in, never use a key, never run a scraper; write `samples/<slug>/manifest.json` with description, rows, columns, date_range, geography, full_dataset_size, how_pulled and issues.

Usage-limit interruptions: the first run hit the account limit at about 01:00 (reset 03:20 ET), which killed every review batch; the second review attempt hit it again after 21 batches at about 93k tokens per 5-repo batch. Workflow agents that hit the limit return null and the workflow "completes" with holes, which is why the catalogue is built from files on disk rather than workflow return values.

The scope cut (Krish, 14 Sep 08:50, "347 batches of 5 is way too much"): at the cut the pool was 1,773 unique repos with 1,635 unreviewed (high 122, medium 480, low 1,033), 1,299 single-scout hits and 340 flagged by scouts as duplicates of holdings. Decisions: only high and medium potential go to review (602 repos); low potential stays in the catalogue as scout-only rows with the scout's description and is never reviewed; 25 per agent, metadata triage, priority and source_family fields added; sampling restricted to priority-1 `sample` verdicts, one per source_family (pre-cut verdicts without a priority count as 1 if possibly_useful); and (09:00) Sonnet triages the medium tier, Opus only the high tier. The tiered run cost 2.15M tokens against roughly 30M implied by the original 347 x 5 plan at 93k per batch.

What the cut dropped: 1,033 low-potential repos were never opened by a reviewer, and 146 of the 204 `sample` verdicts (102 priority 2, 41 priority 3, 3 pre-cut) were never pulled. One review-file defect was found while writing this note and has since been repaired: four Sonnet batch files (507, 509, 517, 518) had not been written by their agents even though the workflow reported 25 of 25 batches complete, so only 502 of the 602 triage verdicts were on disk and 101 medium-tier candidates sat unreviewed. The four batches were recovered from the run's journal (its `type:"started"` lines map each agent key to its batch label and its `type:"result"` lines carry the returned verdict array), `catalog.csv` and `CATALOG_SUMMARY.md` were regenerated with `state.py --merge`, and 742 verdicts covering 739 unique rows are now on disk with a `review_tier` column that names the pass that produced each verdict. The recovered batches yielded three new priority-1 sample jobs, pulled in the sixth run above.

WebSearch budget caveat: WebSearch has a session-wide budget of about 200 calls pooled across all concurrent agents. In the first run 80 of 86 web-modality scouts reported it exhausted; in the resume run every web scout reported it exhausted on its first query. The prescribed fallbacks mostly failed too: DuckDuckGo HTML served CAPTCHAs or refused the connection, Bing ignored the `site:github.com` operator and returned generic pages, `github.com/search` returned HTTP 429 with a one-hour Retry-After, and `gh search` hit its shared 403 limit repeatedly. What worked was `github.com/topics/<tag>` pages, the unauthenticated `api.github.com/search/repositories` endpoint, the Hugging Face and Zenodo JSON APIs, and recall of known repositories. Scout 155 put it plainly: "the search coverage for this entire cataloguing sweep may be far shallower than intended". Web-modality coverage is therefore biased toward repositories that are topic-tagged or already well known, and "nothing found" from a web scout is weaker evidence than "nothing found" from a gh scout. A second quirk: `gh search repos` ANDs every token literally and does not support OR, so multi-word phrase queries returned empty even when matching repos exist; single tokens worked.

## What failed or is weak

The low tier was never reviewed. 1,033 repositories with scout potential "low" are in `catalog.csv` with the scout's `what_it_holds` and category only (`review_tier` = "scout-only (low potential, not sent to Opus)"); by category: methods_tooling 229, travel_volume_transport 191, str_airbnb_direct 128, regulatory_legal 70, housing_rental 62, macro_consumer_spend 59, hotel_lodging 52, web_app_engagement 49, geospatial 48, competitor_ota 43; by kind: 688 acquisition methods, 185 stored datasets, 111 pointers, 49 both. Scouts rated potential on descriptions and READMEs, so some stored datasets in this tier may be better than their rating.

Four Sonnet batch files (507, 509, 517, 518) were not written by their agents even though the workflow reported 25 of 25 batches complete, which left 101 medium-tier candidates unreviewed in the first merge. The defect was caught while writing this note and repaired: the four verdict arrays were recovered from the run journal (the `type:"started"` lines map each agent key to its batch label, the `type:"result"` lines carry the return value), written to `reviews/review_507.json` and the three others, and the catalogue was regenerated. The recovery added 99 verdicts to the catalogue (18 sample, 78 catalog_only, 3 discard) and three priority-1 sample jobs; one candidate still shows `unreviewed` because its recovered verdict URL did not match the catalogue key. The lesson stands: a workflow's completion count is not evidence that files landed, and `state.py` should be run after every workflow.

Duplicates. The source_family dedupe caught 83 families, but pre-cut verdicts carried no family, so the same source was pulled twice in three cases: the Booking.com accommodation-reviews dataset (`bookingcom-rectour24-accommodation-reviews` and `hf-bookingcom-accommodation-reviews`), the edgar-crawler toolkit under its old and new owner, and the FIFA schedule (two repos, one of which contributed only a placeholder).
