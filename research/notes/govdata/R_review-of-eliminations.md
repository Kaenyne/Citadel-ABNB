# R. Review of the V and P eliminations: was any government source dropped for a bad reason, and does "no full workstream" stand?

Date: 12 September 2026. Author: Krishang Surapaneni (compiled with Claude Code). Workstream R of the government data survey (`docs/govdata/BRIEF.md`), branch `krish/gov-data-survey`. Reviews `research/notes/govdata/V_government-travel-volume-survey.md` (38 candidates) and `P_government-travel-price-survey.md` (35 candidates). No V or P file was modified.

Krish's instruction, verbatim: "make sure we dont eliminate an idea for a bad reason." So this note treats every stated first failing criterion as a claim to be checked: every blocked pull was retried by a different route, every coverage date was re-derived from the observed lag, every duplicate claim was tested on quarterly y/y correlations, every survivor was re-run from the cached panels with two extra checks (a 2024Q1+ window scored from 1Q25 that drops the 2022 to 2023 normalisation quarters from both fit and score, and an expanding-mean benchmark), and the supply-only rows were given a use.

Labels: SOURCED = an agency payload cached under `data/processed/govdata/R/raw/` with URL, access time, bytes and sha256 in `MANIFEST.csv`, or a V or P cache cited by file; DESCRIPTIVE = computed here from sourced data or the repo's targets; ASSUMED = a release date inferred from an observed lag or a judgement call. Every number below is one of the three.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Of 73 verdicts, 58 are upheld, 11 reclassified and 4 overturned (DESCRIPTIVE, `R_audit.csv`).** The four overturned facts: Portugal's INE publishes the local-accommodation nights series through a keyless API that V could not find (the "duplicate" of Eurostat PT I552 is a content duplicate with a month of extra timing through the INE flash); Mexico's DATATUR is reachable today with 30 monthly hotel-monitoring files (V had a ConnectionError); the Mexican migration bulletin is mirrored on DATATUR as monthly PDFs; and Eurostat's "other accommodation services" HICP item exists to July 2026 under the coicop18 code CP11203, not the CP11209 P queried. None of the four changes the model. The eleven reclassifications are mostly dates: Eurostat's July nights, NTTO's August Canada rows, Colombia's August entries and the INE Portugal flash all land between 2 October and 5 November, so they are pre-print corroborations rather than "no coverage"; Census QSS, the SPPI and the ONS short-term lets dataset are useless for 5 November but belong on the February FY27 list; the three registry rows are supply signals for the ADR v3 M supply-growth lead; IBGE's "survivor" fails its own jackknife and drops to corroboration; and P's Portugal HICP "both-window survivor" is a short-window survivor once the long window is scored from 1Q23 as V scores it (1.04 vs naive).

2. **The "no full workstream" conclusion stands, for the reason both notes gave and one more.** Nothing recovered or reclassified beats naive on all three windows with a mechanism and a pre-print reading. What does hold on all three windows is a fit to a management bucket that takes two or three distinct values on the reviewer window (EU27 hotel nights on the EMEA bucket 0.77 / 0.79 / 0.56; Portugal total nights on the EMEA bucket 0.79 / 0.79 / 0.62; Brazilian central bank tourism receipts on the LatAm bucket 0.80 / 0.79 / 0.72), or a series whose 3Q26 value arrives after the print (Census QSS 721 revenue, 0.76 / 0.77 / 0.59, the most robust fit in either survey, 19 November). The extra reason: on the reviewer window the expanding mean of the target beats naive on total nights (0.62) and every short-window survivor in V loses to that mean (ratios 1.04 to 1.15 against it), so the short-window results are statements about the target's stability in 2025 to 2026, not about the features. P's flat-slope diagnosis of its own EMEA survivors applies to V's list too.

3. **One gap source is worth twenty-five minutes before the freeze: the Banco Central do Brasil monthly travel account (SGS API, keyless, USD millions, published about the 25th of the following month).** Personal-tourism receipts on the LatAm bucket are the only LatAm feature below 1 on all three windows with jackknife max below 1 on all three (0.80 / 0.79 / 0.72, jk max 0.85 / 0.84 / 0.86; DESCRIPTIVE), with the same step-function caveat as ANAC. Its message is the new number in this review: Brazilians' travel spending abroad was +23.1% y/y in 2Q26 and +21.9% in 1Q26 and slowed to +3.7% in July 2026; card-based outbound +26.4% in July from +32.2%; receipts from foreign visitors -2.9% in July from +11.7% (SOURCED levels, DESCRIPTIVE y/y, nominal USD so BRL moves are inside it). Against a LatAm bucket of +20% and a reviews-index read of +27.5, the origin-market spend series has decelerated sharply in the first month of Q3. One month, nominal, and management says the growth is Brazilians booking on Airbnb, which this series does measure (it is outbound spend). August lands about 25 September, September about 25 October.

4. **P's Portugal HICP recommendation should be dropped, the BEA line kept with its caveat, and V's three-source task kept with one addition and one date correction.** Portugal HICP CP112 on EMEA ex-FX is 0.79 on 2023Q1+ but 1.04 on 2022Q1+ scored from 1Q23 and 0.94 on the reviewer window (jk max 1.30), while the euro-area series J2 already holds is 0.95 / 0.91 / 1.18 on the same construction; carrying Portugal next to it adds a second flat-slope level model, not information. BEA export travel prices in first differences (0.82 on the residual) fail the reviewer window (1.13, jk max 1.36), so the 29 October check is a corroboration line only, as P said. V's StatCan, INE EOAP plus Eurostat I552, and Hawaii task stands; add the INE Portugal flash (September on about 31 October) as the EMEA short-stay reading that exists before the print, and move Eurostat's August from "27 October" to "after the print" (the observed lag is 72 days).

5. **Ordered list of what is worth doing (ASSUMED dates from observed lags unless an agency calendar is cited).**
   - Before 21 September (freeze week): (a) add the BCB travel account to the LatAm read next to ANAC (collector in `R1_retries.py`, probe `bcb_named`); (b) put three sentences in the cards: the official European short-stay market is flat (INE EOAP July -0.03%, Portugal local accommodation 2Q26 -1.2%, EU27 I552 2Q26 +2.8%), Brazilian outbound travel spend slowed to +3.7% in July from +23% in 2Q26, US travel exports were -0.6% y/y in July (Census FT-900); (c) drop Portugal HICP from the EMEA regional read. Checked and not worth a pull: Eurostat monthly balance of payments (no travel line at monthly frequency; quarterly only). Listed only: airport authority traffic (Heathrow's August 2026 xlsx is up; the arrivals class is a normalisation fit in V), US visa issuance by nationality (an origin-market lead, not an accommodation series), central-bank card spend (only small markets publish an accommodation split), cruise and port data and the WTTC and UNWTO barometers (wrong customer or paid).

## 4. Method

- Retries (`R1_retries.py`, 23 probes, 62 manifest rows): each blocked or route-dependent source was tried by a route V or P did not use (a different endpoint or host, the JSON or SDMX API, a data.gov or DATATUR mirror, the Wayback availability API, a bulk file, a browser user agent), with 25 to 90 second timeouts and no registration token (a free token is still a per-user key, so Banxico and INEGI stay out of scope). Outcomes: DATATUR reachable (three pages, 30 hotel zips, UPM and INEGI PDF mirrors); INE Portugal catalogue and indicator 0012088 (one period per call, five retries per period, the endpoint intermittently returns a non-JSON body); Eurostat CP11203; ONS short-term lets bulletin and dataset; BCB catalogue and SGS series; Census exhibit 3; Heathrow traffic page. Still blocked: FRED (six attempts, connection reset and read timeouts), Bank of Greece and HCAA (403 with a browser UA), Korea (JS portal), MLIT minpaku (no report links, login), India (no file links), Croatia DZS and HTZ (404), Singapore and Hong Kong (no open-data route), Mexican migration site (SSL and connection errors, no Wayback snapshot), Banxico front end (HTML without series). Raw caches are tidy CSVs; the xlsx, zip and XML payloads stayed in the session and are identified by sha256 in the manifest.
- Re-runs (`R2_reruns.py`): the note-08 protocol via `I0_protocol.py` unchanged (expanding OLS refit strictly before each scored quarter, RMSE vs naive last quarter, 1,000-shuffle permutation p), with the V and P feature constructions reproduced from their panels (V vintages `_full`, `_lag1`, `_m1`, `_m2`; P level and difference at lags 0 and 1), three windows, a jackknife over scored quarters, the sign and stability of the fitted slope, the number of distinct target values on the scored quarters, and an expanding-mean benchmark (the target's own mean to date as the forecast).
- Audit (`R3_audit.py`): the decisions are hand-coded with the evidence string per candidate and joined to the V and P candidate tables, which are read, not written.
- Release dates are ASSUMED from the lag between the last period in a cache and the dataset's update stamp (Eurostat 72 days; Colombia 7 weeks; BCB about 25 days; INE Portugal flash about 30 days per P's manifest) except where the agency calendar was in V's or P's manifest (StatCan 10 days, Census QSS 19 Nov, BEA 29 Oct).

## 5. What this can and cannot identify

- It can say whether each elimination rests on a true fact. Four did not; the corrected facts add coverage and readings, not a nowcaster.
- It can say that the regional buckets cannot carry a survivor verdict on their own: on the reviewer window the NA bucket takes three values, EMEA two, LatAm three, APAC two. A ratio below 1 there is a fit to a step. The three all-window fits this review found (EU27 hotels, Portugal total nights, BCB receipts) are of that kind and should be shown as corroboration in the deck, not as forecasts.
- It cannot give the six-quarter reviewer window any permutation power (p values of 0.1 to 0.8 throughout); the window is a robustness check on the ordering of models, not a test on its own.
- It cannot separate nominal from real in the BCB and Census trade series; both are USD spend, so the BRL and the dollar are inside them. The July deceleration in Brazilian outbound spend is one month and could be FX.
- It cannot verify that the INE Portugal flash reports nights by establishment type; P's manifest says the July flash gave national ADR, RevPAR and nights, and the by-type split is assumed. If the flash is total-only, the pre-print reading is the all-establishment total, which is the series that beats naive on all three windows anyway.
- It did not parse the 30 DATATUR monthly files or the UPM PDFs, did not retry ISTAT or INSEE (both duplicates of June-dated Eurostat rows, low value), and did not test the airport, visa or card-spend classes.
