# P. Government travel price data: can any official price series nowcast or corroborate Airbnb ADR?

Date: 2026-09-12. Author: Krishang Surapaneni (compiled with Claude Code). Workstream P of the government data survey (`docs/govdata/BRIEF.md`). Scripts `analysis/src/govdata/P1_collect.py`, `P2_backtests.py`, `P3_rank.py`; outputs under `data/processed/govdata/P/`.

Targets: reported blended ADR y/y, disclosed ex-FX ADR y/y, the like-for-like pricing residual (`residual_pricing_pp`, 1Q23 to 2Q26, n 14), and regional ex-FX ADR (NA and EMEA disclosed-chained from 2023, LatAm and APAC disclosed only from 4Q24 and modelled before). The 3Q26 read being corroborated: reported ADR +3.4%, ex-FX +3.9%, residual 4.85 with the mean-reversion downside at 2.40, EMEA ex-FX read from euro-area HICP about 4.6.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No official price series beats naive against the pricing residual on a credible basis.** 428 tests on the residual, best ratio 0.81, and that one (Swiss HICP holiday centres and camping, level, r -0.85) has the wrong sign. The best right-signed feature is the BEA exports-of-travel price index in first differences (0.82 vs naive, 0.67 vs AR(1), jackknife 0.58 to 0.96 with 10 of 10 below one, Bonferroni p 1.00), which is a CPI-lodging-weighted composite and quarterly, so it is a cousin of the series J2 already rejected at 1.03 to 1.05, not a new mechanism. Sourced from `P_backtests.csv`.
2. **The short-term-rental asset class is now covered and it does not help.** Spain's tourist-apartment price index (INE HDPI), Hawaii's vacation-rental ADR, the UK CPI "accommodation services of other establishments" item and Eurostat's 5-digit "holiday centres, camping, hostels" item were all pulled and tested: best ratios 1.10, 1.08, 1.33 and 1.08 (EU aggregate) against any Airbnb target. Eurostat's "other accommodation services" item (CP11209, the ECOICOP v2 class that would hold short-term lets) is not published in `prc_hicp_minr` for any country past 2024-12. Hawaii's series has two comparison-basis breaks inside 2025 to 2026 (provider change in the Aug 2025 vintage, base reversal in the May 2026 vintage), so its y/y flips sign on basis, not price.
3. **One genuine both-window survivor for the EMEA line, none for the blended series.** Portugal's HICP accommodation services (all establishments including local accommodation) against EMEA ex-FX ADR: 0.79 vs naive on 2023Q1+ (jackknife 0.68 to 0.90, 10 of 10 below one) and 0.90 on 2022Q1+ (Bonferroni p 0.001, r 0.88). It extends J2's marginal euro-area survivor (0.91) and is knowable before the print. The Portuguese SPPI for accommodation is a stronger backtest (0.68 and 0.78) but its 3Q26 value publishes in December, so it fails coverage. Spain's rural-house price index "survives" both windows against reported ADR with r -0.83 and fails at 3.35 against EMEA ex-FX: wrong sign, FX artefact, eliminated on the test criterion in spirit even though it passes the letter.
4. **What the 3Q26 readings say (all sourced, July or July-August 2026 against the 2Q26 quarter mean).** European accommodation prices hold or firm: euro-area HICP 4.6 (4.3), EU27 5.0 (4.5), Spain 8.9 (8.6), Spain tourist apartments 6.2 (4.6), Italy 4.0 (2.6), France 7.3 (6.0), UK other establishments 5.7 (5.7), Portugal national ADR +3.2 in July. North American lodging prices decelerate sharply: BLS CPI other lodging incl hotels 2.8 (4.7), PPI hotels and motels 3.4 (5.7), BEA PCE accommodations 3.4 (4.5), StatCan traveller accommodation 2.5 (6.1). Japan hotel charges 0.9 (3.0). A nights-share-weighted read of those changes is about -0.9 pp from 2Q26 to the quarter so far (descriptive, FY25 regional shares 30/40/20/10), which sits between J's persistence point (4.61) and its 2Q26 anchor (4.85) on the downside and nowhere near the mean-reversion case (2.40). The EMEA-specific models imply ex-FX EMEA 3Q26 of 3.7 to 3.9 (Portugal HICP, both windows), 4.6 (euro area), 4.7 (EU27), 4.6 (Italy), 6.5 (Spain) against naive 5.0: mild mean reversion in EMEA, not a break.
5. **Recommendation.** A full workstream before 2 October is not worth it: there is no same-asset-class official price series with a testable history that beats naive, and the residual stays unobserved. Two bounded additions, about half a day together, are worth making: (a) carry Portugal HICP CP112 alongside euro-area HICP in the EMEA regional read (both are level models; Portugal's September flash is 30 September, final 15 October); (b) add the BEA exports-of-travel price index in first differences as a residual corroboration line when the 3Q advance GDP prints on 29 October, with the CPI-composite caveat stated. A third, cheaper line is to log the North American deceleration set (CPI SEHB02, PPI 721110, PCE accommodations, StatCan) as the NA read since three of the four are already held. Skip the SPPI (December), New Zealand CPI (collapses on the long window, 1.82) and the Spanish rural index (wrong sign).

## 3. What the survivors and corroborations say about 3Q26 ADR and the residual

**EMEA ex-FX (disclosed 5.0 in 2Q26).** The only knowable both-window survivor, Portugal HICP CP112 in level, is a flat-slope model (slope 0.23 to 0.30, intercept about 4.0 on either window, descriptive): it beats naive because EMEA ex-FX has sat at 4 to 6 since 2024 while the naive chases quarter-to-quarter noise, not because Portuguese prices move ahead of Airbnb Europe. Its 3Q26 implication is 3.7 to 3.9 on the July reading (-0.7 y/y, Portugal's accommodation CPI has been negative since 2Q25 on a hotel-heavy sample even as INE's own tourist-accommodation ADR runs +3.2). The euro-area and EU27 models give 4.6 and 4.7, Italy 4.6, Spain 6.5. Read together: EMEA ex-FX in the 4 to 5 range, mild reversion from 5.0 rather than a break, consistent with J's 4.6 (sourced readings, descriptive model outputs).

**North America ex-FX (disclosed 6.8 in 2Q26).** Nothing beats naive on the NA target on a knowable basis, but every US and Canadian lodging price series decelerates from 2Q26 into July-August by 1.1 to 3.6 pp (CPI SEHB02 4.7 to 2.8, PPI hotels 5.7 to 3.4, PPI traveler accommodation 4.3 to 3.2, PCE accommodations 4.5 to 3.4, StatCan 6.1 to 2.5), matching the STR weekly ADR fade G recorded (+5.7 July to +0.6 late August). These are hotel prices and NA Airbnb ex-FX ran 2 to 4 pp above them through 2025-26, so the level is not the read; the direction is, and it is down.

**APAC and LatAm.** Japan hotel charges 3.0 to 0.9 (the World Cup effect is in Europe and North America, not Japan); Brazil hospedagem 6.7 to 6.3, flat; Australia's quarterly index is not out until 28 October. The APAC and LatAm walk-forwards score against modelled targets before 4Q24 and should not be read as evidence either way.

**The residual (persistence 4.61 vs mean reversion 2.40; 2Q26 4.85).** No feature identifies it. What the readings support, descriptively: the two halves of the residual's geography are moving in opposite directions, Europe flat-to-firmer, North America clearly softer, Japan softer. A nights-share-weighted change in external accommodation price growth from 2Q26 to the quarter so far is about -0.9 pp (NA -1.9 on CPI SEHB02, EMEA +0.3 on euro-area HICP, APAC -2.1 on Japan, LatAm -0.3 on Brazil; FY25 shares 30/40/20/10; assumed weighting, sourced inputs). Applied to the 2Q26 residual that is about 3.9 to 4.3; applied to the persistence point it is about 3.7 to 4.1. It is a direction, not a nowcast, and it sits on the persistence side of the H card's band, below the 2Q26 anchor and well above the mean-reversion case. The BEA exports-of-travel first-difference model, the best right-signed residual feature, cannot be evaluated until 29 October; on its 2Q26 jump (3.7 to 6.1) it would have said the 2Q26 residual was rising, which it was.

**Same-asset-class readings.** Spain tourist apartments +6.2 in July from +4.6 (accelerating), Hawaii vacation rentals +14.5 on a basis that was -4 to -7 four months earlier (not usable), UK other establishments +5.7 flat, EU holiday centres and camping 4.9 to 5.0 flat, Portugal all-accommodation ADR +3.2. Where the official short-term-rental price is measured it is not decelerating in Europe. That is the one direct, if untestable, piece of support for persistence on the EMEA leg.

## 4. Method

**Collection (P1).** Every source with a keyless machine endpoint was pulled on 12 September 2026 and recorded in `raw/MANIFEST.csv` (url, access time, HTTP status, bytes, sha256, last period, note): Eurostat `prc_hicp_minr` (ECOICOP v2 CP112, CP11201, CP11202, CP11209; RCH_A and I15; 22 geos kept), `sts_sepp_q` NACE I55 (PCH_SM and I21; 17 geos); INE Spain JSON tables 1989 (HDPI), 2007 (rural), 1990 (campsites), 2058 (hotel ADR), 2056 (RevPAR), last 150 months; BLS API v2 keyless (PPI 721110 and 7211, CPI SEHB02 NSA and SA; the B&B 721191, other-accommodation 721199 and 7212 PPIs do not exist); StatCan WDS vector v41691191; ABS SDMX CPI quarterly (30033, 40101, 40102) plus the 2017-2025 monthly indicator and the complete monthly CPI from Nov 2025, spliced on y/y; IBGE SIDRA tables 7060 and 1419, item 7201090; FRED csv for BEA NIPA travel export and import price indexes; ONS mm23 JSON (d7cx, l7ie, l7ik, d7hb); Stats NZ June 2026 CPI index-numbers csv (SE9096, SE909601); e-Stat 2025-base item workbook 000040491331 (宿泊料, serial 638); Hawaii DBEDT vacation rental reports, xlsx and pdf, 2023-06 to 2026-07 (68 vintages parsed; 2019-09 to 2023-05 returned HTTP 429 from Cloudflare after the first burst and were not retrievable within budget, the URLs are recorded). Twenty record-only candidates were probed once and their status recorded. Raw caches are the tidy CSVs under `raw/`; the Hawaii and e-Stat binaries stayed in the session scratchpad.

**Features (P2).** Monthly indexes to y/y then quarter mean; published rates quarter-averaged; quarterly indexes to y/y on the quarter; Hawaii y/y taken within each report vintage (current month against the same month a year earlier on that vintage's basis). Level and first difference, lags 0 and 1. The 3Q26 reading is the mean of the months in hand.

**Tests (P2).** `analysis/src/adrq3/I0_protocol.py` unchanged: expanding OLS refit strictly before each scored quarter from 1Q24, RMSE ratio vs naive last quarter, prior year and AR(1), 1,000-shuffle permutation p; plus a leave-one-scored-quarter-out jackknife on the naive ratio and the knowable-before-print flag. Windows 2023Q1+ and 2022Q1+ (the residual exists only from 1Q23). Bonferroni over all tests on each target, as J2. Per feature the targets are the residual, blended ex-FX, reported ADR and the regional ex-FX series of the feature's region.

**Verdicts (P3).** Survivor if some (feature, transform, lag, target) beats naive on both windows with six or more scored quarters; short-window survivor if on 2023Q1+ only; corroboration if it has a 3Q26 reading and does not beat naive or cannot be tested; eliminations state the first failing criterion in the BRIEF order. Two overrides are hand-coded with the fact stated in the row: the SPPI fails coverage before the test (3Q26 in December), and Hawaii fails history (two basis breaks) before the test.

## 5. What this can and cannot identify

- **It can say the official price universe for the short-term-rental class is now exhausted for this purpose.** Spain's IPAP, Hawaii's vacation-rental ADR, the UK and EU "other establishments" items and Portugal's all-accommodation ADR are the only official series that price the asset class, and none has a testable history that beats naive. The natural next series, Eurostat CP11209, is not published.
- **It cannot separate the residual from the mix terms.** Every external price is a hotel or all-accommodation price on a fixed basket; Airbnb's residual is what is left after the mix terms, and the note-03 pattern (co-movement on one jump, the 2Q26 acceleration, and nowhere else) is what the flagged first-difference features show again here.
- **Multiple comparisons are the main hazard in this table.** 2,996 tests, 672 on EMEA alone; the wrong-signed "survivors" (Swiss camping, Greek hotels, Spanish rural houses) are the expected number of accidents at these counts, and only the Portugal HICP result carries a Bonferroni p below 0.05 with the right sign and a knowable release.
- **A flat-slope level model beating naive is a statement about the target's stability, not about the feature.** Portugal HICP, euro-area HICP and Spain RevPAR all beat naive on EMEA ex-FX because the target sat at 4 to 6 for ten quarters. They will fail the quarter the target moves.
- **The regional targets are partly modelled.** APAC and LatAm ex-FX before 4Q24 and NA and EMEA before 2023 come from the ADR decomposition model, so long-window regional ratios score partly against model output.
- **Hawaii is a warning about vendor-fed government series.** The report is official but the data are a private provider's, and the provider changed; the y/y in any single vintage is internally consistent, the series across vintages is not.
