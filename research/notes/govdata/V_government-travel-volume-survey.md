# V. Government travel volume data: what official arrivals, nights and lodging series can add to the 3Q26 nights read

Date: 12 September 2026. Author: Krishang Surapaneni (compiled with Claude Code). Workstream V of the government data survey (`docs/govdata/BRIEF.md`), branch `krish/gov-data-survey`.

Question: is there official (government or statistical-agency) travel VOLUME data, not yet in the repo, that either beats naive out of sample on Airbnb nights (total or by region) and is knowable before 5 November, or corroborates the 3Q26 read with a reading we do not already hold? Targets: total nights y/y (`abnb_driver_history_quarterly.csv`) and the regional nights mids in `data/processed/overnight/10_regional_panel_quarterly.csv`. The read to corroborate is 3Q26 nights +9.9% (band 8.5 to 10.3) with a reviews-index regional split of NA +6.9, EMEA +0.9, LatAm +27.5, APAC +10.0.

Labels: SOURCED = taken from an agency payload cached under `data/processed/govdata/V/raw/` with URL, access date, bytes and sha256 in `MANIFEST.csv`; DESCRIPTIVE = computed here from sourced data or from the repo's own targets; ASSUMED = my reading of a release calendar or a judgement call. Every number below is one of the three.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Nothing new beats naive on both windows with a reading available before the print, so there is no volume nowcaster to add to the model.** Thirty-eight candidates were screened, 17 were pulled and 836 walk-forward tests run (DESCRIPTIVE, `V_backtests.csv`). Twenty-six feature-target pairs beat naive on both the 2022Q1+ and 2023Q1+ windows. Every one of them is either not knowable before 5 November (Census QSS 721 revenue, 0.76 and 0.77, arrives 19 Nov; Eurostat Portugal I552 short-stay nights, 0.78 and 0.81, July and August arrive about 27 Oct and September after the print; NTTO Canada rows, 0.79 and 0.79, lag two months), or is a LatAm-bucket fit on Brazil air passengers where the target has taken only five distinct values in ten quarters. The 106 pairs that beat naive on the short window only are the same post-COVID normalisation artefact G found: any series that decelerated smoothly through 2023 and 2024 fits a nights series that did the same, and the 2022 reopening quarters (y/y arrivals of +300% to +6,000% for Japan and Canada) blow the long window up to ratios of 3 to 63.

2. **The one genuinely new fast source is Statistics Canada's leading indicator of international arrivals (tables 24-10-0056 air by residence and 24-10-0058 vehicles by licence plate).** It is daily, keyless, and the August 2026 month was published on 11 September, so two of the three Q3 months are in hand today (SOURCED). No other official series in this survey reaches August. It is a short-window survivor on total nights (0.66 to 0.81 on 2023Q1+, permutation p below 0.01, jackknife max 0.80 to 0.98, but 3.3 on 2022Q1+) and does not beat naive on the NA bucket (1.07), so it is a corroboration for NA cross-border, not a forecaster. Its message: US residents flying into Canada for overnight stays were +4.9% y/y in July and +6.5% in August against +6.8% in April to May and -0.3% in June; residents of other countries +6.8% and +9.8%; American-plated vehicles crossing by land +6.8% in July then +0.8% in August. Canada-US air travel is re-accelerating into Q3, the land border is not. That is consistent with the NA +8% bucket and the reviews-index NA +6.9, and it argues against the 2025 "Canada boycott" framing still being a Q3 drag.

3. **Spain's tourist-apartment survey (INE EOAP, table 1998) is the only official short-term-rental-class nights series that reaches July today, and its July read is flat.** Holiday-apartment overnight stays in Spain were -0.03% y/y in July 2026 (foreign residents -2.2%, Spanish residents +3.8%) while travellers were +7.9%, so stays are getting shorter (SOURCED, DESCRIPTIVE y/y). It does not beat naive on any target (1.2 to 1.7), so it is a corroboration. Read against the EMEA bucket of +8% and the reviews-index EMEA +0.9, it sides with the reviews index: the Spanish apartment market is not growing in volume in Q3, so whatever EMEA growth Airbnb prints is share and geography, not the market. August publishes about 23 September (ASSUMED from the 23 August release date of July), before the 2 October pitch.

4. **Eurostat `tour_occ_nim` NACE I552 (holiday and other short-stay accommodation) is the right asset class for EMEA and it is decelerating, but it is a June series today.** EU27 I552 nights were +4.6% y/y in 1Q26 and +2.8% in 2Q26; Spain -1.2%, France -0.8%, Portugal -0.9%, Germany -1.8%, Croatia -0.6%, Greece +2.1%, Italy +10.6% (DESCRIPTIVE from SOURCED monthly levels). EU27 hotel nights (I551) beat naive on the EMEA bucket on both windows (0.77 and 0.79, p 0.001, jackknife max 0.92), the best regional result in the survey, but July and August will only be published around 27 October and September after 5 November, so the verdict is no coverage today. It becomes a pre-print corroboration on 27 October and the strongest candidate for the EMEA share gauge the overnight-10 note asked for.

5. **The only official series that measures Airbnb's own asset class with both demand and rate is Hawaii DBEDT's vacation rental performance report, and it says July was the first positive demand month of 2026 at +2.3% y/y with supply -6.8%, occupancy 54.9% (+4.9 pts) and ADR $543 (+14.5%).** It is compiled by a state agency from Transparent Intelligence listing data (SOURCED). History starts January 2024 (the 2024 report files return 404; the 2025 reports carry prior-year columns), so it cannot be walk-forward tested and is a corroboration only. It is one market, but it is the only public place where an STR ADR of +14.5% sits next to an STR demand of +2.3% for a Q3 month.

6. **Japan, Australia, New Zealand and Brazil all reach July, all beat naive on the short window only, and all say the same thing: APAC and LatAm market volumes are flat to low single digits while Airbnb's buckets are +18% and +20%.** Japan Tourism Agency guest nights July -3.5% y/y (foreign -4.6%, on a January 2026 methodology break the agency flags); JNTO arrivals July +0.1% (Korea +31.9%, Taiwan +26.4%, US +3.3%, China -56.1%); Australia short-term visitor arrivals July -4.3%; New Zealand commercial guest nights July +11.6% (international +9.3%); Brazil domestic air passengers July +1.7%, international +3.3%; IBGE accommodation volume July -4.6% (SOURCED levels, DESCRIPTIVE y/y). None of these markets is booming. Airbnb's APAC and LatAm growth is penetration and origin mix (India, Brazil origin), which no destination-country arrival series will ever see. The regional buckets are also too coarse to score: the APAC mid has taken three values since 4Q24 and LatAm four, so a "survivor" on those targets is a fit to a step function.

7. **Stacking every observable-today short-window survivor gives 3Q26 total nights +9.6% (median of 23 specs, range +8.6% to +10.1%) against naive +10.3% and the team baseline +9.9%** (DESCRIPTIVE, in-sample OLS on 1Q23 to 2Q26, specs not independent). Same conclusion as G's +9.2%: the outside world is consistent with high single digits to ten, and nothing here is precise enough to move the band.

8. **Recommendation: no full workstream.** The reason is structural, not effort: official volume data measures destination markets, Airbnb's growth is share and origin mix, and the regional targets are management buckets. A bounded two-day task is worth doing on three sources only, listed in section 6: the StatCan leading indicator (daily NA cross-border, August in hand, September about 10 October), Spain INE EOAP plus Eurostat I552 as the EMEA reality-check panel (August EOAP about 23 September, Eurostat July and August about 27 October), and the Hawaii vacation rental report (August about 25 September). Everything else on the list is either a duplicate of those, blocked, or a hotel or arrivals series that adds a fourth version of the same normalisation fit.

## 3. What each survivor or corroboration says about 3Q26, by region

All y/y figures DESCRIPTIVE from SOURCED levels in `V_readings_3q26.csv` and `V_feature_panel.csv`. "2Q26" is the same feature's value for the second quarter (or its first month or two months where the 3Q26 vintage is partial, so like is compared with like).

**North America (bucket +8% in 2Q26; reviews index +6.9 for 3Q26).**
- StatCan leading indicator, July and August combined: US residents entering Canada by air, overnight, +5.7% (2Q26 same vintage +6.8%); all non-resident visitors by air +6.9% (+4.0%); residents of countries other than the US by air +8.2% (+1.6%); all international visitors entering or returning by air, which includes Canadians coming home, +2.6% (+1.7%); American-plated vehicles entering by land +3.7% (+6.7%), with August alone +0.8%. ## 4. Method

- Sources: 38 candidates from the brief's list plus additions found on the way (StatCan leading indicator, JTA accommodation survey, NZ ADP CSV on the TEIC mirror, IBGE PMS, Colombia Socrata, Hawaii VR). Every pull is in `analysis/src/govdata/V1_collect.py` with the URL, HTTP status, bytes, sha256 and last period in `raw/MANIFEST.csv`. Files over 5 MB (ANAC 99 MB CSV, NZ ADP 35 MB, StatCan daily) were aggregated in memory and only the monthly aggregate is committed. Blocked pulls are kept as rows with the exact status: DATATUR and the Mexican migration bulletin (ConnectionError), Banxico (400, free token required), Bank of Greece and HCAA (403), KTO (302 to a login), ISTAT SDMX (timeout), FRED air RPMs (timeout and connection reset on four attempts), BTS T-100 (form-gated), MLIT minpaku (PDF only).
- Features: calendar-quarter y/y of the summed monthly level (`_full`), the first month of the quarter (`_m1`), the first two months where August exists (`_m2`, StatCan only), and the prior quarter (`_lag1`). Regional mapping: NA gets StatCan, NTTO, Census, Hawaii; EMEA gets Eurostat, INE, avia; LatAm gets ANAC, IBGE, Colombia; APAC gets JNTO, JTA, ABS, NZ. Every feature is also tested on total nights.
- Test: `analysis/src/adrq3/I0_protocol.py` unchanged (expanding OLS refit strictly before each scored quarter, RMSE against naive last quarter, prior year and AR(1), 1,000-shuffle permutation p), two windows (2022Q1+ scored from 1Q23; 2023Q1+ scored from 1Q24), both ending 2Q26. Jackknife added in `V2_backtests.py` by re-running the same walk-forward loop and dropping each scored quarter in turn. Knowable-before-print flag per feature from the release calendar observed on 12 September (ASSUMED where a future date is inferred from the lag pattern).
- Verdicts: `V3_rank.py` applies the brief's criteria in order and stops at the first failure. Coverage is read strictly: a series must reach July or August today, or September before 5 November. Eurostat, StatCan 24-10-0053, Census QSS, Colombia and the NTTO Canada and Mexico rows fail on that reading even where July or August will land before the print; the note says when.

## 5. What this can and cannot identify

- It can say whether an official volume series moves with Airbnb's reported nights out of sample. Almost all do on the 2023Q1+ window and almost none on 2022Q1+, and the reason is the shared normalisation path, so the short-window ratios are not evidence of a mechanism.
- It can give destination-market context by region for 3Q26, which is new: flat Spain apartments, +3% EU short-stay, flat Japan, -4% Australia inbound, +2% Brazil air, +2% Hawaii VR demand with +14% ADR, +6% US residents into Canada by air.
- It cannot see origin-mix growth (India, Brazil origin), penetration, or Airbnb's share of any market, which is where management locates the growth. It cannot score the regional buckets meaningfully: the NA and APAC mids have moved in 1 to 3 point steps and the LatAm mid in 2 point steps, so an OLS on them is a fit to a step function and a ratio below 1 there should not be read as a forecast.
- It cannot deliver September before the pitch. The earliest September readings are the StatCan leading indicator (about 10 October) and JNTO (about 15 October).
- Regulatory series (Scotland, NYC, Florida) are supply counts and were eliminated on asset class, not on merit; the repo already holds the regulatory package.
