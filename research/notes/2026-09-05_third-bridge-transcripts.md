## 1. The take-rate debate (the gap flagged in the pitch landscape note)

**Supplier power sits with property managers, not Airbnb (T5).** The US VRM expert is blunt: "Airbnb doesn't control the marketplace. They just don't have the dominance... They literally don't have it." Airbnb "has lost some of that exclusivity" as Booking.com gained a US foothold and Vrbo stayed dominant. Over the last three years OTAs have competed for supply with **rebates tied to revenue targets** and **pay-for-placement**; "Vrbo has been the most aggressive by far... Airbnb definitely has their backs against the wall." Airbnb is "100% a partner, not a competitor" to managers.

- Read-through: the 6–10% host-fee pilot (Aug 2026), hotel price-match credits, and the flat 2026 take-rate guide are the symptoms of this. Bernstein's 15.5% single-fee take-rate path assumes pricing power that professional hosts say Airbnb does not have.
- Sponsored listings already exist in economic substance on the host side (paid placement, rebates); the question for ABNB is whether they become a revenue line or stay a cost of retaining supply.

**Fee stack on a $100 night (T5).** Airbnb ~15%; property manager commission 20–25% of rent, plus guest-paid fees (cleaning, damage waiver, booking fee) that the manager keeps 100%. Average manager "take rate" ~30%, "average platform" ~40%. So an owner using a manager on Airbnb nets roughly $60–65. Management fees have drifted down slightly since 2018 (Vacasa/TurnKey era) and PE entrants offer 0% first-year commission; owner retention 80–85%/yr. Manager pricing is a flat annual contract, one-year term, 90-day notice.

**Europe (T4).** Awaze pays Airbnb "15–16%" (later "16–17%") and treats it as one channel among Vrbo, HomeToGo, own site, Google. A VRMC "will always come at a higher take rate than Airbnb" because it does cleaning, inspections, key handling. The expert says the risk of homeowners forcing commissions down is "rather low" because "Airbnb and the rest have set a plateau." Supports a floor for Airbnb's host fee but not expansion.

**Booking's own take rate (T1).** Net take rate ~14.5% in 2025; Street models ~13.6% flat; expert sees a small improvement from AI search converting better, but "nothing out there right now that would make a huge difference" and no path back to the 15–16% pre-pandemic level while booking windows stay short and prices high. Useful comp framing: Airbnb's 13.2% is already close to Booking's and neither expert sees structural upside.

**India (T3).** Airbnb used to charge standalone (non-channel-manager) hosts a lower commission plus a guest fee; it is now moving them to the higher host commission. The expert's conclusion: "no incentive now for them to remain unbranded... this will give an advantage to the aggregators." Confirms the single-fee migration is global and that it pushes supply toward professional managers.

## 2. AI: threat or lever

**Disintermediation is small and slow (T2, T1).** Booking's AI PM estimates only ~3% of accommodation bookings shift to AI-native transactions in 12–24 months (weekend trips under ~$1,000, free-cancellation only), with **no EBITDA impact** in that window. OTAs took ~30 years (1996–2026) to reach ~50% share; AI won't take 50% in two. Trust and post-purchase servicing (payments, local methods, cancellations, chargebacks) are the moat. The ex-Booking director agrees: metasearch and business-travel sites are most at risk, "slop travel" stories hurt AI booking trust, and there are no switching costs so users default to brands that fix problems.

**Where Airbnb is exposed (T2).** The one scenario that lowers margins is if LLMs cannibalise *existing direct traffic*: "Airbnb has a huge direct traffic. If they do not come to Airbnb but they go to ChatGPT... it will translate into paying a higher margin." Airbnb's 90%-direct mix is a bigger asset and a bigger exposure than Booking's ~60%. Referral fees would be "some percentage" but not drastic because fulfilment stays with the OTA.

**Where Airbnb benefits (T2).** Natural-language "vibe" search helps long-tail inventory get discovered; the expert expects Airbnb-type aggregators' top line to benefit "after 24 months," mainly in Western Europe and the US, and sizes the Gen Z long-tail segment going from ~15% to 16–17% of OTA revenue over two years. Structured amenity data still matters, so Airbnb's listing-data investment (AI highlights, comparisons) is necessary, not optional. AI bundling/upsell could lift OTA basket size by "5% at best."

**Who wins the AI-native interface (T2).** Not the OTAs: culture is optimised for existing verticals, and Google/Amazon/Meta have UX, trust and cross-category learning. Markets most likely to trust an AI OTA: Japan, Western Europe, Australia, high-income users. Relevant to Chesky's claim that Airbnb search will be AI-native within a year.

**AI as cost lever (T1).** Booking already sees lower cost per call and faster handling of simple tickets; consistent with Airbnb's −16% support cost per booking. Nobody in these calls disputes the cost story.

## 3. Loyalty, connected trip and the "one-stop shop" race

- **Booking's loyalty is the moat Airbnb lacks (T1).** Genius Level 2/3 members drive high-50s to low-60s % of Booking's room nights, up ~5 pts YoY; US direct traffic is high-50s to mid-60s and growing every quarter; a no-fee co-branded card launched in 2025. Direct traffic converts better, cancels less, repeats more. Airbnb has only Delta miles and loyalty "tests."
- **Connected trip is a 10-year build (T1).** Booking's connected transactions are low double digits of total today, high-teens by Q4 2026, 20s in 2027; ~50% is "close to 10 years best case." Booking is still building the merchant model needed for dynamic packaging. Contrast with Chesky's "book me a two-week trip to Scotland" within 18 months. Either Airbnb's services model (partner-fulfilled, asset-light) is faster, or the timeline is optimistic.
- **Booking's view of Airbnb (T1).** "Their priorities seem to be a little bit more fragmented" now that hotels, experiences, luggage, meals and possibly flights are added. In Europe, Booking has "significantly more inventory... in the short-term rental space than Airbnb and they're a much stronger brand" and is "not concerned about losing share." In the US, "Airbnb is a verb." Airbnb's target growth countries named: Brazil, Japan, Australia. Expedia is focused on B2B, not consumer, so near-term competitive pressure on Booking is low.
- **Other entrants (T1):** Uber Travel (brand is local, like Tripadvisor's failed booking push), banks such as Chase/Citi with millions of customers, and B2B marketplaces are seen as bigger threats to OTAs than AI.

## 4. Demand and macro (mid-2026)

- **Vacation rentals sit on the value side of leisure (T5, T4).** 2026 is "extremely" strong in the US because air and international travel got expensive; groups split costs and cook. In the GFC, ADR fell in 2009–10 but occupancy offset it. Supports resilience of nights in a slowdown, at the cost of ADR.
- **Europe is flat to low single digit (T4).** Affordability, not fear, is the constraint: fuel and utilities from the Middle East conflict, inflation, higher rates. Staycation helps UK domestic but families cut the third and fourth trip. Last-minute booking is rising because people wait to see if they have the money and what the weather is; a bad summer costs 10–15% of bookings. Promotions of 5–10% raise volume but not profit.
- **Booking's regional benchmarks (T1):** APAC mid-to-high teens (13–18%), North America high-single to low-double digits, Europe mid-to-high single digits and "market share defence." Middle East cost Booking "a couple of points" in Q1 2026; mid-scale travel rebounded by July; length of stay still shorter. Airbnb's Q2 2026 regional nights (NA HSD, EMEA HSD, LatAm ~20%, APAC high-teens) roughly match Booking, so Airbnb's out-growth is mostly ADR, FX and product, not regional mix.

## 5. Supply, regulation and host economics

- **US supply is hyper-fragmented (T5).** ~2M professionally managed rentals, average firm ~100 units, ~20,000 firms; all PE roll-ups combined ~10% share. Platform managers trade at 13.5–15.5x EBITDA, small firms 3–7x, distressed 1–3x. Alpine bought TowneBank's VRM for $250M (Apr 2026); Ares bought Awayday (May 2025). Florida: ~60% of properties in condo/HOA associations with captive rental programs.
- **Europe (T4).** ~18M second homes, ~3M let, ~1M urban / ~2M non-urban. Manager portfolios are flat (Awaze 24–25k, Sykes ~20k, Travel Chapter ~15k); annual owner churn ~15% (high-single-digit uncontrollable plus 7–8% dissatisfaction). "You need to continue to acquire properties just to stand still." Non-exclusive listings are double-counted across platforms, so listing counts overstate supply.
- **Regulation is a cost and a supply driver at once (T4).** 60–90-day caps in big cities, EU VAT on rentals since 2023, UK second-home tax, wealth-tax risk; buy-to-let owners exiting. Some owners start letting to cover the new costs, others leave. Net effect on Airbnb supply is ambiguous rather than one-way negative.
- **Marketing cost inflation (T4).** "SEO is disappearing. You have to pay for every click, and that has become much more expensive." Relevant to Airbnb's S&M growing 27–33% and to the value of its direct traffic.
- **Airbnb will not buy managers (T4).** "Why would you? ... what you buy is a relationship for one year." Managers keep their best properties for direct channels. Consistent with Airbnb's partner-led services model and its WeRoad minority stake rather than acquisitions.

## 6. India sizing (T3)

- Alt-acco ~INR 30 crore/day, ~8% of ~INR 370 crore domestic hotels; ~INR 10,000 crore (~$1.2B) a year. Growing faster than hotels (hotels ~12–13% in 2026, boosted by domestic substitution during the war). ~2.5–3.5 lakh rooms; ~80k–100k use channel managers (30–35%). Occupancy: hotels 60–65%, alt-acco ~40%. Branded villa ADR 2–3x average; unbranded villas price like hotels.
- Read-through: Airbnb's India headlines (first-time bookers doubled, origin nights +60%) sit on a ~$1B GBV category. India is a narrative for the deck, not a number in the model.

## 7. How to use this in the pitch

1. **Take rate slide:** cite T5 and T4 for "host fee has a floor near 15–16% but no pricing power above it"; contrast with Bernstein's 15.5% path. The 6–10% pilot is the tell.
2. **AI slide:** use T2's 3% / no-EBITDA-impact estimate to neutralise the disintermediation bear, but note Airbnb's direct-traffic exposure is the largest in the group.
3. **Competition slide:** Booking's loyalty (60% of nights from Genius 2/3) and European STR supply lead are the sharpest bear points on Airbnb's moat; Airbnb's US brand ("a verb") is the counter.
4. **Demand slide:** value-side resilience (nights hold, ADR gives) supports a downside case where nights grow and take rate and ADR compress.
5. **New businesses slide:** Booking's 10-year connected-trip path is the base rate against Chesky's 18-month one-stop-shop target.
