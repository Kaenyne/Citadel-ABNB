# 03. Can macro, category or alt data nowcast Airbnb's quarterly KPIs?

**Date:** 2026-09-06
**Author:** Krishang Surapaneni (compiled with Claude Code)
**Question:** Is there any series we hold, available before the print, that nowcasts or leads ABNB nights y/y, nights acceleration, GBV y/y, ADR y/y (reported and ex-FX), the revenue beat vs the guide midpoint, or the 1-day reaction?
**Script and outputs:** `analysis/src/predictive/03_nowcast_tests.py` -> `data/processed/predictive/03_nowcast_results.csv` (890 macro pairs + 10 alt-data pairs, one row per series x transform x lag x target, statistics on three windows), `03_quarterly_panel.csv` (the aligned panel), `03_altdata_quarterly.csv`, `03_cc_velocity_annual.csv`, `03_booking_curve_ranking.csv`.

**Sources.** ABNB KPIs and the letters' ex-FX ADR: `data/processed/abnb_kpi_vs_category_quarterly.csv` and `data/raw/letters/*.htm` (ex-FX ADR hardcoded in the script for 2Q22 to 2Q26; 3Q23 and 4Q23 are "less than 1%", coded 0.5; letters before 2Q22 give no global ex-FX figure). Guidance vs actual: `data/external/abnb_revenue_guidance_vs_actual.csv`. Reactions: `data/external/abnb_earnings_reactions.csv`. BEA PCE underlying detail (accommodations, hotels, inbound and outbound travel; nominal, real, price): `data/raw/bea/bea_pce_travel_monthly_2015_2026.csv`. FRED keyless CSVs: `data/raw/fred/` (CPI lodging SA, airfare, all items) and `data/raw/macro/` (gitignored, downloaded 2026-09-05 via `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=SERIES`: CUUR0000SEHB, CUSR0000SETA01, UMCSENT, PSAVERT, UNRATE, RSXFS, PCEC96, DSPIC96, PCES, PCEDG, USLAH, AIRRPMTSID11, TRFVOLUSM227NFWA, ICSA, DTWEXBGS, DEXUSEU). Theo's `analysis/src/acquisition/run_macro.py` (branch `theo/alt-data-acquisition`) pulls the same FRED set through the keyed API into `raw_expansion/`, which is not present in this worktree, so the keyless CSVs were used; his TSA pull returned HTTP 403. Alt data: `data/external/inside_airbnb_like_for_like.csv`, `inside_airbnb_city_snapshots.csv`, `cc_matched_listings.csv`, `booking_curves_by_market.csv`. Prior work read first: `theos-past-research/research/edge-discovery/*` (every source INCONCLUSIVE or WATCH; nothing was tested against outcomes) and his IC-brief metrics (activity-to-guidance Pearson 0.78 on n=16, guidance-to-return 0.08).

**Method.** Monthly (or daily/weekly) series are averaged by calendar quarter and expressed as y/y % (indices, levels) or as the quarterly level (rates: sentiment, saving rate, unemployment). Each predictor enters as a level and as a first difference, at lag 0 (same quarter as the KPI) and lag 1. Per pair: n, Pearson r and p, Spearman, permutation p (1,000 shuffles of the target), leave-one-out OLS nowcast RMSE against three benchmarks (naive last quarter, seasonal naive t-4, LOO mean; for the beat also zero), sign stability across halves, Benjamini-Hochberg q and Bonferroni across all 890 pairs. Everything is computed on three windows: full (1Q21 to 2Q26, n up to 22), ex-2021 (from 1Q22, n up to 18) and post-2022 (from 1Q23, n up to 14). The windows matter more than anything else in this note: nights grew 197% y/y in 2Q21 and 59% in 1Q22, and every reopening series did the same, so full-sample correlations are leverage artefacts. **The post-2022 window is the one to read.**

---

## Bottom line

1. **The only clean, usable nowcast is FX, and it is mechanical.** EUR/USD y/y (quarter average) explains the FX contribution to reported ADR growth (reported minus ex-FX, from the letters) with r = +0.99 on n = 17 (perm p 0.001; post-2022 r = +0.97, LOO RMSE 0.57 pp vs 2.11 pp naive and 2.29 pp LOO-mean). The trade-weighted dollar does the same (r = -0.96; fitted FX effect = 0.5 - 0.72 x USD y/y). Reported ADR y/y itself is -0.92 correlated with the dollar since 2023 (LOO RMSE 1.17 pp vs 1.93 naive). These are the only pairs that survive Bonferroni on the post-2022 window without being trend artefacts (see 3). For the 3Q26 print: the dollar is -0.4% y/y quarter-to-date (to 28 Aug), so the FX tailwind to ADR is roughly +0.8 pp, down from +1.3 pp in 2Q26 and +5.0 pp in 1Q26; reported ADR growth should converge on the ex-FX run-rate (3 to 4%). That is a specific, checkable call and it is already knowable.

2. **Nothing nowcasts nights or GBV growth in the current regime.** BEA hotels nominal spend y/y (lag 0) has r = +0.88 with nights y/y post-2022 (LOO RMSE 1.65 pp vs 3.02 naive; Bonferroni-significant), but Spearman is only 0.71, r falls to 0.64 when 1Q23 is dropped and to 0.28 from 1Q24 onward (n = 10). It is the 2023 normalisation trend: nights decelerated 19 -> 9 while every US nominal series decelerated 21 -> 0. Since 2024 nights y/y has sat in a 7 to 12 band while BEA accommodations ranged -3.7 to +6.2 with no relationship. Proof that these are common-trend hits: all-items CPI (r = +0.85) and new-vehicle CPI (r = +0.78) "nowcast" Airbnb nights just as well as lodging spend does.

3. **Nights acceleration: a weak coincident signal, not a lead.** Same-quarter change in BEA accommodations nominal y/y vs change in nights y/y: r = +0.71 post-2022 (perm p 0.016, BH q 0.11), retail sales ex-auto change r = +0.64 (q 0.21); LOO RMSE 2.4 to 2.7 pp vs 4.9 naive but only vs 3.2 for the LOO mean. The full-sample r of 0.95 is COVID. Neither survives BH at 10% post-2022, and both are lag 0 (BEA month-3 data lands about a week before the print, current vintage only). No lag-1 pair leads nights acceleration.

4. **ADR ex-FX vs initial jobless claims y/y is the one non-mechanical pair that survives everything, and I would still call it WATCH.** r = -0.86 on n = 17 (perm p 0.001), post-2022 r = -0.78 (Spearman -0.82, BH q 0.04), r = -0.83 even from 1Q24 onward; LOO RMSE 0.82 pp vs 1.05 naive and 1.19 mean. Direction: rising claims, weaker ex-FX pricing. Airfare CPI y/y is the runner-up (r = +0.79 / +0.70 post-2022, q 0.11). Caveats: the target is integer-rounded (0.5 to 12), claims y/y is dominated by 2022 base effects (-70% to +10%) and both series are trending. Claims are -10% y/y quarter-to-date in 3Q26, which under this relationship says ex-FX ADR holds at 4%; treat as a hypothesis to score in November, not a model input.

5. **Revenue beat vs guide midpoint: no macro series helps; the constant does.** ABNB has beaten the midpoint in 19 of 19 guided quarters (4Q21 to 2Q26), mean +2.5%, range +0.9 to +6.8, post-2022 mean +2.2% with std 1.15; 15 of 19 landed at or above the high end. RMSE of "assume zero beat" is 2.97 pp; the LOO-mean beat 1.59 pp; the best available macro pair post-2022 (retail sales ex-auto change, r = -0.55) reaches 1.06 pp but has BH q 0.33 and flips sign in the full sample. Full-sample "hits" (air RPM change, r = -0.70) collapse ex-2021 (-0.35). The share gap (nights y/y minus BEA real accommodations) at lag 1 has r = -0.05 with the beat ex-2021. A pitch should model the beat as a +2% conservatism bias and not pretend to forecast its variation.

6. **1-day excess return: nothing, consistent with Theo's guidance-to-return result.** Best full-sample |r| is 0.41 (PCE durables, lag 1), no perm p under 0.05; the best post-2022 pair (saving rate, r = -0.70) is +0.03 in the full sample, a sign flip. The macro that is known before the print is priced before the print.

7. **Alt data: no signal yet, and mostly for coverage reasons.** Inside Airbnb like-for-like price change vs ADR y/y: r = +0.07 (n = 8, 4Q23 to 3Q25, Rome alone until 4Q24); Inside Airbnb dropped listed prices from late-2025 dumps, so this series cannot be extended. Matched-listing review growth vs nights y/y r = +0.20 (n = 11), all-listing reviews_ltm y/y r = +0.30; the city mix goes from 1 city to 13 across the sample so the series is not comparable through time. Common Crawl review velocity y/y vs annual nights growth r = +0.44 on n = 5 (meaningless). The booking-curve file is one snapshot per market and is used only descriptively below.

**Multiple comparisons.** 890 macro pairs were tested (25 series x 2 transforms x 2 lags x 8 targets, less the circular share-gap combinations). At 5% we expect about 45 false positives, and the pairs are far from independent (each series appears in 4 transforms; the six BEA series and three CPI series are near-collinear), so BH is approximate. Post-2022 window: 128 pairs pass the |r| > 0.5 and perm p < 0.05 flag, 37 have BH q < 0.10, 24 have q < 0.05, 5 pass Bonferroni (0.05/890): EUR/USD and the dollar vs the FX effect on ADR, the dollar vs reported ADR, and BEA accommodations and hotels nominal vs nights y/y. Of the 24 with q < 0.05, 18 are nights-y/y level pairs that I attribute to the 2023 trend (finding 2). 36 pairs are flagged with the same sign on all three windows; 11 of those are the FX mechanism, 9 are nights-level trend pairs, 6 are ADR ex-FX (claims, airfare, saving rate, real DPI). Full-sample counts (265 flagged, 73 Bonferroni) are reported in the CSV but are COVID artefacts and should not be quoted.

## Alt data

**Inside Airbnb like-for-like price vs ADR.** Year-ago matched-listing median price change, averaged across cities by the quarter of the later dump (simple and weighted by matched priced entire homes), 4Q23 to 3Q25, n = 8. Against reported ADR y/y: r = +0.07 (p 0.87), weighted +0.10; against ADR ex-FX: +0.12 / +0.09; LOO RMSE 2.16 pp vs 1.67 for naive last quarter, so worse than doing nothing. The series is Rome alone for four quarters, Rome and Paris for two, four cities for two; Inside Airbnb removed listed prices from dumps after about September 2025 (`price_comparable` is False for 145 of 258 pairs), so no further quarters can be built from this source. Verdict: **no signal, and the source is dead for prices**. Realised price will have to come from the calendar files Theo pulled (per-date price alongside availability), captured monthly.

**Inside Airbnb review activity vs nights.** Matched-listing trailing-12-month review change (survivor listings only) averaged across cities: r = +0.20 with nights y/y (n = 11, 4Q23 to 2Q26, p 0.56); against nights acceleration r = -0.55 (p 0.08), the wrong sign for a nowcast and driven by the 2024 Rome-only quarters. All-listing `reviews_ltm_sum` y/y from dumps 11 to 13 months apart: r = +0.30 with nights y/y, -0.34 with GBV y/y, +0.28 with the beat; listing-count y/y r = +0.17. The composition moves from 1 city (Rome) to 13 across the sample, and review growth in these cities ran +35% to -8% while global nights sat at 7 to 12%, so the level is not comparable across quarters or with the global KPI. Verdict: **inconclusive; needs a fixed city panel**. Rome's own reviews_ltm y/y decelerated from +35% (Dec 2023) to +8% (May to Jun 2026) while global nights held 9 to 10%, so a single city is not a proxy for the platform.

**Common Crawl review velocity vs annual nights growth** (`03_cc_velocity_annual.csv`). Median reviews per year on matched listings with 90+ days between captures, by year of the later capture, 250 pairs per year: 9.4 (2021), 11.5, 17.4, 13.1, 12.3, 12.5 (2026 to date). Year-over-year change in velocity vs annual nights growth (2022 +31%, 2023 +14%, 2024 +10%, 2025 +8%, 1H26 +10%): r = +0.44 on n = 5; velocity level vs nights growth r = -0.25. 2023 velocity rose 52% while nights grew 14%; 2024 velocity fell 25% while nights grew 10%. n is five and the panel is 250 listings a year drawn from whatever Common Crawl happened to capture (the 2026 mean is negative because a few listings lost their review counts, so only the median is usable). Verdict: **nothing testable here**; the CC panel is a supply-quality panel (Superhost 58% -> 61%, Guest Favorite 71% -> 52% of captures, 2025 to 2026), not a demand series.

**Booking-curve snapshot, descriptive only** (`03_booking_curve_ranking.csv`; 120 markets, one snapshot each between 14 June and 10 August 2026; "blocked" includes host blocks, not only bookings). Deepest 31-to-60-day blocked rates: Copenhagen 0.85 (23k listings), Menorca 0.76, Pays Basque 0.76, Amsterdam 0.74, Mallorca 0.73, Stockholm 0.73, Girona 0.68, Oslo 0.67, Euskadi 0.67, Crete 0.66; all European summer markets captured in late June, so the ranking is largely a seasonality-and-capture-date ranking. Shallowest: Nairobi 0.06, Istanbul 0.15, Bogota 0.17, Bangkok 0.18, Singapore 0.18, Belize 0.20, Sao Paulo 0.21, Newark 0.23, Dallas 0.24, Mornington Peninsula 0.24. Europe's curves are also flatter (Copenhagen 0.85 at 31-60d vs 0.77 at 91-180d) than the US and LatAm ones (Dallas 0.24 vs 0.22; Bogota 0.17 vs 0.12), consistent with longer European lead times. None of this becomes a nowcast until the same market is captured monthly at the same horizon so a y/y at fixed horizon exists; one snapshot cannot separate seasonality from momentum.

## What to build forward (so a real test exists by the pitch)

1. **FX-adjusted ADR tracker, now.** Quarter-to-date EUR/USD and trade-weighted USD y/y -> FX effect on ADR via the fitted 0.5 - 0.72 x USD y/y (or the EUR/USD fit) -> reported ADR y/y = ex-FX run-rate + FX effect. Update weekly; the 3Q26 reading is +0.8 pp FX vs +1.3 in 2Q26 and +5.0 in 1Q26. This is the one thing in this note that belongs on a slide.
2. **Monthly Inside Airbnb capture on a fixed city panel** (the 13 cities already in hand plus Theo's 34 US markets), same day each month, storing per-market: listings, reviews_l30d and reviews_ltm sums, calendar blocked rate at 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 91-180 days, and the calendar per-date median price. Twelve captures give one y/y point per market at fixed horizon; the test is blocked-rate y/y and calendar-price y/y in month 2 of the quarter against the print six weeks later. Nothing shorter than a year of captures will produce a usable test, so start in September.
3. **Point-in-time BEA and CPI vintages.** Our BEA file is the revised vintage. Log the first-print month-3 values for accommodations and hotels (nominal and price) each release from now, so the "mostly available" flag can be checked against what an analyst actually saw. ALFRED has the CPI vintages if a FRED key is synced (Theo's ADR pilot stalled on that).
4. **Weekly initial-claims y/y and airfare CPI as the two ADR-ex-FX watch series**; score the 3Q26 and 4Q26 prints against the finding-4 relationship before promoting it.
5. **Do not spend more time on** the revenue beat (model it as +2%), the 1-day reaction, the share gap as a lead, the Common Crawl panel as a demand series, or Inside Airbnb listed prices (discontinued).
6. **If the acquisition layer is re-run**, add Eurostat `tour_ce_*` platform nights (monthly, 32 countries, 2018 to 2026, on Theo's branch) as the only free source that measures platform stays directly; it is the natural nights nowcast candidate and was not testable here because the pulled files are not in this worktree.
