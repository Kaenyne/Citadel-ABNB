## 2. KPIs people actually underwrite (ranked by how often they decide the stock)

1. **Nights & Seats Booked growth.** The single most-cited number. 7% (Q2'25) → 9% → 10% (Q4'25) → 9% (Q1'26) → 10% (Q2'26); Q3'26 guide "low double digits". Bernstein's whole PT rests on "five consecutive quarters of ~10%". Bears (Wolf of Harcourt, Morgan Stanley) anchor on the 8% Q1'25 trough and on supply/occupancy.
2. **GBV growth and ADR.** GBV +16% (Q2'26), +19% (Q1'26, FX-aided). ADR +5% (+4% ex-FX). Bulls like that GBV > nights (pricing, mix to larger homes); bears note ~3 pts of 2026 growth is FX.
3. **Take rate (revenue / GBV).** The most contested metric. Q2'26 13.2% flat YoY; LTM down ~31 bps; guided **flat for 2026** after May guidance of "modest upside". Drivers: RNPL timing (payments shift toward check-in), hotel price-match credits and other incentives, offset by single-fee migration (15.5%, ~50% of listings, rest by YE26) and insurance (+60%). Bulls (Bernstein) model take rate → 15.5%; bears (Phaetrix, MBI) call it the tell. New wrinkle: 6–10% host-fee test for host-sourced bookings (Aug 2026), the first explicit take-rate *cut*.
4. **Adj. EBITDA margin / FCF margin.** FY26 guide ≥35.5%; TTM FCF $4.8B (37%). Q3'26 margin guided down slightly on investment timing. Every skeptic adjusts for SBC ($1.4B, ~12–13% of revenue): SBC-adjusted FCF margin ~25%, SBC-adj FCF yield ~4.2% at $140.
5. **Sales & marketing intensity.** S&M +27% in Q2'26 (+33% Q1) vs revenue +17%. Bears: growth is bought; bulls: 90% of traffic still direct/unpaid.
6. **First-time bookers.** +11% Q2'26, best in 4 years; Gen Z; India first-timers doubled, Brazil +40%. Used as the "structural not transitory" evidence.
7. **App share of nights.** 64% (from 59%); app nights +23%. Read as engagement/CAC proxy.
8. **Hotels.** Single-digit % of nights, growing ~3x homes; 35% of first-time hotel guests book a home within a year (55% cited in Q1). 20+ cities, Lark Hotels (75+ properties). Unit economics undisclosed, so this is a key debate.
9. **RNPL.** >20% of global GBV; +1 pt platform cancellation rate (16%→17%); contributed ~3 pts of nights growth in Q1'26. Lapping starts Q3'26 (US) / Q1'27 (global).
10. **Experiences / Services.** Supply +80% YoY; ~50% of Experience bookers have no stay; car rentals "biggest by far", luggage storage "sleeper hit". Management: 3–5 years to materiality. Most-mocked part of the bull case on X.
11. **Customer-support cost per booking.** −16% YoY; AI resolves ~45% of tickets. The one AI number everyone agrees is real.
12. **Regional mix.** NA 42%, EMEA 39%, LatAm 9%, APAC 9%; expansion markets growing ~2x core; LatAm nights ~+20%, APAC high-teens.
13. **Capital return.** $2.1B bought back H1'26, $3.4B authorization left, diluted shares 652M vs 673M; net share count down ~9% since 2022. $2.5B IG bond (A-/Baa1, Mar 2026) refinanced the 2026 convert.
14. **Bedroom nights** (new, Q2'26). +12% vs +10% nights; >1B TTM. Management's counter to "nights understate demand".

**Implication for our deck:** the Street's PT dispersion ($125–$220) is almost entirely a *multiple* debate on a fairly consensual ~10–12% revenue growth path. Differentiated work has to move either (a) the nights/take-rate path (RNPL lapping, single fee, host-fee test, hotels incentives) or (b) the SBC/marketing-adjusted margin, not the multiple.

## 4. What people care about most (the live debates)

1. **Is the reacceleration structural or borrowed?** Bulls: 5 quarters of ~10% nights, first-timers +11%, core markets HSD. Bears: ~2–4 pts came from RNPL + cancellation-policy changes + fee migration (management's own attribution), ~3 pts from FX, World Cup one-off; RNPL laps from Q3'26.
2. **Take rate: expansion story or incentive treadmill?** Single fee (→15.5%) and insurance vs RNPL timing, hotel credits, and now a 6–10% host-fee pilot. Guidance already slipped from "modest upside" (May) to "flat" (Aug).
3. **Are the new businesses worth anything?** Hotels is the first one the Street believes (3x growth, conversion to homes, Booking-veteran hires). Experiences/Services remain the punchline on X. No disclosed unit economics for any of them.
4. **AI: cost lever vs disintermediation risk.** Consensus accepts support-cost savings (−16%/booking) and dev velocity (+80% features). Open questions: AI search conversion, agentic-web bypass of cross-listed inventory (Speedwell), and whether Chesky's outside AI lab (Jun 2026) dilutes focus.
5. **Supply and regulation.** Morgan Stanley's listings-growth deceleration; NYC LL18 (−90% supply), Chicago suit (Jun 2026), NYC suit (Apr 2026), Barcelona 2028, Paris/Amsterdam caps, Spain's 86k delistings; US host occupancy 57%→50%. Counter: no city >2% of revenue, 70% of GBV from 5 countries.
6. **Quality of cash and margins.** SBC ~12–13% of revenue; guest float ($12B) inside "cash"; S&M growing 1.5x revenue; product-dev +298% since 2018 with unclear ROI; Q3'26 margin guided down.
7. **Governance / insiders.** Founders ~90% votes with ~27% economics; CEO comp tranches up to $485; steady 10b5-1 selling by Blecharczyk and Gebbia (Jul–Aug 2026), Chesky gifting shares; hedge-fund holders fell 87→75 in Q2'26.
