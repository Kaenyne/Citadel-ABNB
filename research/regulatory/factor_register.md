# ABNB regulatory factor register

As of **5 September 2026**. See [research memo and methodology](README.md). Each factor separates the rule, observed evidence and analyst inference. Source dates can be unknown or month-only; access dates do not establish publication dates.

## Tier 1: Implemented regulation and enforcement (including subsequent reversals)

### REG-01 | United States / New York City | Local Law 18: transaction verification makes existing STR limits enforceable

**Priority within tier:** 1 · **Status:** Enforced

**Dates:** Enacted 2022-01-09; platform enforcement 2023-09-05; FY26 update available

**Rule / event:** Platforms must verify registration before processing covered bookings. Residential stays under 30 days generally require a permanent occupant present and no more than two paying guests. Rent-regulated and NYCHA units cannot register.

**Observed evidence:** Airbnb disclosed NYC represented approximately 1% of global revenue before enforcement. OSE reports over 3,500 active approved hosts in FY26; these are registrations, not Airbnb listing counts.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Best documented case for how a city can restrict Airbnb's bookable supply through the payment/booking platform. Lost local nights may partly migrate to nearby destinations, longer stays or hotels sold by Airbnb.

**Limits on inference:** The 1% is historical gross exposure, not a measured 1% consolidated revenue loss. LL18 enforces longstanding housing rules; compliant home-sharing remains legal. City housing-benefit claims and Airbnb's claims about hotel prices are not causal estimates.

**Next catalyst:** Registration renewals expected October 2026; enforcement and any enacted amendments.

**Sources:** [REG-S01: NYC: enforcement of short-term rental registration](https://www.nyc.gov/site/specialenforcement/registration-law/enforcement.page); [REG-S02: Airbnb Q3 2023 shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559720/000119312523268164/d481318dex991.htm); [REG-S03: NYC FY26 registration results (page lacks exact publication date)](https://www.nyc.gov/site/specialenforcement/news/new-yorkers-registered-to-host-surpassed-3500-for-first-time.page)

### REG-02 | Spain / national consumer enforcement | Listing removals and EUR64.1m Airbnb fine

**Priority within tier:** 2 · **Status:** Implemented enforcement; fine disputed; May 2026 surety bond suspends enforcement pending court proceedings

**Dates:** Removal order 2025-05-19; removals July 2025; fine December 2025; stay denied March 2026

**Rule / event:** Consumer authorities pursued missing/incorrect regional licence identifiers, misleading host identity information and failures to comply with information/removal orders.

**Observed evidence:** Ministry reported 65,122 offending advertisements removed by July 2025. The December 2025 fine was EUR64,055,311. A March 2026 request to stay payment was denied, but the Q2 2026 Form 10-Q subsequently discloses a EUR70m ($80m) surety bond obtained in May 2026 to suspend enforcement pending resolution. Airbnb describes the fine at approximately EUR65m ($76m) and the potential loss as neither probable nor estimable.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Direct platform liability plus reduced bookable inventory makes this the clearest national enforcement case. Cash/legal expense and recurring supply risk require separate model lines.

**Limits on inference:** Ads are not necessarily unique productive properties. Removal orders overlap. Bond face value is not a recognized expense or proof of equal cash payment; bond premium/collateral not quantified here. Do not assume the consumer fine is automatically excluded from adjusted EBITDA.

**Next catalyst:** Court resolution, bond conditions, and changes in accounting recognition or cash settlement.

**Sources:** [REG-S04: Spain ministry: court denies stay of Airbnb fine](https://www.dsca.gob.es/es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/justicia-obliga-airbnb-pagar-64-millones-euros-previstos-multa-que-le); [REG-S05: AP: Spain orders nearly 66,000 Airbnb listings blocked](https://apnews.com/article/a91e605f6342db1b169d4f9d1681e9d4); [REG-S45: Airbnb Q2 2026 10-Q: Spain fine and surety bond](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559720/000155972026000027/abnb-20260630.htm)

**Related factors:** REG-03

### REG-03 | Spain / national registration | 2025 national registration rollout; 2026 partial annulment

**Priority within tier:** 3 · **Status:** Implemented historically; material provisions annulled

**Dates:** Operational rollout 2025-07-01; judgment 2026-05-19, published 2026-06-08; consolidated law updated 2026-07-18

**Rule / event:** RD1312/2024 introduced a national registration procedure and digital data gateway. The Supreme Court annulled the single-registry procedure and property/movable-register inscription requirement, including Articles 5 and 8-10 and related provisions.

**Observed evidence:** The official consolidated BOE text explicitly records annulment; this changes the status of widely reported 2025 registration requirements.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Historical delistings still matter, but continuing national-register supply haircuts cannot be based on the original decree alone. Regional licensing and surviving EU reporting obligations remain distinct.

**Limits on inference:** Current replacement/implementation mechanics need further confirmation before host-level modeling. The judgment does not establish that all regional STR rules or the consumer fine were cancelled.

**Next catalyst:** Replacement legislation, platform implementation notices, and any reactivation of listings.

**Sources:** [REG-S06: Spain Royal Decree 1312/2024, consolidated through July 2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-26931); [REG-S07: Supreme Court judgment of May 19, published June 8](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2026-12300)

**Related factors:** REG-02, REG-17

### REG-04 | Greece / Athens districts 1, 2 and 3 | Freeze on first registrations; transfer-related loss of eligibility

**Priority within tier:** 4 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** 2025-01-01 through 2026-12-31; Law 5313/2026 adds current transfer treatment

**Rule / event:** New first registrations are prohibited in specified central districts. Current government guidance says covered properties transferred during life are removed from the registry and cannot re-register while the restriction lasts.

**Observed evidence:** Government housing portal confirms the 2026 extension and transfer restriction; minimum first-offence fine EUR20,000.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Restricts new supply and can erode incumbent supply as ownership changes. This is more consequential than a purely grandfathered new-entry freeze.

**Limits on inference:** No company-disclosed city revenue loss identified. Not an Athens-wide or Greece-wide ban on existing compliant STRs.

**Next catalyst:** Year-end extension, enforcement against transfer/re-registration workarounds.

**Sources:** [REG-S11: Greek government housing portal: temporary STR restrictions](https://stegasi.gov.gr/programs/prosorinoi-periorismoi-stis-vrachychronies-misthoseis/)

**Related factors:** REG-05, REG-20

### REG-06 | Portugal / Lisbon | Revised AL containment zones

**Priority within tier:** 5 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** Approved 2025-12-02; published 2025-12-05

**Rule / event:** Absolute containment applies at ratios of at least 10% AL establishments to permanent housing at municipal/parish/neighbourhood levels; relative containment at 5% to below 10%. New permissions and transfers are constrained, subject to exceptions.

**Observed evidence:** Final published regulation confirms thresholds; earlier proposal used different thresholds. The final text controls. Follow-up: city reported cancellation of 6,765 registrations described as inactive because required insurance evidence was missing. This is a separate compliance-cleanup cohort, not 6,765 demonstrated productive Airbnb removals.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Limits growth in saturated neighbourhoods even after national liberalisation. Transfer restrictions and limited exceptions affect replacement supply and host property values.

**Limits on inference:** Ratios use regulatory establishments and census housing, not Airbnb active listings. Do not assume a citywide removal of all existing AL or use an earlier temporary suspension as the current rule.

**Next catalyst:** Monthly ratio monitoring, containment maps, exceptions and licence transfers.

**Sources:** [REG-S10: Lisbon amended municipal AL regulation](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/aviso/29926-a-2025-964380181); [REG-S47: Lisbon: cancellation of 6,765 inactive local accommodation registrations](https://informacao.lisboa.pt/en/news/detail/local-authority-cancels-40-of-inactive-local-accommodation-registrations)

**Related factors:** REG-07

### REG-08 | France / Paris | Principal-residence cap reduced from 120 to 90 days

**Priority within tier:** 6 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** 2025-01-01

**Rule / event:** Main residences may be rented as tourist furnished accommodation for 90 days per year instead of 120, subject to legal exceptions. Other premises face separate authorisation rules.

**Observed evidence:** City guidance confirms implementation and ongoing 90-day limit.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Reduces annual sellable nights of hosts who previously rented above 90 days; can particularly constrain peak-demand capacity and increase compliance friction.

**Limits on inference:** A 25% reduction in the legal ceiling is not a 25% reduction in Paris nights or ABNB revenue. Many hosts operate below the cap; professional licensed accommodation follows different rules.

**Next catalyst:** Enforcement and separate proposals targeting multi-property operators.

**Sources:** [REG-S12: Paris lowers principal-residence cap to 90 days](https://www.paris.fr/pages/location-limitee-a-90-jours-paris-serre-la-vis-sur-les-meubles-touristiques-29653)

**Related factors:** REG-26

### REG-10 | Canada / British Columbia | Principal-residence requirement and platform registry verification

**Priority within tier:** 7 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** Residence rule 2024-05-01; registry 2025-05-01

**Rule / event:** Covered communities limit STRs to a principal residence plus one secondary suite or accessory dwelling unit. Provincial registration and platform duties extend beyond the residence-rule geography, with exemptions.

**Observed evidence:** January 2025 government statement described an approximately 10% decline in entire-home listings from March 2024. A newer June 2, 2026 government release reports active STR listings declining from 28,000 to just over 23,000. These are different metric/time references and neither isolates a causal Airbnb revenue loss.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Large subnational example of housing policy restricting investment-property supply and requiring platform participation.

**Limits on inference:** Government-reported listing change is not a causal estimate or ABNB-specific revenue result. Resort/exempt properties and local opt-in/out rules require address-level classification.

**Next catalyst:** Registry enforcement, geographic exemptions and annual opt-in/out changes.

**Sources:** [REG-S14: BC: rules effective May 1 2024](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2024HOUS0020-000590); [REG-S15: BC: province-wide STR registry](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2025HMA0001-000024.htm); [REG-S46: BC housing update: active STR listings from 28,000 to just over 23,000](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026HMA0028-000639.pdf)

### REG-12 | Spain / Madrid | Plan RESIDE and enforcement against illegal tourist flats

**Priority within tier:** 8 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** September 2025

**Rule / event:** City protects residential use and shifts tourist accommodation away from dispersed flats in residential buildings in the historic centre; compliant accommodation categories remain possible.

**Observed evidence:** City's 2025 implementation report confirms September commencement. Q3 2025 Airbnb call names Madrid as a regulation-constrained hotel-pilot market.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Directly connects policy constraints with Airbnb's hotel strategy. Growth may require a different accommodation mix rather than simple host acquisition.

**Limits on inference:** Grandfathering, building use and geography matter. Do not translate the plan into a ban on all Madrid Airbnb supply.

**Next catalyst:** Licence issuance, illegal-unit closures, and hotel-pilot conversion economics.

**Sources:** [REG-S18: Madrid reports 2025 permitting and Plan RESIDE implementation](https://diario.madrid.es/blog/notas-de-prensa/el-ayuntamiento-concedio-en-2025-un-41-mas-de-licencias-residenciales-y-recupero-para-este-uso-un-33-mas-de-vut-ilegales/)

### REG-14 | Italy / Florence | Historic-centre restriction expands to surrounding zones

**Priority within tier:** 9 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** Regulation 2025-05-31; expansion approved 2026-06-04, effective 2026-06-21

**Rule / event:** New STR activities prohibited in zones A1, A3 and A4. Regulation requires five-year authorisations; lawfully operating 2024 units receive a three-year transition under the 2025 framework.

**Observed evidence:** Current city portal confirms the June expansion was enacted, superseding May 2026 Reuters proposal coverage.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Expands the geographic reach of housing-driven supply constraints. End of the transition could affect incumbent supply as well as new entry.

**Limits on inference:** Do not still classify the June expansion as merely proposed. Transitional protection is not an unconditional permanent right; future reductions are not yet quantified.

### REG-07 | Portugal / national | Mais Habitacao restrictions partly reversed

**Priority within tier:** 10 · **Status:** Historical tightening followed by liberalisation

**Dates:** 2023 housing package; Decree-Law 76/2024 published 2024-10-23

**Rule / event:** The 2023 package curtailed new AL registrations with territorial exceptions and added renewal/administrative restrictions. The 2024 decree repealed housing measures and returned stronger discretion to municipalities.

**Observed evidence:** Official original package and later decree document a change in national policy direction.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Useful counterweight to a one-way regulatory bear case: national restrictions can reverse while municipal constraints persist. Model Lisbon separately from rural Portugal.

**Limits on inference:** Do not present the 2023 national registration freeze or automatic renewal regime as unchanged current law. Separate tax repeal details require their own law; not modeled here.

**Next catalyst:** Municipal containment decisions and national changes to the decentralised framework.

**Sources:** [REG-S08: Portugal: original Mais Habitacao AL measures](https://portugal.gov.pt/pt/gc23/comunicacao/noticias/alojamento-local-perguntas-e-respostas); [REG-S09: Portugal Decree-Law 76/2024](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/atualidades/legislativa/Paginas/Decreto_Lei_76_2024.aspx)

**Related factors:** REG-06

### REG-09 | Netherlands / Amsterdam selected districts | 15-night cap in eight central neighbourhoods

**Priority within tier:** 11 · **Status:** In force

**Dates:** 2026-04-01

**Rule / event:** Selected Centre and De Pijp neighbourhoods moved from 30 to 15 holiday-rental nights annually. Nights already rented before April count toward 2026's cap.

**Observed evidence:** Current Dutch city guidance lists eight covered areas and directs hosts to adjust incompatible reservations. Older English page describes a proposal and is stale on status.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Restricts capacity of occasional whole-home hosts in highly visited neighbourhoods and demonstrates further tightening after earlier regulation.

**Limits on inference:** Not a citywide 15-night rule; not all B&B/hotel supply is covered. Halving the cap does not establish a halving of actual bookings.

**Next catalyst:** Compliance, cancellations and any further geographic expansion.

**Sources:** [REG-S13: Amsterdam: 15-night rules in selected Centre/De Pijp districts](https://www.amsterdam.nl/wonen-bouwen-verbouwen/huisvestingsvergunning/vergunning-vakantieverhuur-aanvragen/regels-vakantieverhuur-deel-centrum-en/)

## Tier 3: Proposals, uncertain implementation and blocked initiatives

### REG-25 | European Union | Affordable Housing Act: draft rules for housing-stress areas

**Priority within tier:** 1 · **Status:** Draft reported; not adopted legislation

**Dates:** Reuters 2026-09-04; presentation expected 2026-09-09

**Rule / event:** Reported draft proposes a framework for assessing housing stress and proportionate local STR restrictions; quantitative caps and grandfathering are among options.

**Observed evidence:** Reuters obtained a draft; Commission independently confirms work on an Affordable Housing Act. Parliament and member-state consideration still required.

**ABNB implication (analysis):** Highest-priority near-term policy catalyst because a common framework could facilitate restrictions across multiple countries and challenge a city-diversification defence.
