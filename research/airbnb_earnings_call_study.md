# Airbnb (ABNB): 22 Quarters of Earnings Calls, Q1 2021 – Q2 2026

*Data, narrative history, sell-side tone, forward view, and follow-up Q&A. Compiled September 1, 2026 from the Bloomberg transcripts of all 22 calls plus Airbnb's SEC-filed shareholder letters and 10-K/10-Q disclosures.*

---

## 1. Summary of conclusions

1. **Volume growth has not changed much; monetization and timing have.** Nights (and, since Q2 2025, "nights and seats") booked have grown 7–10% year-over-year for ten consecutive quarters. Reported revenue growth accelerated to 17–18% in H1 2026 because of ADR (+5–9%), a ~3-point FX tailwind, and booking-timing effects, not a step-change in units. Ex-FX revenue growth was 13% in Q2 2026.
2. **The quantified drivers of the 2025–26 reacceleration are three product changes, not "AI."** Reserve Now, Pay Later (RNPL), relaxed cancellation policies, and the single-service-fee migration contributed ~2 points of nights and ~3 points of GBV growth in Q4 2025 and ~3 points / ~4 points in Q1 2026 (management's own estimates). The AI narrative arrived in Q2 2026 as attribution became diffuse.
3. **Management's story has cycled through six framings** (travel rebound → growth-and-profitability-at-scale → affordability/supply → "inflection point" → beyond stays → AI-native company). Several headline ideas faded without comment: live-anywhere/long-term stays, affordability, supply-growth disclosure, Rooms, Icons, Categories.
4. **Sell-side tone tracks the second derivative of nights growth, not the level.** Analysts were harshest during record 2022–23 quarters when growth was decelerating (scores 3–4/10) and friendliest in 2026 (7–8/10) when nights growth was only 9–10% but accelerating and guidance was being raised.
5. **The stock trades on nights, GBV, take rate and margin, not revenue.** Next-quarter revenue is ~65–90% pre-funded by unearned fees, so revenue guides are tight and beats are small. Examples: −14% after-hours on Q2 2024 (nights moderation warning despite record nights), +14.5% on Q4 2024 (nights beat despite a below-consensus revenue guide), −7% on Q2 2025 (beat, cautious nights guide).
6. **Main forward risks:** lapping RNPL from Q3 2026 (US) and Q1 2027 (global); FX tailwind fading; flat take rate because new-business incentives offset monetization gains; sales & marketing growing 27% vs. revenue 17%; no gross-margin leverage yet; hotels/services still single-digit contributors with undisclosed unit economics; ~70% of revenue from five countries.

## 2. Scope and method

- **Source file:** a Bloomberg transcript export (plain text with a .pdf extension) of 22 calls, Q1 2021 through Q2 2026 (August 6, 2026). Each call was parsed into prepared remarks, analyst questions and management answers. All prepared remarks and all analyst questions were read; management answers were read in full for Q4 2025–Q2 2026 and selectively earlier.
- **Numeric series:** the calls do not contain a consistent KPI table (absolute GBV, ADR and nights are absent from most 2023–24 calls; gross margin is never discussed). The series below was rebuilt from Airbnb shareholder letters filed as 8-K Exhibit 99.1: Q2 2026 (quarterly summary Q1 2024–Q2 2026), Q4 2025 (2023–2025), Q4 2023 (2022–2023 with 2019), Q3 2022 (Q1 2021–Q3 2022), Q2 2024, Q2 2022, and the Q4 2021 letter for Q4 2021 cost of revenue.
- **Gross margin** is not a company-reported metric. It is computed as revenue minus "cost of revenue" (payment processing, hosting, etc.). Q1 2021, Q1 2022, Q1 2025, Q1 2026 and Q3 2021–2025 are derived by subtracting reported half-year, nine-month or full-year figures.
- **Sentiment scores** are judgment-based readings of analyst question text (1 = hostile/bearish, 10 = congratulatory/bullish), cross-checked with a keyword tally of congratulatory vs. probing language and topic mix. No stock-price or estimate-revision data was used.
- **Transcript caveat:** Bloomberg's own disclaimer notes transcripts "may not be 100 percent accurate"; where a garbled transcript number conflicted with a letter, the letter was used.

## 4. How management's story changed, era by era

**Era 1 — "Travel rebound / travel revolution" (Q1 2021–Q1 2022).** The single 2021 priority was to "prepare for the coming travel rebound" via four workstreams: educating the world about hosting, recruiting hosts, simplifying the guest journey, and world-class service (Q1 2021 call). The thesis: COVID permanently changed travel — "people can now travel anytime… everywhere… staying longer"; 24% of Q1 2021 nights were 28+ days. By Q3 2021: "a revolution in how we live and work… Airbnb makes it possible to work from any home." For 2022 the priorities were "Live anywhere on Airbnb, unlock the next generation of hosts, and Airbnb becoming the ultimate host" (Q4 2021 call).

**Era 2 — "Growth and profitability at scale" (Q2–Q4 2022).** The narrative pivoted to "guests are returning to cities and crossing borders" plus "demand drives supply." The $2B buyback was announced "only a year and a half after our IPO" (Q2 2022 call). Q4 2022 introduced the three-priority framework: make hosting mainstream, perfect the core service, expand beyond the core.

**Era 3 — Affordability, supply, and "perfecting the core" (2023).** With ADR ~40% above 2019 and nights decelerating to 11% (Q2 2023), the story became affordability: total price display, host pricing tools, Similar Listings, Airbnb Rooms at "$67 a night," reduced fees on 3+ month stays. Supply growth accelerated to 18–19% while demand grew 11–14%. International expansion (Germany, Brazil, Korea) entered the script.

**Era 4 — "Inflection point / next chapter" (Q4 2023–Q4 2024).** Chesky: "Airbnb is at an inflection point… now is the time for us to expand beyond our business and reinvent Airbnb," citing GamePlanner.AI and the CFO transition to Ellie Mertz. 2024 focused on quality (listing removals, Guest Favorites), the app, Icons, and expansion markets. Q2 2024 was the low point: "shorter booking lead times globally and some signs of slowing demand from U.S. guests." Q4 2024 reported reacceleration to 12% nights growth and a $200–250M plan to launch new businesses with a ≥34.5% margin floor.

**Era 5 — "Beyond stays" and the innovation-model reboot (2025).** May 13, 2025 launched Services, re-imagined Experiences and a rebuilt app; the KPI was renamed "nights and seats booked." Q1 2025 was defensive (US softness, tariff uncertainty); from Q3 2025 the tone shifted to reacceleration via RNPL, hotel pilots and AI support. Q4 2025 reframed around "Project Y/Hawaii" — small teams shipping hundreds of conversion improvements — and quantified the three-feature lift.

**Era 6 — "AI-native company" (2026).** Q1 2026: 60% of code AI-written, 40% of support self-resolved, RNPL ~20% of GBV, guidance raised. Q2 2026: "AI is the best thing to ever happen to Airbnb," first-time bookers +11%, hotels ~3x homes, guidance raised again to "at least mid-teens" revenue growth and ≥35.5% margin. Chesky rejected single-driver explanations: "There is no silver bullet."

**Ideas that faded without comment:** live-anywhere/long-term stays (24% of nights → 18–19%, now mix-shifting toward short entire-home stays); affordability (ADR at record highs); supply-growth disclosure (dropped in 2024); Rooms, Icons, Categories.

**Ideas that persisted:** twice-yearly releases; "perfect the core before expanding"; expansion markets ~2x core; capital return.

## 6. Key ideas: past, present, forward

**The key idea going forward (management's framing).** Three horizons (Q2 2026 call): near term — homes, via an AI host-pricing model ("many multiples bigger than RNPL"), Project Y conversion work, payments and supply; next — international expansion and hotels ("billions of dollars incremental revenue"); multi-year — Services and Experiences. AI is positioned as internal accelerant (60% AI-written code, ~80% more features shipped) and as the defense against disintermediation (200M verified IDs, 500M reviews, messaging, payments, support, insurance). The unifying concept is "the Airbnb trip": one app where each part strengthens the others, with a one-stop-travel-shop ambition within 12–18 months and eventually "from traveling to living."

**The next two quarters.** Q3 2026 revenue guide $4.69–4.77B (+15.5% at midpoint, ~12–13% ex-FX); nights guided to low-double-digit from 10%, helped by World Cup stays; margin down vs. 50% in Q3 2025 on investment timing. FY2026 ≥ mid-teens revenue growth implies Q4 must hold close to Q3's rate while lapping RNPL's US launch (Q3 2025) and its up-funnel merchandising (Q4 2025).

**Watch-list:** RNPL comps (US Q3 2026; global Q1 2027); FX tailwind fading; take rate flat despite insurance +60% and single fee because of new-business incentives; single-fee migration completion; AI search scaling from a small opt-in test; unearned fees/FCF timing.

## 7. Pushback on the narrative

1. **The acceleration is smaller in units than in dollars.** Nights growth 7→9→10→9→10% over five quarters; reported revenue +17–18% carries ~3 pts FX and timing; ex-FX revenue +13% in Q2 2026; North America and EMEA nights at high-single digits.
2. **Attribution is drifting.** Q4 2025/Q1 2026 quantified specific drivers; Q2 2026 became "no single product… AI-native company" just as the quantified drivers approach their anniversaries.
3. **RNPL is partly a pull-forward with real costs**: longer lead times, +~1 pt cancellations, deferred cash, lower unearned fees.
4. **ADR contradicts years of affordability messaging**; North America ADR +7%; an analyst flagged ADR growing faster than hotels.
5. **No gross-margin or opex leverage yet**: derived gross margin flat YoY; S&M +27% vs. revenue +17% in Q2 2026; margin expansion came from AI-support savings, not new businesses.
6. **Inconsistent messaging on AI cost**: "will not affect the P&L" (Q4 2025) vs. guidance "assumes a material increase in AI spend" (Q2 2026).
7. **New businesses are bought with incentives and remain small**: price match, up to 15% hotel credit; take rate flat "accounting for higher customer incentives related to new businesses."
8. **Concentration and disclosure**: ~70% of revenue from five countries; supply-growth disclosure withdrawn; "nights and seats" bundles Experiences seats into core volume; hotel-guest return rate quoted as 55% then ~35%.
9. **Cash returns decelerating relative to the equity story**: TTM FCF margin 44% → 36–37%; NOLs consumed; $2.5B debt raised; SBC ~13% of revenue.
10. **Undiscussed**: European regulatory outcomes; durability of first-time-booker growth once World Cup cohorts lap; whether hotels' higher conversion cannibalizes home economics (asserted, not shown).

## 8. Follow-up Q&A

### 8.1 How does the hotels business work?

Airbnb is not buying hotels and is not doing chain-level deals. It is a distribution channel: independent and boutique hotels list rooms on Airbnb, Airbnb sends them guests from existing traffic, and takes a commission. Chesky: "we went to hotels in New York City… we said we have a huge amount of traffic… we gave you a best-in-class commission… And they said, yeah" (Q3 2025). Recruiting hotels is "much easier than recruiting homes because a hotel they have a phone number."

- *Why boutique/independent:* they pay higher commissions to big OTAs than chains, lack loyalty programs, feel pressure to franchise, and want another channel (Q1 2026, Q4 2025). Chains are "not saying where we're eventually going."
- *Product:* custom hotel listing pages, filters, room-type selection, commingled results with carousel tests; long-term principle is AI personalization by trip type.
- *Connectivity:* via APIs/connectivity partners; friction reported with multiple rate types (Q4 2025 Q&A); back end works, front-end merchandising remains (Q1 2026).
- *Guest incentives:* price match guarantee and up to 15% Airbnb credit on featured hotels, funded by Airbnb.
- *Economics:* hotel commission undisclosed; described as "best-in-class," "low," "extremely favorable." Standard host fee is now a single 15.5% service fee.
- *Scale:* pilots in NYC, LA, Madrid (Q3 2025) → "thousands" of hotels across 20+ destinations (Q2 2026); single-digit % of nights; growing ~3x homes.
- *Rationale:* fill regulatory supply gaps; serve one-night/last-minute/business trips; onboard new guests who migrate to homes (55% per Q1 2026 call; ~35% per Q2 2026 letter). Chesky admits Airbnb's conversion "is significantly lower than, say, Booking.com," so hotels can be "a multi-billion dollar revenue business" purely by converting existing traffic.
- *Pushback:* scope grew from "backfill… not really an incremental investment" (Q3 2025) to "opening the aperture" with incentives and hiring (Q1 2026); cannibalization asserted via an Amazon analogy, not data; the return-to-home statistic changed between quarters.

### 8.2 What is Reserve Now, Pay Later and why does it matter?

**Mechanics.** Historically guests paid in full at booking (or two instalments via "Pay Part Now, Part Later"). With RNPL an eligible guest pays $0 at booking and the payment method is charged on a scheduled date shown at checkout; at US launch, full payment was due eight days before the end of the listing's free-cancellation period. It is not a loan (no credit check, no interest). Eligibility is set by Airbnb: listings with flexible or moderate cancellation policies only; US domestic at launch. Cancel before the payment date and the scheduled payment is simply cancelled; host cancellation terms and payouts are unchanged; add-ons such as travel insurance are charged at booking.

**Rollout.** US launch start of Q3 2025 → merchandised up-funnel in Q4 2025 → most of the rest of the world in Q1 2026 → desktop and US cross-border → eligible booking types expanded in July 2026. Adoption ~70% of eligible bookings; >20% of total GBV in Q1–Q2 2026.

**Why it lifts the numbers (management's four channels):** (1) conversion — removes the upfront card charge that stops hesitant and first-time bookers; net of cancellations, tested cohort by cohort; (2) longer lead times — "locking in earlier calendar share" before guests shop Booking/Vrbo; (3) higher ADR/mix — guests choose "a slightly nicer listing," mix shifts to 4+ bedroom homes; (4) reopened policy lever — requires flexible/moderate policies, coinciding with the retirement of Strict and migration to Firm. The bundle of RNPL + cancellation changes + single fee delivered ~2 pts nights / ~3 pts GBV (Q4 2025) and ~3 pts / ~4 pts (Q1 2026); RNPL's own share is not isolated, though Mertz attributed the Q3 2025 US acceleration "in part" to it and Chesky called it "a uniquely large bullet."

**Costs:** cancellations ~16% → ~17% aggregate (higher within the RNPL cohort); deferred cash (unearned fees flat YoY; lower in Q1/Q2, higher in Q3); take-rate timing optics; host risk of late cancellation when a card fails.

**On the "largest near-term upside driver" thesis (Deutsche Bank):** plausible because adoption is ~70% wherever offered, so each eligibility expansion mechanically adds bookings, and management sees payments flexibility as a growth law ("the more flexibility you give people on the way to pay, the faster the growth"). Against overweighting it: it is a level shift, not a growth-rate change, once lapped (US Q3 2026; global Q1 2027; Mertz already flagged "tougher comps in the back half"); management stopped isolating it after Q4 2025; Chesky now calls AI pricing "many multiples bigger"; the eligible universe is capped by flexible/moderate-policy share and undisclosed risk rules.

### 8.3 Why are Q1 and Q4 weak, and why is Q1 ADR the highest?

Revenue is recognized upon check-in; fees collected earlier sit as unearned fees. So revenue follows when people *travel*; GBV, nights and ADR are recorded when they *book*. Management: highest GBV in Q1/Q2 with comparatively lower check-ins; highest revenue in Q3, the strongest quarter for check-ins.

Average 2023–2025: revenue share by quarter 19/25/34/22%; GBV share 28/26/25/22%; implied take rate ~9/13/18/14%; ADR (2025) $171/$174/$171/$168. Q1 is the biggest quarter for forward indicators and the smallest for revenue; Q4 is genuinely soft on both.

Winter is not a boost because Airbnb is a Northern-Hemisphere family-leisure business (~70% of revenue from five countries; US ~40%; best use case is groups in 4+ bedroom homes); summer vacations dominate, ski/holiday travel is a niche, and December involves visiting family. Q1 has two fewer days; Easter shifts 1–2 points between Q1 and Q2.

Q1 ADR is highest because ADR = GBV ÷ nights booked, both dated at booking: Q1 is when guests lock in peak-season summer stays in large entire homes with long lead times; Q3/Q4 bookings skew to shoulder-season dates, shorter urban trips, last-minute. GBV is also net of cancellations occurring in the quarter, so cancellations of Q1's summer bookings depress Q2/Q3 GBV and ADR. RNPL lengthens lead times and shifts mix to larger homes, pulling more high-priced summer bookings into Q1 (Q1 2026 GBV +19%, ADR $187). Only YoY, ex-FX, same-quarter ADR comparisons are meaningful.

### 8.4 Is GBV backlog? What does the stock trade on?

**GBV is a flow, not a stock.** It is the value of bookings made in the period, gross of host earnings/cleaning fees/taxes, and net of cancellations of any prior booking that occur in the period; Airbnb's claim on it is the ~13% take rate. The closest reported backlog is **unearned fees**, which have covered ~65–70% of the next quarter's revenue (and ~88% for Q1→Q2, 2023–2025). RNPL now distorts this: Q1 2026 unearned fees covered only 76% of Q2 revenue vs. 87–89% in prior years because RNPL bookings don't generate unearned fees until charged — the reported backlog understates booked business.

**Lag.** Airbnb does not disclose average lead time. A non-negative lag fit of post-COVID quarterly revenue on lagged GBV puts about two-thirds of the weight on the prior quarter's GBV and one-third on the quarter before, at a blended ~14% conversion: a quarter's revenue is mostly determined by bookings made one to two quarters earlier.

**What the stock trades on.** Nights, GBV, take rate and margin, not revenue. Q2 2024: record nights and in-line revenue guide, stock −14% after hours on a nights-moderation and lead-time warning. Q4 2024: +14.5% on nights of 111M vs. 108.5M estimated, despite a Q1 revenue guide ($2.23–2.27B) below the $2.29B consensus. Q2 2025: revenue/EPS beat, stock −7% on cautious nights and margin guidance. Because next-quarter revenue is ~70–90% pre-funded, revenue guides are tight and carry little surprise.

**Is forecasting next year's nights/GBV the differentiation?** Only partly: nights growth has been 7–10% for ten quarters, so the level was not the differentiator. The money was in (1) second-derivative calls around product catalysts (RNPL, cancellation policy, single fee, hotels, events), (2) the revenue-per-night stack — ADR mix, FX, take rate (fees and insurance vs. incentives), and (3) margin/reinvestment. GBV gives roughly two quarters of visibility; forecasting further out means forecasting booking behavior (macro, lead times, RNPL eligibility). The most under-analysed forward metric is unearned fees adjusted for RNPL.
