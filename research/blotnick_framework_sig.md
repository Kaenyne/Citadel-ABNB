# Blotnick's 13 "Levels of the Game" Applied to Signet (SIG) — Framework Audit for the Long Pitch

*Prepared 2026-08-29. Source text: Gregory Blotnick post, Aug 27, 2026 (`research/blotnick_post_fulltext.md`, x.com/gregoryblotnick/status/2093071610614084036). SIG ~$82.70; Q2 FY27 print Sept 9, 2026 7:00am ET; FY2027 = calendar 2026. This is a synthesis of the existing research corpus (business_model_kpis, guidance_history, channel_checks_sources, credit_card_data, altdata_tenoris, altdata_lgd_wholesale, sept9_expectations, deferred_revenue_esp, earnings_call_analyst_analysis, brand_actions, vic_short_evaluation) plus targeted transcript greps — no new external research.*

**Why this matters:** Blotnick's list is a catalog of the ways consumer-retail comps, guides, and alt-data mislead sophisticated investors. Every one of his 13 traps either (a) exists at Signet and must be conceded before a judge finds it, (b) exists at Signet but we have already diligenced it, converting the trap into an edge, or (c) doesn't apply. The audit below runs each point through five gates: what it means, whether the dynamic exists at SIG, what data addresses it, what's on the record about it, and the verdict for a long.

## 1. "Alt data QTD SSS% is often more accurate than mgmt SSS% est — so 3D chess becomes 'will mgmt lie,' and/or imply in their SSS% guide some sequential accel due to a new product intro"

**TRANSLATION.** Intra-quarter alt data can out-forecast management's own comp guide. The higher-order game is credibility analysis: is the guide honest, and does it quietly embed a sequential acceleration (e.g., from a product launch) that the trend doesn't support?

**SIG APPLICATION — the dynamic exists, but INVERTED.** At Signet the guide has been a *floor*, not a stretch: adjusted operating income has printed **at or above the high end of the company guide five consecutive quarters** (Q1 FY26 +17% above high end; Q3 FY26 ~2x the high end; Q1 FY27 above it — guidance_history §4a). Management's QTD comp commentary at each print (a full table exists in guidance_history §5a) has been consistently honest and subsequently validated — e.g., Q1 FY27 call: "good Mother's Day… momentum hold… mid-single-digit two-year stacks." The Q2 FY27 comp guide (+0.5% to +2.5%) embeds **no heroic sequential acceleration**: it includes a disclosed ~70bps definitional benefit (see #5) against a Q1 that printed +1.8% *with* a ~1pt James Allen drag — the implied like-for-like two-year stack is flat-to-conservative vs. the run rate. The one "new product intro" style claim to stress is the Kay/Zales/Jared website relaunches "early Q3" — but those are in the **H2** guide, not Q2 (see #9).

Meanwhile the alt data itself is weak as a SIG nowcaster: Tenoris (the only public monthly jewelry POS) level-correlates +0.71 with SIG comps but agrees on quarter-over-quarter *direction* only 4 of 8 times, and ran +7.5% the quarter SIG printed −0.7% (Q4 FY26) (altdata_tenoris §4). So Blotnick's premise — alt data beats the guide — does not hold for this name; the guide (adjusted upward for the sandbagging pattern) beats the alt data.

**DATA NEEDED.** Tenoris May–Jul posts (all out: +1.8%/+13%/+11%), Placer mall index (positive all 3 months, July dwell-time inflection), promo scrape, Census NAICS 448. All say the quarter was fine — but frame as category tide, not SIG comp prediction (sept9 §3.8).

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** UBS's Serna has called direction right four straight quarters and **understated the beat every time** (called 13c/5c/10c, got ~40c/~39c/18c — sept9 §3.1); his checks are expert calls + public data, not a card panel (sept9 §3.0). Hutchinson (BofA), Mar 19, 2025: "it just seems like a pretty sharp deceleration for the rest of the year… you have an opportunity to do even better" — guidance challenged from the *upside*. Symancyk, Q1 FY27: "we knew where we were going to be in Q1 and have shown up consistent with that guide."

**VERDICT: SAFER.** The "will mgmt lie" test resolves in our favor with a 5-quarter receipts table — but only for the current regime (FY25 under prior management missed even the cut guide; never blend it in). **Pre-Sept-9 check:** compare the Q2 guide's implied like-for-like 2-yr stack (strip the 70bps) against QTD language from June 2; anything ≤ the Q1 stack means no embedded acceleration.

## 2. "Knowing which retailers have a high % of cash transactions (thus tanking RSQ on cc/alt data)"

**TRANSLATION.** Card panels only see card rails. Where a big slice of revenue is cash — or anything else off-panel — the panel-to-reported-revenue regression fit (R²) collapses and panel reads mislead.

**SIG APPLICATION — the dynamic exists in FINANCING form, and it's big.** Signet's blind spot isn't cash, it's third-party financing: **42.0% of FY26 eligible NA sales ($2,306.6M) were funded through Comenity PLCC / Concora second-look / Progressive lease-to-own / Affirm BNPL** (FY26 10-K; business_model_kpis §1e). Bloomberg Second Measure states in its own Signet piece: *"transaction data does not include transactions made through financing programs at jewelry companies or through BNPL"* (credit_card_data header). Layer on high-ticket, low-frequency purchases (small n per panel member) and the panel error is banner-skewed: least bad for Banter/Zales (low ticket, more debit), worst for Jared/Diamonds Direct (high ticket, heavy financing). Corroborating evidence that panels run cool on SIG: Consumer Edge's Mar 2024 note tracked −7.3% vs. reported −6.2%. And notably, **no analyst note in Jul–Aug 2026 cites card-panel data on Signet at all** (sept9 §3.7) — the pros have effectively conceded the point.

**DATA NEEDED.** If we show any card-panel exhibit (Earnest Dash free tier, Facteus), it must carry the financing-exclusion caveat verbatim. Better: use the credit-participation rate itself (42.0% FY26 vs 42.6% FY25) as a *consumer-health* KPI and cite management's "applications, approvals, amount financed very consistent" (Q1 FY27, Hilson).

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Second Measure's own disclaimer (above) is the citable authority. Boruchow (Wells Fargo), Aug 31, 2023: "is there a profit share embedded in your credit arrangements… potential headwinds on your top line in a tightening credit environment" — the sell side probes credit as a demand channel, not via panels.

**VERDICT: SAFER — say it before a judge does.** One line in the deck: *"~42% of Signet's North America sales run through financing rails that card panels cannot see — which is why we don't quote panel data on SIG, and why anyone who does is measuring less than 60% of the business."* **Check:** confirm participation rate stability in the Q2 10-Q.

## 3. "Mgmt will buy the comp → SSS upside + GM downside, forecasted with confidence — but you don't know how the stock reacts even if your numbers are 100% right"

**TRANSLATION.** Management can always trade margin for comps (promos), so a comp beat with a margin miss is predictable — and even a perfect forecast doesn't tell you the stock's reaction.

**SIG APPLICATION — both halves are documented at SIG.**
- *Buying the comp:* Q4 FY26 is the exhibit. Nov–mid-Dec ran ~−3%; management pivoted to "broader promotions ahead of high-volume December days," recovered to −0.7% comps (near the top of a +0.5%/−5% band) — and Q4 adjusted operating margin fell y/y **15.1% → 14.0%** partly on the promo pivot (business_model_kpis §2, §4; guidance_history §3b). They will buy the comp when holiday softens; they did it 9 months ago.
- *Reaction unknowable:* Signet is the case study. Three consecutive beat-and-raises produced −3.8% (Q3 FY26, cautious holiday framing), ~flat-then-fade (Q4 FY26), and +3.7% fully round-tripped in 10 weeks (Q1 FY27) (sept9 §6.5). Earnings-day moves vs. alt-data strength: correlation +0.26 — noise (altdata_tenoris §4). The stock trades the *H2 margin question*, not the print.

**DATA NEEDED.** The promo-intensity scrape (Wayback backfill of kay.com/zales.com/jared.com hero banners, 3-yr series) is the real-time detector of comp-buying: promo depth up y/y into a strong comp print = bought comp = discount the quality (channel_checks Tier 1 #1). Currently the scrape narrative and management agree: Jared discounting cut 25% y/y (Q3 FY26), "fewer days on sale, shallower discounts" not lapped until H2 FY27.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Lejuez (Citi), Dec 2, 2025: "the 10 days leading up to Christmas… you kind of had a miss there last year." Symancyk's candid answer: "it was not a traffic opportunity… it was a conversion opportunity." Symancyk, Q1 FY27: "there's not more promo in the business. We're just seeing better health across the base." Evercore ISI's holiday FY25 note cited "more intense promotional activity than planned" (channel_checks §2) — the sell side watches exactly this.

**VERDICT: RISKIER — concede the reaction-function problem explicitly.** Our own scenario map says the modal outcome (B: in-line beat, low-end raise) is the *lowest-payoff* one (sept9 §6.4). **Operationalize:** frame Sept 9 in the pitch as a checkpoint, not the thesis; the tripwire is H2 merch-margin language (#9), and the holiday-window promo scrape (weekly from October) is our early warning that comps are being bought at margin's expense.

## 4. "Mgmt juicing qtr-end SSS% through a 1Q promo where expenses won't hit til 2Q ('exit rate accel'); alt data rips into q-end even though promos/cannibalization are 1–2 quarters away"

**TRANSLATION.** End-of-quarter promotions inflate exit rates and QTD commentary; the margin/return bill arrives a quarter later. Alt data reads the sugar high as momentum.

**SIG APPLICATION — the pattern exists but currently runs the OTHER direction.** Signet's Q1 FY27 mid-quarter slowdown was the *opposite* of exit-rate juicing: they turned promo OFF between Valentine's and Mother's Day ("we turn off promotion, rely a little more on clearance… spend a little bit less in marketing"), the comp sagged while staying positive, then recovered when "we put our signs back up" (Q1 FY27, Symancyk — guidance_history §5a note). That's a disclosed calendar artifact, not manufactured acceleration. The place to apply Blotnick's skepticism is the **Q4 FY26 December promo pivot**: the exit-rate improvement into January that management cited ("further improvement in January… positive Valentine's Day… continued quarter-to-date") was partly promo-led, and its margin cost did land in-quarter (Q4 margin down y/y) — but watch whether Q1/Q2 FY27 gross margin quietly carried residual markdown drag (Q1 GM −100bps was attributed to gold; the attribution is the thing to interrogate). Also note the alt-data version of the trap: Tenoris/Placer both **inflected upward in July** — the back half of SIG's Q2 — which our own preview leans on (sept9 §3.8). If that July strength were promo-bought industry-wide, our read-through would be exactly Blotnick's sucker trade. Mitigant: Tenoris' July strength was **AUR-led (+16% spend/item), not discount-led**, and management says promo cadence is flat y/y.

**DATA NEEDED.** Weekly promo scrape vs. same weeks LY (the Evidence-Lab clone) — the only tool that distinguishes organic exit-rate strength from promo-bought strength in real time. Secondary: clearance-item counts on the banners' sale pages.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Management's own repeated framing: "not more promo in the business… consistent with the run rate" (Symancyk, Q1 FY27); "reduced off-holiday discounting" listed as a tariff-mitigation lever (Q4 FY26). No analyst has caught them juicing an exit rate in the current regime; the FY26 Q4 pivot was disclosed on the call, not discovered.

**VERDICT: SAFER, with one caution.** We can *check* this one (promo scrape), and current evidence supports promo discipline. The caution feeds #3: the Q4 FY26 pivot proves the playbook exists. **Pre-Sept-9 check:** run the scrape for Aug vs. Aug-LY; on the call, listen for whether the Q2 exit rate / "momentum" language is paired with any promo-cadence change into Q3.

## 5. "Mgmt's ability to play games with the comp base (impossible to forecast as minority shareholder)… ditto remodels, relos, expansions (remodels inflate comps entering comp base), or excluding one-off weather/disruption events"

**TRANSLATION.** The comp base is management's sandbox: definitional changes, remodel re-entries, closures of weak doors, and one-off exclusions can all flatter reported SSS in ways outsiders can't model.

**SIG APPLICATION — this is the single most on-point Blotnick trap at SIG. Every sub-mechanism is live in FY27:**
1. **Two definition changes in one year.** (a) Q1 FY27: SSS moved to cash-basis ESP (plans sold, not revenue recognized) — worth **+20–40bps** while plan sales grow; FY26 comps restated upward (Q1 FY26 2.5%→2.7%). (b) Q2 FY27 for four quarters: **Blue Nile and James Allen excluded from the comp base** — management's own quantification: **~70bps benefit in Q2, ~60 Q3, ~50 Q4**, baked into guidance (business_model_kpis §3).
2. **Remodels entering the base:** **200+ renovations in FY27 (~10% of the fleet, up ~30% y/y)**, with renovated stores "generating incremental low-single-digit comps" — a rolling comp tailwind as they re-enter (business_model_kpis §6; guidance_history §1d).
3. **Closures pruning the base:** ~100 closures/yr (skewed to low-volume Banter kiosks), up to 20 repositions of "healthy doors in declining venues" — survivorship steadily upgrades the base.
4. **One-off exclusions:** Q4 FY26 call: "Excluding James Allen and the net impact of weather, comps grew 1%" (FY2026_Q4.txt line 209) — a textbook weather carve-out, though offered as color, not a restated headline.

Net: **reported FY27 comps are ~60–110bps flattered vs. the FY26 basis.** The mitigating difference from Blotnick's "impossible to forecast" version: Signet disclosed and quantified everything to the basis point, with a full restated table — unusually clean execution of a dirty-looking play.

**DATA NEEDED.** None external — this is arithmetic on disclosures. Build a "like-for-like comp" line in our model: reported comp minus (BN/JA benefit per quarter) minus (ESP delta), and quote *that* everywhere.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Serna (UBS) asked the question directly on the Q1 FY27 call — whether pulling JA/BN out flatters comps. Symancyk: **"no, we're not hiding the ball there. I think we see strength on the balance of the business"** (brand_actions §10). Our own brand-actions file lists "comp optics" as bear ammunition #3; business_model_kpis §3 flags "the optics of changing the comp definition in the same year the guide depends on comps are fair game for judges."

**VERDICT: RISKIER — the trap is real and live; neutralize it by owning it.** A judge who lands this unanswered wins the exchange. **Operationalize:** a deck slide that shows reported vs. like-for-like comps side by side, states the +50–70bps and +20–40bps effects, cites the "not hiding the ball" exchange, and notes the underlying two-year stack is *better* than optics because Q1 absorbed a 1-pt JA drag. Being harder on the comp math than the bear is the play.

## 6. "Mgmt's ability to play games with omni + ecom in triangulating consolidated SSS% — stores soft, ecom strong, report 'blended comp' with minimal disclosure otherwise"

**TRANSLATION.** A single blended comp lets management hide channel deterioration (weak stores masked by strong e-com, or vice versa).

**SIG APPLICATION — the structure exists; the current direction is the benign one.** Signet reports **one blended SSS** (stores + e-commerce, open ≥12 months, constant FX) and stopped publishing **banner-level comps after FY24** — disclosure has thinned exactly as Blotnick describes. But right now the blend runs the *conservative* way: e-commerce penetration has **fallen** (22.9% FY24 → 21.8% FY26 → 20.7% Q1 FY27), driven by the self-inflicted James Allen wind-down, so the blended comp has been dragged by digital while stores outperform — Q1 FY27 comps were positive "in most brands" with only DD (nearly positive) and JA negative. The trap flips to live risk in H2: the Kay/Zales/Jared **website relaunches (complete "early Q3 FY27")** are the holiday conversion bet; if holiday comps come in strong, ask how much is a one-time e-com re-platform bounce entering the blend.

**DATA NEEDED.** Channel disaggregation from each 10-Q (e-com $ and %; Q1 FY27: $322.1M, 20.7%); SimilarWeb monthly screenshots of kay.com/zales.com/bluenile.com (free tier) as the between-print e-com proxy — noting it's a web proxy, not transactions.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** No analyst has pressed the blended-comp construction in the current era (the FY27 Q1 Q&A focused on the comp-base exclusion instead, which management answered — see #5). The disclosure regression (banner comps pulled after FY24) has gone unchallenged on calls; qualitative banner color continues (e.g., "Kay+Zales+Jared combined +5%" Q2 FY26).

**VERDICT: NEUTRAL.** The disclosure thinness is a fair criticism to concede; the current channel mix makes the blended number conservative, not flattering. **Check:** on Sept 9, note e-com penetration y/y — continued decline plus positive comps = stores carrying the number (thesis-friendly); a sudden e-com pop post-relaunch in Q3/holiday should be flagged in any update round as re-platform bounce, not run-rate.

## 7. "Ditto BOPIS/curbside/ship-from-store… ditto what store an ecom sale gets assigned to… ditto return rates (book sales 1Q juiced by promos, returns hit 2Q)"

**TRANSLATION.** Omni-fulfillment mechanics — where an online sale is credited, and how returns reverse promo-inflated sales a quarter later — are invisible levers inside the comp.

**SIG APPLICATION — present but unobservable, and probably small.** Signet defines e-commerce as online-*originated* sales including BOPIS/ship-to-store (business_model_kpis KPI table), so BOPIS sits in the e-com line rather than store comps — one specific assignment rule is at least disclosed. Ship-from-store was scaled to ~1,850 stores in the COVID era (FY2022_Q3 transcript). Beyond that: **Signet discloses no return-rate data anywhere**, and a grep of FY2024–FY2027 transcripts finds **zero analyst questions on merchandise returns** — the topic is simply dark. Two structural mitigants: (1) jewelry returns skew lower than apparel (sized/engraved/custom items; bridal is considered-purchase), and (2) the revenue line most exposed to timing games — ESP — is deferred, not bookable early (see #12). The Q1-promo→Q2-returns mechanism would matter most around holiday (Q4 comps → Q1 returns); nothing in the Q1 FY27 print suggested a returns drag, but we can't prove a negative.

**DATA NEEDED.** None exists publicly. The honest posture is to note the limits: return reserves appear only in aggregate balance-sheet refund liabilities. Not worth deck space.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Nothing — this is the dog that doesn't bark. The transcript corpus (1,026 question blocks) contains no return-rate or sales-assignment probing in the modern era.

**VERDICT: NEUTRAL (unobservable in both directions).** Concede if asked in Q&A: "returns aren't disclosed; jewelry's category mechanics make it a smaller lever than apparel, and the biggest deferred line (ESP) is recognized conservatively by construction." No pre-Sept-9 action available.

**VERDICT: SAFER (N/A).** One fewer obfuscation channel than the typical consumer name. Worth one spoken line if a judge runs the Blotnick checklist: "no franchise mix, no license conversions — the store base is fully consolidated."

## 9. "Getting the quarter right, the guide right (say 1Q/2Q), and then the 2H guide is trash for reasons you could never have alt-data'd your way into"

**TRANSLATION.** You can nail the near quarter and still lose because the back-half guide breaks on something no panel tracks (input costs, hedges, mix).

**SIG APPLICATION — this is the central risk of our entire trade, and we should say so.** The FY27 P&L shape is explicitly back-loaded on faith: merchandise margin guided "**down in the first half… flat to slightly up in the back half**" (Hilson), resting on (a) lapping last fall's pricing/promo architecture work, (b) **gold hedges that hit the P&L "backloaded over Q3 and Q4"** (Citi conference, Mar 2026), (c) gold behaving (+30% y/y, +121% since Jan-2024 — it *swamps* LGD deflation in the P&L, altdata_lgd_wholesale §0), and (d) a mid-teens effective tariff rate holding. No alt-data source tracks any of these. This is also precisely why all three post-earnings pops faded: "in all three cases the fade was driven by the same worry: gold + tariffs compressing merchandise margin in the back half" (sept9 §6.5). And the cautionary precedent is FY25: guidance reaffirmed in June and September, then **cut in December, cut again in January, and missed even the cut** — an H2 blowup no Q2 data flagged (guidance_history §3c; prior regime, but the seasonal concentration that enabled it — Q4 ≈ half of annual adjusted OI — is structural).

**DATA NEEDED.** Not alt data — three trackables: (1) spot gold vs. the hedge narrative (weekly); (2) the promo scrape through Oct–Dec (is "fewer days on sale" holding as they lap it?); (3) tariff headlines on India/Italy. Plus the wildcard that cuts the other way: **IEEPA tariff refunds** — Movado booked $0.11/sh in the identical calendar quarter; Signet's guide includes "only a minimal amount" (sept9 §5.2) — the H2 guide may be trash in the *good* direction.

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** Sanderson (Northcoast) drew out the H1/H2 margin split on the Q4 FY26 call; Hutchinson (Dec 2, 2025): "how do you balance the need to offset some of these cost pressures with the consumer struggles?"; management's own Q3 FY26 caution ("softer traffic in the past five weeks") is the template for how they'll hedge H2 language if worried. Our sept9 checklist item #3: "H2 merchandise margin language — *this is the trade*."

**VERDICT: RISKIER — the trap we must concede and convert into the catalyst framing.** The pitch should state plainly: the quarter is probably fine; the stock trades on whether management **re-confirms the H2 merch-margin inflection** on Sept 9. If they reconfirm, the fade pattern breaks (scenario C→D); if they hedge, expect the beat to fade by October and treat it as a better entry, not a thesis break. **Check:** the exact words used about H2 merch margin on the call, and any change to the "mid-teens tariff" assumption.

## 10. "Timing of aging inventory / channel stuffing (will inventory be marked down in 3 months, 6 months — unclear)"

**TRANSLATION.** Aged inventory is a deferred P&L hit of unknowable timing; channel/inventory build masquerades as health until the markdown lands.

**SIG APPLICATION — three distinct threads.**
1. *The markdown that already landed:* the **$32.7M James Allen/Rocksbox inventory write-down** (Q1 FY27) is a live example of Blotnick's timing problem resolving — inventory whose impairment was arguably embedded for years got marked only when the brands were killed, and it was routed through *adjusted* exclusions (restructuring), so it never touched the headline EPS bulls quote (brand_actions §10; business_model_kpis §4).
2. *The slow-turn lag that's still in front of us:* Signet turns inventory slowly (~$2.0B, roughly flat; CFO: 0.1x turn = ~$100M FCF), which means **record gold costs flow into COGS with a multi-quarter delay** — the VIC short's point 5, which we scored CONFIRMED ("slow inventory turns delay the hit, meaning the pain is still arriving," vic_short §5). This is the inventory-timing mechanism most likely to bite, and it feeds directly into #9. Partial offset unique to this cycle: Signet is **melting clearance product into record gold prices** — aging inventory has a salvage bid for once.
3. *The industry-level stuffing risk:* Tenoris' Apr 2026 post flags **LGD inventory:sales up ~50% vs 2020 — "a growing structural imbalance"** with an explicit bullwhip warning, echoed by JCK ("retail margins may be next") and Zimnisky ("current levels are too high") (altdata_tenoris §5). If independents mark down LGD en masse, the retail umbrella our thesis monetizes compresses faster.

**DATA NEEDED.** Balance-sheet inventory vs. sales each print (watch Sept 9: inventory materially up y/y into holiday = build risk); Tenoris inventory posts (they track 3M inventory units monthly); our clearance-page item counts on the banners; BRLT gross margin as the LGD-native canary (vic_short tripwire #7).

**WHAT OTHERS SAY.** FY2019's Q&A had 20 inventory/promo question blocks during the last clearance purge (earnings_call §2) — the sell side knows this company's markdown history. Management currently frames inventory as a source of FCF, not risk ("0.1 turn is worth $100 million… in free cash flow," Hilson, Q4 FY26). The bear's version is Tenoris' own bullwhip post — the counter-exhibit inside our best data source.

**VERDICT: NEUTRAL-to-RISKIER.** The company-level markdown (JA) is behind us and disclosed; the gold-in-inventory lag and the industry LGD bullwhip are live and feed the H2 risk (#9). **Check:** inventory $ y/y on Sept 9 and any change in the "flat inventory despite gold" framing; monthly Tenoris inventory commentary through holiday.

## 12. "Accounting for loyalty/gift-card/card credit programs to boost EBIT/EPS"

**TRANSLATION.** Deferred-revenue programs (loyalty points, gift cards, warranties, credit revenue-share) carry judgment-heavy recognition assumptions that can be tuned to flatter EBIT.

**SIG APPLICATION — the most accounting-rich point on the list, and Signet is a giant of the genre.**
- **The ESP book is ~$1.2B** of customer prepayments recognized "in proportion to expected claims" over ≤13 years — a KPMG **critical audit matter** with disclosed sensitivity (±$6M revenue per 1pt of first-year recognition rate; −$15M per +1yr of period). The forensic issue: **a decade of recognition-assumption drift, every step in the revenue-friendly direction** — Q2 FY2016 ring-sizing change accelerated recognition to ~57% in the first two years (vs 42–45%); FY22 10-K: 17→14 years; FY23: →13 years; FY26: 60–70% in first two years (deferred_revenue_esp §5f). Company states each change was immaterial; the *direction* is the concession to make.
- **The SSS definition change** (#5) moved ESP into comps on a cash basis — the comp and the accounting now diverge by construction.
- **The UK $14.9M** point-of-sale renewal-commission change (see #11).
- **Credit:** fully outsourced (no receivables since FY22), so there is no credit-book accounting to game — the 2016-era recency-accounting scandal machinery is gone. But note the forward lever: Comenity/Concora agreements expire Dec 2028 and Hilson has flagged "potential opportunity to improve the economics of our private credit programs" — a future EBIT tailwind of undisclosed size, i.e., the next place program economics quietly move the P&L.
