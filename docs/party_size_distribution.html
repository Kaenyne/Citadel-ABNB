How many people stay in an Airbnb?

Airbnb never publishes guests per booking. Three independent disclosures pin it anyway: the mean is about 3.0 people per stay, roughly one in six stays is solo, and half of all stays are parties of three or more — the size at which a hotel needs a second room. Hotels are the mirror image: ~1.8 people per stay, close to half of stays solo, fewer than one in five with three or more.

The distribution

People per stay, share of Airbnb bookings. Bars are the central fit; whiskers span a mean of 2.7–3.1 and a solo share of 13–19%.

Three anchors that pin it

1 · The mean: guest arrivals ÷ bookings ≈ 2.9–3.0

Airbnb counts every person who checks in as a “guest arrival” and reports cumulative milestones. Bookings are nights booked ÷ average nights per booking (both from the 10-K). Divide the arrivals added between milestones by the bookings made in the same window and you get people per booking. Every window longer than 2½ years lands between 2.8 and 3.05.

Cross-check: Airbnb’s new Bedroom Nights Booked metric passed 1 billion in the twelve months to Q2 2026 against roughly 560 million nights booked — about 1.8 bedrooms per stay. At 1.6 people per bedroom that is again ≈ 2.9 people.

2 · The solo share: under 20% of bookings, ~24% of nights

  • “More than 80% of bookings on Airbnb are group trips” — Airbnb 2024 Summer Release. So solo stays are at most a fifth of bookings; the fit uses 16%.
  • “Almost a quarter of nights booked worldwide on Airbnb in 2022 were by guests traveling on their own,” and about half of those nights were long-term (28+) stays — Airbnb Newsroom, 2023. Solo travellers stay roughly 1.5× longer, which is exactly why 16% of bookings can be 24% of nights.
  • Solo travellers and millennials are the most likely to book a private room; over 80% of private-room stays were under $100/night (Airbnb 2023 Summer Release).

3 · The shape: rental parties run bigger than hotel parties

Hawaii’s state visitor survey is the only public dataset that reports party size by accommodation type. Rental-house parties are 8% larger than hotel parties, one-third are 3+ (vs 29% for hotels), and they stay 2.2 days longer.

Hotels: the same distribution

Hotels sell rooms, not homes, so there are two honest ways to count. Per room-booking is the transactions lens (a family of five is two bookings). Per travel party is the choice lens — the group that decided between a hotel and an Airbnb. Airbnb’s number is both at once, because one listing holds the whole party.

  • Solo is the hotel’s core, not Airbnb’s. Business travel is 42% of U.S. room nights (439M of 1,044M in 2023, AHLA) and corporate bookings are 80% single-occupancy in the Portuguese ledger, so roughly 45% of U.S. hotel stays are one person versus 16% on Airbnb. That is the 2.1-night average stay in one number.
  • Hotels almost never host 4+ in a room. 3% of bookings have four people and 0.1% five or more; parties of 5+ that do stay in hotels split across rooms (Vegas: 6% of parties are 5+, Hawaii 3+ parties average 4.0 people). On Airbnb 16% of bookings are 5+ under one roof.
  • Even leisure-only hotel markets are couple markets. Hawaii hotel parties: 43% pairs; Las Vegas: 64% pairs; Portuguese leisure bookings: 70% pairs. Airbnb’s pair share (36%) is the lowest of the set because both tails — solo long-stays and groups — are fatter.
  • Hotel guests per room rise with the trip: Portuguese solo stays average 2.5 nights, pairs 3.6, threes 3.7 — the same “bigger party, not longer” pattern as Airbnb once you pass two people.

Capacity is not party size

From the June-2026 Inside Airbnb calendars (8 U.S. cities, 273k booked stays): 13% of stays are private rooms, 12% entire homes sleeping 1–2, 23% sleeping 3–4, 20% sleeping 5–6 and 33% sleeping 7+. Set against the fitted distribution (16% of parties are 5+), guests fill roughly half of listed capacity — a family of four books the six-sleeper. That gap is the product: space is the amenity, not just beds. It also means accommodates in listing data is an upper bound on party size, never an estimate of it.

Bull read

Half of Airbnb’s stays are parties of 3+, which is the cross-over where an entire home beats the cost of two hotel rooms (see the party-size note). Group and family demand is compounding faster than the platform: family nights +15% y/y, multigenerational +35% (H1 2024), bedroom nights +12% vs total nights +10% (Q2 2026), 4+ bedroom homes the fastest-growing category.

Bear read

Fewer than 10% of group trips add a co-traveller to the reservation, so Airbnb knows one person per group — weak data for loyalty and personalisation, and the other 2 people are equally reachable by hotels. The 16% solo and 36% couple segments (half of bookings) are the contestable ones, and couples are where a hotel room is 20% cheaper.

Caveats

  • The distribution is a fit to anchors, not a disclosed histogram. The mean and the solo share are firm; the split between 3, 4, 5+ is the least certain part and is where a card-panel or Airbnb guest-count dataset would help most.
  • Guest arrivals are counted at check-in and bookings at reservation; over long windows the lead-time lag washes out, over short windows it doesn’t (hence 2.3–3.2 in sub-2-year windows).
  • Hawaii parties are self-reported immediate travel parties of air visitors; two families sharing one rental house appear as two parties.
  • Regional mix matters: North America books 4.1 nights and the biggest homes; APAC books 3.3 nights and mostly small units, so a North-America-only distribution would sit right of the global one.
