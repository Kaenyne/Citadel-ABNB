Margin build run, 13-14 Sep 2026. Slug `21_red_team`. Interpreter `py -3.13`.
Code `analysis/src/margin_build/21_red_team/` (`run.py`, `README.md`, `checks/check_01..check_10`).
Findings `data/processed/margin_build/21_red_team/21_findings.csv` (27 rows).

## Bottom line

**The run is clean on the things that are usually wrong and weak on the thing it claims.** Every mechanical
point-in-time rule passes on all 32 registry files and 87,628 rows: no input dated after its vintage, no
Street value stamped later than the vintage that uses it, no quarter forecast after it printed, no horizon or
window inconsistency, no broken quantile ladder, no kill-list number, no licensed row in a tracked path, and a
clean byte-identical replay of every `run.py`. The discipline held.

What does not hold is the headline. **The scoreboard's pass criterion — `survives_both_windows` AND
`rw_survives_both_windows` — is close to free at this n.** A sign-flip null over the 74 margin h=0 PIT cells
produces 22.1 survivors on average (median 16, 5-95 pct [1, 61]); the run observed 25, P(null ≥ observed) =
**0.39**. W2's ten quarters are a subset of W1's fourteen and the two weightings are highly correlated, so
"survives both windows" is one sample plus its own tail, not two tests. And **no method that does not start
from the Street beats seasonal naive by an amount distinguishable from zero**: across the 59 surviving
cell-windows, 9 reach p<0.05 and **every one of them is W2-only — not a single cell is significant in W1** —
7 of the 9 are M5, which begins from the consensus and so inherits the Street's advantage, and the other 2 are
M3's pin (section 2.3 shows where that comes from).

**Three specific things must change before WS20/23 ranks anything.** (1) Seven registered specs feed the
*actual* revenue or nights into rows labelled `prior_basis=PIT`; the best margin cell in the run by MAE is one
of them. (2) M1's `d_steps_rw` uses WS04 step dummies without the `knowable_from` gate — a real PIT violation
at 7 of 14 W1 vintages. (3) M3's two passing specs are post-hoc on the note's own pre-registration text, and
its win is carried by a single break quarter.

**One result in the run is genuinely strong and should be the thing the pitch leans on.** M5's
`street_plus_flowthrough` (Street EBITDA plus the fitted flow-through of the team's revenue surprise) beats the
raw Street on 3Q-type dollar EBITDA with a paired t of −2.71 (p 0.007, 9 of 10 quarters) in W2 and 11 of 14
quarters in W1 (sign test p 0.029). That is the only object in the run that beats the hardest available
baseline — the Street — at p<0.05.
M5's *margin* claim does not (t −0.57 W1 / −1.46 W2), and M5's LIVE 3Q26 beat is an artefact of a clipped
constant, not of the fitted dispersion relationship.

## 1. Leakage and point-in-time

### 1.1 What passes (check 01)

`check_01_registry_pit_rules.py` runs eight mechanical rules over all 32 registry files (87,628 rows, M5 included):

| rule | violations |
|---|---|
| `knowable_from` > `vintage_date` | **0** |
| `street_as_of` > `vintage_date` (the kill-list "September consensus at a historical date") | **0** (700 street rows, 288 street-bias rows) |
| target quarter already printed at the vintage | **0** |
| `horizon_q` ≠ quarters from the vintage's quarter | **0** |
| window / vintage-date mismatch (W1 row at a non-W1 date, etc.) | **0** |
| non-monotone quantile ladder | **0** |
| duplicate registry keys | **0** |
| both replays present per (object, target, spec, horizon) | 0 for the seven methods; **all seven baselines fail — see R09** |

The harness's own PIT slice is sound: `history_as_of` selects on `print_date <= vintage_date`, the guide
lookups (`fy_guide_in_force`, `q_guide_in_force`) filter on `guide_date <= vintage_date`, the cushion pool and
the seasonal shares are re-estimated at each vintage under `prior_basis="PIT"`, and the margin wrapper adds the
check the frozen revenue function lacks (it refuses a revenue guide whose guide date postdates the vintage).

**The M2 leakage surface the orchestrator flagged does not exist.** `q_guide_in_force` keys on the guidance
ledger's `print_date`, and every one of the 38 numeric margin guides in `02_guidance_ledger.csv` has
`target_period = print_quarter + 1` (or an FY). There is no retrospective row: a quarterly sentence is always
issued at the print of the preceding quarter, strictly before the quarter it describes ends. M2's k-fit pool
and its forecast both read a sentence that was public at the vintage.

### 1.2 What fails

WS04 dates `event_E09_cor_cash` (the hosting re-cut) as ON from 2025Q1 but knowable only **2026-02-12**, and
`event_E10_pd_cash` as ON from 2023Q1, knowable 2023-03-01:

| 2024-08-06 | `event_E09_cor_cash` | 2026-02-12 | 1 (2025Q1) | W1/W2 |
| 2024-11-07 | `event_E09` (+`E05`) | 2026-02-12 | 2 | W1/W2 |
| 2025-02-13 | `event_E09` (+`E05`) | 2026-02-12 | 3 | W1/W2 |
| 2025-05-01 | `event_E09_cor_cash` | 2026-02-12 | 4 | W1/W2 |

`e_revknown` do not touch the step columns. M4, which uses the same signals, gates them correctly
(`known(ks[q], vd)`), so the fix is a two-line change modelled on M4.

**R03 — seven oracle specs sit in the registry labelled `prior_basis=PIT`.** M_common rule 6 explicitly allows
a "revenue-known" spec so the reader can split cost error from revenue error, and every author labelled it in
`spec_id`. But FORMAT 1.0 has no flag for it, the scorer does not know, and the **best margin cell in the whole
run by MAE** (`driver-lines/margin_v2|e_revknown_rw`, 1.96pp W1 h=0, ratio 0.876) is one of them. The full list:

`driver-lines e_revknown_rw` (margin_v2, lines_v2) · `cycle-flex revknown_rw` (flex_margin, flex_lines) ·
`margin-ts k4_rw_revknown`, `drift_k4_rw_revknown`, `g_k4_rw_nightsknown` · `guide-policy-margin rw_hl4_revknown`.

Two of the eleven margin survivors are oracle specs. Any automated "best spec" scan in WS20 or WS23 will pick
one. Filter on the substrings `revknown` / `nightsknown` until a column exists.

### 1.3 Conventions that are legitimate but must be stated (R17)

`history_as_of` includes the same-day letter, so every h=0 forecast in this run uses the shareholder letter and
the guidance sentence published that evening. This is the frozen revenue-harness convention and it is applied
consistently, but it means **h=0 is a post-letter, post-guide forecast**, which is not the position a trade into
the 5 Nov print occupies for 4Q26. The card must say so.

## 2. Overfitting — the central finding

### 2.1 `survives_both_windows` is close to free (R01, check 07)

W2's ten target quarters are a **subset** of W1's fourteen, and the equal- and recency-weighted MAEs of the
same errors are highly correlated. The flag therefore is not two independent tests.

Null: each cell's paired loss differential `d_q = |e_method| − |e_naive|` is exchangeable with mean zero. Draw
one sign vector over the 14 W1 quarters per iteration and apply it to **every** cell (preserving cross-cell
correlation), recompute all four comparisons, count survivors. 2,000 draws over the 74 margin h=0 PIT cells
(201 margin cells are scored in total; 74 have both windows):

| | survivors |
|---|---|
| null mean | **22.1** |
| null median | 16 |
| null 5-95 pct | [1, 61] |
| **observed** | **25** |
| P(null ≥ observed) | **0.39** |
| per-cell null survival probability | 0.298 (observed rate 0.338) |

The observed count sits comfortably inside the null distribution. **The survivor list is not evidence of skill
— it is roughly what a coin flip produces**, and 30 of the 201 margin cells carry the flag. (Run without M5,
which starts from the Street and is the only method with genuine lift against naive: 11 observed against a
null mean of 16.9, P = 0.54.)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 2.4 M3's passing specs are post-hoc on its own text (R05)

M3 is the only method note in the run with **no commit predating its results**: pre-registration and results
landed together in `5bd8fb5`. M1, M2 and M7 pre-registered at `d5da4e4`, M6 at `bebbf4e`, M4 at `fdf3447`
(verified with `git log --reverse`). Worse, the amendment at line 46 says "the pre-registration above lists
five cushion specs plus `rw_hl4_pin` and `rw_hl4_revknown`" — but the pre-registration paragraph at lines
22-24 lists exactly five: `rw_hl4`, `equal`, `last`, `nocushion`, `rw_hl4_prorata`. `rw_hl4_pin` is not in it.
Both specs that pass (`rw_hl4_pin`, `last_pin`) are therefore post-hoc on the note's own account, while the
pre-registered main spec `rw_hl4` fails at 3.83pp (W1) / 1.97pp (W2). M3 should say which specs were on disk
before the first fit; until then the pin is a hypothesis for 5 Nov, not a backtested pass.

### 2.5 M2's sentence rule: the hypothesis came from the test set (R07, check 03)

`q_sentence_direction` exists because WS10 looked at the full sample and reported the direction rule right
13/14 times; M2 then pre-registered it and tested it on the same 14 quarters. What the sentence actually
contains:

| window | n | sentences with a non-zero direction | −1 / +1 | sign changes along the sequence | a constant "always −k" would agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | 14 | 13 | 9 / 4 | **3** | 8/13 |
| W2 | 10 | 9 | 7 / 2 | **2** | 6/9 |

The rule's only input flips sign three times in W1 and twice in W2, and 1Q26's "point" sentence makes that
quarter pure seasonal naive. The incremental content rests on four positive-direction quarters, three of them
in the 2023 recovery. Its MAE ratio (0.924 W1) has t = −0.22. Present it as a descriptive regularity.

### 2.6 Line-level wins are close to free (R14, check 06)

**31 of 58 (method, spec, window) cells at h=0 show the five cash lines beating seasonal naive on average
(mean MAE ratio 0.51-0.78) while the margin loses to it (ratio > 1)** — including M1's own main spec:

| method | spec | window | mean line ratio | margin ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| alt-augmented | `best1_rw` | W1 | 0.508 | 1.087 |
| driver-lines | `b_elastic_rw` | W1 | 0.532 | **1.010** |
| driver-lines | `d_steps_rw` | W1 | 0.552 | 1.142 |
| cycle-flex | `dl_rw` | W2 | 0.781 | 1.330 |
| margin-ts | `drift_k4_rw` | W1 | 0.705 | 1.690 |

Seasonal naive is a weak baseline for a growing dollar series and a strong one for a ratio, so a line-level MAE
ratio of 0.5 is nearly automatic. This confirms and generalises M4's cross-method warning: **every line claim
in this run must be re-quoted on `adj_ebitda_margin_pct`, and against `seasonal_naive_drift` or
`pct_rev_last4`, not `seasonal_naive`.**

## 3. M5 (street-bias) — audited last, after its note landed

M5's note is the most self-critical in the run: it reports that its pre-registered pass line failed for all
three objects (0 of 18 pairs pass all 8 cells), that h=1 has no harvestable bias, and that its quantiles are
unusable. The audit confirms all three and adds three findings.

**R23 (major). The margin claim does not survive a paired test; the dollar claim does.** Against the raw
Street baseline on matched quarters (check 10):

| object | target | window | n | ratio | NW(1) t | p | quarters better | sign-test p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dispersion_conditioned` `rw_hl4` | margin pt | W1 | 14 | 0.835 | −0.57 | 0.57 | 9/14 | 0.21 |
| `dispersion_conditioned` `rw_hl4` | margin pt | W2 | 10 | 0.567 | −1.46 | 0.14 | 7/10 | 0.17 |
| **`street_plus_flowthrough` `rw_hl4`** | **EBITDA $** | **W1** | 14 | **0.660** | −1.39 | 0.16 | **11/14** | **0.029** |
| **`street_plus_flowthrough` `rw_hl4`** | **EBITDA $** | **W2** | 10 | **0.581** | **−2.71** | **0.007** | **9/10** | **0.011** |

4 of 144 street-bias cells beat Street at p<0.05, and all four are the same object and target in W2 across
correlated specs — so it is one finding, not four. But it is the only cell in the entire audit that survives
an adversarial test, and it is corroborated in the other window by the sign test. **Quote M5's dollar EBITDA
flow-through; do not quote its margin-point improvement.**

**R24 (major). The dispersion regression that motivates the best margin object is significant only on the
sample M5's own pre-registration excludes.** `M5_secondary_tests.json`: signed slope +28.3, p **0.005**, n 19
— but on the pre-registered estimation pool (2022Q1+) it is +17.1, se 8.1, p **0.052**, R² 0.23, n 17. The
method declares 2021 unrepresentative and drops it from every bias pool, then justifies its conditioning
variable on a regression that keeps it.

**R25 (major). `dispersion_conditioned` is not conditioning on dispersion in the recent regime; the clip is.**
The multiplier `clip(disp/disp_ref, 0.5, 2.0)` sits **exactly at the 0.5 floor in 21 of 39 backtest
vintage-rows**, and at the floor at every h=0 vintage from 2023-05 onward except the three February dispersion
spikes (Feb-24 1.34, Feb-25 1.93, Feb-26 0.58). In that regime the object is simply `street_plus_bias` with
the bias halved by a fixed constant. At the LIVE vintage the raw ratio is **0.083** — six times below the floor
and below the whole backtest range of `disp` (0.0198-0.2257) — so the LIVE call is a pure clip. M5 says this
itself ("the cap binds; uncapped the 3Q26 margin call would be 49.9%, i.e. essentially the Street"). **The
headline LIVE 3Q26 +0.41pt / +$51M beat is produced by the clip bound, not by the fitted relationship.** On
the card it should read: the Street is at 49.78%, the bias history says higher, and record-low dispersion says
the beat is small — a directional statement, with the point taken from the dollar flow-through object.

**R26 (pass). M5's consensus inputs are point-in-time, and the 6 Aug vintage is genuinely pre-guide.** All
3,888 street-bias registry rows carry `street_as_of <= vintage_date` (zero violations; the minimum lag of 0
days is the admissible `as_of == vintage` TODAY row). At the 2026-08-06 LIVE vintage the row used is LSEG role
`guided_q_pre_guide`, `street_as_of` 2026-08-05 (D−1), `obs_date` 2026-08-02, n_est 34, 3Q26 EBITDA
**$2,323.8M** — distinct from the post-guide 11 Sep value of $2,361.5M used at the TODAY vintage. The surprise
pool filters `print_date <= vintage`, the dispersion reference pool filters `vintage_date <= vintage`, and the
unbacktested 1Q27-4Q27 `_fyalloc` rows are labelled by the author. One note: at the 2026-08-06 vintage the
history includes the same-day 2Q26 letter while the Street value is pre-letter — correct by design (the bias is
estimated from already-printed quarters), but worth stating.

**R27 (minor). M5's quantiles are unusable and the author says so**: `cov80 = cov90 = 1.00` in every scored
cell and the dollar objects' CRPS ($73-103M) is worse than the raw Street's ($48-50M), because the PIT residual
pool still holds the 2022-23 errors. Same failure as R10, in its most extreme form.
