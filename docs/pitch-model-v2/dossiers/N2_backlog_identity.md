# N2 — Backlog identity term: booked-not-yet-stayed nights from unearned fees and the RNPL unpaid share

## 1. Header
- Line: N2 · Judge's question: "How many nights sit booked but not yet stayed at each quarter-end, how did Reserve Now Pay Later change that, and how many growth points does the change in that backlog contribute to reported nights, past quarters and 3Q26–2Q27?"
- Digger: opus · Date: 2026-09-18 · Commit: d7fba07 (branch `theo/pitch-model-v2`)

**One-sentence answer to the judge.** At 30 June 2026 about **137 million nights** sat booked but not yet stayed — **0.92 quarters of booked nights, a mean lead of 2.8 months** — of which **21 million were unpaid RNPL nights** that did not exist two years ago; RNPL did **not** enlarge that backlog, it **reclassified** it (at an equal RNPL/non-RNPL ADR the backlog is exactly invariant to the unpaid share, and only lead-time lengthening `B` and the RNPL ADR premium `r` move it at all); the backlog build added roughly **+1.6 points to 1Q26 and +3.9 points to 2Q26** of reported nights growth and reverses to about **−4.7 points in 3Q26** and then to **0 / −0.5 / +0.6 in 4Q26–2Q27** once the RNPL GBV share stabilises — but we tell the judge plainly that **this term fails its one validity test** (subtracting it makes reported nights *less* like measured stays, correlation 0.863 → 0.503) and that its pre-RNPL same-season scatter is ±2–3 points, so it belongs in the memo as a **falsifier on the 5 November unearned-fees print**, not as a nights forecast.

The backlog level is well behaved; the growth term is not. Since 1Q23 `K/N` has sat in a tight seasonal channel — Q1 0.80–0.84, Q2 0.92–0.93, Q3 0.60–0.62, Q4 0.68–0.69 quarters of booked nights (2022 is recovery-distorted and excluded) — which is the same 1.8–3.0 month mean lead that Little's law on the unearned-fee pool already gave the team. The *paid* half of that coverage is what RNPL visibly broke: `K_UF/N` fell from 0.916 at 2Q25 to 0.781 at 2Q26 while total `K/N` held at 0.92. The second difference of that stock, which is what `b` is, carries a pre-RNPL same-season standard deviation of **1.9–2.6pp in Q1–Q3** (Q4 alone is tight at 0.1pp, n = 3). Holding the fee share at 0.133, the only divisor the FY2025 10-K supports, the 3Q26 band narrows only to **−13.63 to −2.44pp**.

Two things the band above does **not** contain. First, the registered norm divides unearned fees by **next-quarter** revenue, so 2Q26 inherits the team's assumed $4,800M 3Q26 revenue point: ±5% on that number moves `b(2Q26)` from **−0.10 to +7.95pp**. A same-quarter-GBV norm removes the dependence entirely and lands at **+4.25pp** against the registered +3.92pp (`norm_sensitivity.csv`). Second, `b` is a *component* of reported nights growth under `g_N = g_S + b`, not a haircut to a nights forecast; it must never be added to the RNPL module's `total_rnpl_pts` or to the bundle points from the call, which are different objects (§7).

Strongest known failure: **subtracting the backlog term from reported nights growth makes the remainder a worse match to the measured stays index than reported nights growth itself (0.863 → 0.503, and no cell of the 72-cell registered grid gets above 0.603), so the identity is arithmetically exact but empirically dominated by measurement noise in the backlog stock.** The second-strongest is that the term's headline 2Q26 value is a hostage to an assumed number: ±5% on the team's $4,800M 3Q26 revenue point moves `b(2Q26)` from −0.10 to +7.95pp. The third is that the LTM sum of `b` reached **+5.0 points in the four quarters to 2Q26** — the highest in the sample — but had already reached **+3.6 points in the four quarters to 1Q25, with no RNPL at all**, so the RNPL signal sits barely outside the pre-RNPL range.

**What a 3Q26 unearned-fees print inside or outside the band would mean.** Our `b(3Q26)` is a monotone reading of one number Airbnb will print on 5 November: quarter-end unearned fees. Under the 10-K accounting the migration term is zero, so the print maps one-for-one to an implied unpaid share and thence to an implied RNPL GBV share: $1,957M (+7.5% y/y) implies a 12.5% share and `b` = −2.8pp; **$1,781M (−2.1%) implies 21.0% and `b` = −4.2pp**; $1,667M (−8.4%) implies 26.4% and `b` = −5.1pp; $1,519M (−16.6%) implies 33.4% and `b` = −6.3pp. Inverting that mapping, the registered 21–27% adoption band is exactly a print of **$1,656–1,782M, or −9.0% to −2.1% y/y** — and its 21% end, $1,782M, sits within $20M of the governing audit's own corrected 5 November rule (unearned-fees y/y minus GBV y/y at or below −18 points, which at mid-teens GBV maps to about −3%, ~$1,765M). A print **inside** that window tells us the backlog drain is the arithmetic of an adoption plateau, which is the short case's own mechanism and nothing more. A print **below about $1,650M** puts the implied unpaid share above 25% and the implied RNPL GBV share above 27% — outside every registered scenario — and would mean either that adoption ran far past the disclosed 21%, or that unearned fees are being depressed by something this construction cannot see (a payment-terms change, a mix shift into Pay Less Upfront, or a genuine pre-payment cancellation wave that never touched either balance-sheet line). A print **at or above about $1,930M** (+6% y/y, D1's own pre-registered weakening threshold) falsifies the backlog half of N2 outright: the deferred fees would be arriving on schedule, exactly as management predicted, and the term would be withdrawn per prereg §5. Either extreme is informative; the middle is not, which is precisely why this line is a falsifier and not a forecast.

## 8. Open choices
1. **How to carry N2 in the memo.** Options: (a) as a quantified nights term in the D1/D2 bridge; (b) as a *falsifier only* — one line in the 5 November score sheet on unearned fees, with the backlog level (137m nights, 0.92 quarters, 21m unpaid) quoted as colour; (c) drop it. **Recommendation: (b).** Why: the term reproduces exactly from disclosed balance-sheet lines, but it fails the only out-of-sample check available to it, and its own pre-RNPL noise (sd 1.9–2.6pp in Q1–Q3) is as large as the effect. Why: the two agree at the team revenue point (+4.25 vs +3.92pp at 2Q26), but only (b) is forecast-free, and the ±5% revenue stress is the single widest lever in the whole construction.
4. **Backlog-scale path B(t).** Options: (a) `ramp` — lengthening scales with the RNPL GBV share (3Q25 4%, 4Q25 9%, 1Q26 20%, 2Q26 21%); (b) `step` — B jumps to its full value in 3Q25. **Recommendation: (a).** Why: a discontinuous jump in the platform-wide lead time at one launch quarter is not credible, it injects a spurious one-year level step into `b`, and `ramp` is the only variant whose implied 3Q25 RNPL GBV share (2.6%) stays inside D1's own registered 2.5–6% range — `step` implies 6.7% at B 1.05 and 9.3% at B 1.10. ## 9. Judge Q&A
1. Q: You say 137 million nights are sitting booked and unstayed. How do you know, and why should I believe a number you derived rather than one Airbnb disclosed? A: Airbnb discloses the dollars, not the nights. Unearned fees is the service fees — host *and* guest, per Note 2 of the FY2025 10-K — collected at booking and released to revenue at check-in, so the balance is a direct measure of the fee-bearing book. Divide $2,831M by the realised fee take of 13.3% of GBV and by the $183.73 ADR and you get 116 million paid nights; add the unpaid RNPL book of 21 million and you get 137 million, which is 0.92 quarters of booked nights and a 2.8-month mean lead. The cross-check is that this ratio has sat in the same seasonal channel every year since 1Q23 (Q3 0.60–0.62, Q2 0.92–0.93) and matches the 1.8–3.0 month lead the team got independently from Little's law. The band on the level is wide — 113 to 162 million across the fee-share and lead-time grid — and I would quote it.
2. Q: Did RNPL make the backlog bigger? A: No, and that is the finding I would lead with. RNPL moves a booking out of the fee-bearing pool and into an unpaid pool; the night is in the book either way. In our arithmetic, if RNPL bookings carried the same ADR as everything else the backlog would be *exactly* invariant to the unpaid share. What RNPL actually changes is two second-order things: the book is 4–6% longer-dated, which management has said in every print since 3Q25 but has never quantified, and RNPL bookings carry a higher ADR, so the same dollars hold fewer nights. The 21 million unpaid nights are a reclassification with a risk attached — the 2Q26 10-Q says in audited MD&A that they cancel more often — not 21 million extra nights of backlog.
3. Q: Then how many points of the 3Q26 nights print does this explain? A: Our central reading is that the backlog term swings from +3.9 points in 2Q26 to about −4.7 points in 3Q26, and I will tell you straight away why I would not put that in the memo as a forecast. The term's own pre-RNPL scatter in a Q3 is 1.9 points, the fee-share grid alone spans −13.6 to −2.4, and when we test it — subtract the term from reported nights growth and see whether the remainder looks more like the measured stays index — the correlation falls from 0.86 to 0.50, and no cell of the 72 we registered gets above 0.60. So the honest use is as a falsifier: on 5 November the unearned-fees line maps one-for-one to the implied unpaid share, and a print above about $1,960M kills the backlog half of this line outright.
4. Q: Your own team note from 11 September said the fee migration was confounding unearned fees and that we should score funds payable instead. Which is it? A: The later audit governs, and it refuted that note. Note 2 of the 10-K puts both fees in unearned fees, so the single-fee migration is accretive to that line by 3–4%, not dilutive; the joint solve that produced the migration share returns −1.4% for 3Q25, three months before the migration existed; and funds payable carries an ±8.7 point annual FX translation swing while unearned fees reconciles to the cash-flow statement within $3–8 million.
