**One-sentence answer to the judge.** About **0.8 points** of the 4Q26 y/y laps — not because demand turns, but because Airbnb's own 3Q25 and 4Q25 shareholder letters date the cancellation redesign and single-fee tranche 1 to **October–December 2025 and describe them as global**, while the team's inherited nights model (PR #32) laps those two legs in North America only; putting them on their disclosed dates moves 4Q26 from **+8.86% (132.7m) to +8.12% (131.8m)**, and we say plainly that this is a **comp-arithmetic claim, not a demand claim** — the one test that would have shown a demand/cancellation signature (a 34-market calendar reopening study) **did not find one**, and the 5 Nov guide alone cannot separate the two cases because both sit inside our own pre-registered inconclusive band.

## 7. Kill list and consistency
- **Kill-list check: none of this line's numbers is on AGENT_BRIEF §6.** The module's own parameter file lists the withdrawn items (−3.4pp FX step, "82% determined", +4.05% fee uplift, the 9/9 drift rule, "half of ADR is unit size", the "+10.2% consensus") explicitly as **not used**, so nobody re-imports them. Three near-misses checked and cleared: (i) the H1–H2 bridge's −1.5 / −2.5 / −3 nights overlays are unfitted and are **superseded**, not quoted, by every governing note here; (ii) the single-fee tranche-2 ramp is carried at **0.0 points of nights** on PR #32's own fee-elasticity work, not at the withdrawn +4.05% uplift; (iii) the "+10.2% consensus" that appears in older nights notes is the team's own frozen 5 Nov card, not consensus — the Street bar used here is the 12 Sep Bloomberg MODL 134.0m.
- **Conflict 1, and it is the one to fix: memo v3 publishes a range where the model carries a point.** The memo says "our 4Q26 nights **+8.1–8.9%**". The model, the bridge, the adopted print object and the registered `rnpl_v2` path all carry **8.12% / 131.8m** as the point, with 8.86% as the *top of the band*. Publishing "8.1–8.9" makes the team baseline look like a co-equal case when two independent notes (WS-D 11 Sep, N memo 2 11 Sep) recommend it be the band top. It also leaves the memo without a 4Q26 nights number a judge can check.
- **Conflict 2, the 3Q26 carry.** The 8.90 reference is PR #32's, and PR #32's 3Q26 is **+9.89%**, while DEC-0004 fixes 3Q26 at **+9.5%**. The adopted print object resolves this by *defining* its centre at Q3 = 9.5 (`q4 = 8.1 + 0.5*(Q3 − 9.5)`) while the 8.1 itself was built off a Q3 = 9.89 world. So the object uses a carry coefficient of **0.5 at the margin and 0.0 at the centre**. Applying its own β to the 0.39pt 3Q26 downgrade would put the 4Q26 base at **7.93% (131.6m)**; at β = 1.0 it is 7.73% (131.3m). This is not a rounding question; it is a −0.2 to −0.4pt question on the line the memo trades.
- **Conflict 3, the pre-registered threshold row quotes a lap range its own script rules out.** `D1_prereg_thresholds.csv`, the 4Q26-guide row, says the omitted global lap is "worth **0.9 to 1.8 points**". `D1_exna_4q26_gap.csv`, written by the **same script in the same run**, marks the 0.7-share (1.22pt) and 1.0-share (1.75pt) rows as `consistent_with_4q25_disclosure = no`. The admissible range is **0.70 to 0.88**. The threshold row should be corrected before the card freezes, or a judge who opens two files from one folder finds them disagreeing by a factor of two.
- **Conflict 4, the 2Q27 ordering inverts.** On the ledger-dated schedule the lap-only base is **6.42%** while the lap-plus-tail short is **6.44%** — the "base" is harsher than the "short". The cause is real and worth stating: by 2Q27 the module's own M1 (−1.55) is *gentler* than the ledger's dated full lap (−1.57 to −1.93) because the module nets the July-2026 expansion against it. Either the 2Q27 lap-only line or the module's M1 schedule has to move; today they cross.
- **Conflict 5, D-11 (live).** PR #32 applies a flat **−1.75** points from 1Q27 through 4Q27 and **nothing** in 4Q26. If the 4Q26 ex-NA lap is adopted and PR #32's 1Q27 row is used as-is, the fee-and-cancellation leg is charged twice — about **0.8pp**. Every 1Q27/2Q27 number in §2a is built on the `exna_lap=False` reference for exactly this reason, and the module does the same by construction. Anyone rebuilding FY27 from PR #32's `exna_lap=True` row will double count.
- **Conflict 6, the pin's provenance.** The 4Q25 "over 200 basis points" sentence (D014) that pins the 40–50% split — and therefore sizes both the cross-check and the 4Q26 gap — is flagged in the ledger as **`official_or_mirror = mirror`**, sourced to a stockanalysis.com transcript page. ## 9. Judge Q&A
1. Q: How much of the 2026 re-acceleration laps in 4Q26, and how do you know it is not demand? A: About **0.8 points** of the year-over-year rate, taking 4Q26 nights from +8.9% to **+8.1%, 131.8 million**. We know it is a lap and not a demand call because we did not estimate it — we dated it. Airbnb's own shareholder letters say the cancellation redesign was announced **in October 2025, globally**, and that PMS hosts moved to the single 15.5% fee **from October** and most remaining single-fee hosts **from December**. The team's inherited nights model laps those two features in North America only. Put them on the dates and the geography the filings give and the comp gets harder from 4Q26 by construction, whatever demand does. The one free parameter — how much of the non-North-American bundle those two legs are — is pinned at 40 to 50% by requiring the model to reproduce management's own "over 200 basis points" figure for 4Q25, a quarter it was not fitted on. And I will give you the limit: that is a comp-arithmetic claim. It is not a claim that demand is deteriorating, and I would not make that claim, because we tested for it and did not find it.
2. Q: You say you tested for it. What did you run and what did it show? A: A calendar-reopening study across 34 markets, pre-registered: if Reserve Now Pay Later were generating excess cancellations, listings outside the US should have reopened more after the 17 February 2026 international launch than US listings did. The contrast is **+2.1 points with a permutation p of 0.26** — not significant at any conventional threshold — and it collapses when you drop one 29-listing market, and what does move is **Australia**, which is in the opposite season. So we retired that line of attack and we keep the null on the record. It matters for how you should read the thesis: the cancellation drag is **−0.1 to −0.9 points** on the cohort engine's central cells, which is two to fifteen times smaller than the level lap. This is a lap-and-pull-forward thesis with a cancellation tail attached, not a cancellation thesis.
3. Q: Your own model says 132.7 million. Why is your number 131.8? A: Both numbers are in our model and the difference is a single, stated assumption. 132.7 is the inherited build, which treats the cancellation redesign and the fee migration as North-American. 131.8 dates them globally, as the letters do. Two of our workstreams reached 131.8 independently on the same day from the same files, and the memo carries 132.7 as the **top of the band**, not as a rejected case. In revenue terms the gap is about **$21 million** at our 4Q26 ADR — this is a growth-rate disagreement, not a dollar disagreement, and it should be read that way against the print.
4. Q: What is the weakest link? A: The 40-to-50% split. It is the only assumed parameter in the chain, it is pinned by a single sentence from the 4Q25 call, and in our repository that sentence sits as a transcript mirror rather than the official IR file — the *dates* are SEC-filed letters, the *magnitude* is not. Worse for us: our own pre-registered thresholds say a 4Q26 nights guide between 7.6% and 9.4% is inconclusive, and **both** our cases sit inside it. So the guide number alone will not settle this on 5 November.
5. Q: Then what does settle it, and when? A: Two things. On **5 November**, whether management repeats a quantified bundle contribution for 3Q26. They gave "over 200 basis points" for 4Q25 and "approximately three points" for 1Q26, then stopped in 2Q26. A figure at or below **1.5 points**, or silence again, says the anniversary is biting broadly, which is our case; **2.5 points or more** says the bundle is still adding regardless of geography, and our case weakens. On **11 February**, the 1Q27 guide: at or above **+8.2%, 169.0 million**, our module is falsified outright, because that requires both the partial ex-Northern-America lap and the pull-forward reversal to be absent. Those are written down, dated before the print, and we will score them either way.
6. Q: What would a bull have to believe? A: That the tranche-2 fee ramp and the July 2026 booking-type expansion — which Airbnb disclosed but has never sized — more than cover the whole 2025 bundle lap. That is the honest bull case and it is the largest unquantified offset in the model; we carry it at +0.1 to +0.3 points and a bull could reasonably carry it higher. If they are right, 4Q26 comes in at **10% or better, 134.1 million**, which is the Street's bar, and our adopted distribution puts that at **0.31**.
