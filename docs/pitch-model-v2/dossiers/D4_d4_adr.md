# D4 — ADR ex-FX and its decomposition

## 1. Header
- Line: D4 · Judge's question: "Your ADR sits on consensus for 3Q26; where does the price leg come from and how much is mix?"
- Digger: opus · Date: 2026-09-18 · Commit: e39d9e4

- One-line answer: the price leg is **not measured**. 4.85 of the 3.86pp of 3Q26 ex-FX ADR growth is an unobserved like-for-like pricing residual carried forward from 2Q26 by an assumed persistence rule; the three *measured* mix terms (geo, unit size, length of stay) net to **−0.57pp** and the two assumed fills to **−0.58pp**. Mix is therefore a net drag of about one point, not a support, and "bigger units" is +0.80pp — a fifth of the ex-FX move, never half.

## 2. The number

Convention. Point, low and high are the three scenarios; the scenario device is the one line that moves, the unobserved residual and its fee-migration increment, set from K4's residual scenario table and K2's pre-stated coefficient range:
- **short** — residual at the K4 floor (3Q26 2.398 = the 2023–25 mean, mean reversion; 4Q26 3.056 = every dated product step laps at its anniversary), K line at its central mechanics point.
- **base** — residual at `last_q` 4.849 (the pre-registered v3 rule = the 2Q26 value), K line at its point. This is the committed card v3 `v3_with_K` / midpoint-FX cell.
- **breaker** — residual at `last_q`, K line at the top of K2's pre-stated coefficient range (0.038 per pp of migrated-share change).

Measured and assumed mix terms are held at their card points in all three scenarios; FX is N's midpoint estimator in all three. On the three **headline** rows the low/high columns carry the scenario envelope (short point → breaker point), so they repeat across the three scenario rows; on the **term** rows they carry that term's own lo/hi from the card. Every row is **descriptive**: the L3 audit (13 Sep) rules that this decomposition is conditional on information unavailable at a historical guide date, so no row below is a validated forecast. ## 9. Judge Q&A
1. Q: "Your ADR sits on consensus for 3Q26 — where does the price leg come from and how much is mix?"
   A: Almost none of it is mix, and we cannot measure the price leg. Of +3.86% ex-FX, the three terms we actually measured — geography −1.43, unit size +0.80, length of stay +0.06 — net to **−0.57pp**, a drag. Two assumed fills, seats/new-business dilution and an interaction term, take off another −0.58pp. Everything left, **+4.85pp**, is an unobserved like-for-like pricing residual, and our forecast of it is simply "the same as last quarter". We label that line on the slide rather than hide it, because it is where the whole forecast sits. That is also why we are on consensus for 3Q26 and do not trade ADR: our variant view is nights. The price leg lands in 4Q26, when FX turns and this residual has to persist at 1H26 levels for the Street's number to work.
2. Q: "Airbnb told you bedroom nights grew over 12% against nights at 10%. Isn't half your ADR growth bigger homes?"
   A: No, and that is a mapping error the Street is making. The +2pp wedge is in bedroom *count*, not price. On Airbnb's own quote data the local elasticity of ADR to bedroom count is **0.23**: the average booked listing has 1.65 bedrooms, so one more bedroom is +61% of bedroom count but only **+15.05%** of price. Priced through the same hedonics, the disclosed wedge is worth about +1.26pp of ADR; our direct measurement on 29 paired markets gives **+0.63pp**, and the card's review-weighted construction gives +0.80pp. Call it +0.6 to +0.8pp — about a fifth of ex-FX growth, not half. We also corroborated the disclosure rather than dismissing it: three independent size measures agree, and enlarging the panel from 12 to 29 markets *halved* the global wedge, so the data now supports **less** of the ≥+2pp than the urban-only panel did. Two warnings if you check our work: Inside Airbnb's `bedrooms` field changes population inside the window, almost all on private rooms, and reading it naively prints +1.44pp instead of +0.89pp; and the size story is a North America and EMEA phenomenon — in LatAm and APAC, which carry 52% of Airbnb's nights growth, the wedge is nil or negative.
3. Q: "You say your decomposition beats naive. Does it?"
   A: On one target, in one sense, with three caveats we will give you unprompted. On unrounded reported dollar ADR y/y it beats "no change in ex-FX" at ratios 0.87–0.92 across ten and nine quarters under both FX estimators, with every drop-one sample below one. First caveat: on the letter's own whole-point ex-FX sentence it is indistinguishable from naive — 0.985 and 1.080. Second: the rule was chosen after seeing the sensitivity table and pre-registered afterwards, and the binding jackknife margin is 0.011. Third, and the one that matters: our own 13 September audit found the walk-forward reads realised quarter-t mix and retrospectively calibrated FX, so those ratios are conditional on information nobody had at a guide date. We treat them as a description of a persistent series, not as a forecasting edge, and we say so on the slide.
4. Q: "What is the downside if you are wrong on the residual?"
   A: It is one-sided and bigger than our band looks. If the residual mean-reverts to its 2023–25 level of 2.40 instead of holding at 4.85, 3Q26 reported ADR is **+0.98% / $172.97** instead of +3.43% / $177.17 — 2.45pp of ADR, about $616M of 3Q26 GBV, $110M of 3Q26 revenue, roughly $2.4bn annualised. Nothing we measured points the other way: the fee reprice is worth 0.2pp at central mechanics, new-listing composition 0.05pp, and the regional carry sits 0.3–0.7pp below the rule. We priced that branch at roughly 0.12–0.17 and carry it as the downside scenario, not a tail.
5. Q: "What would change your mind, and when?"
   A: Three disclosures on 5 November, in order of power. First, a management split of the product bundle's ADR contribution by feature — or the RNPL GBV share alongside the RNPL nights share for the same quarter, whose ratio is the RNPL ADR premium and would pin the mix leg and leave the fee leg as the remainder. Second, whether the Bedroom Nights Booked metric returns at all: it has exactly one data point, and this company has form for dropping metrics that stop flattering the story — it discontinued the 1BR-versus-hotel comparison after 4Q23 and the long-term-stay share after 1Q24. Third, the printed residual itself: near 5.0 keeps our rule; 3.9–4.1 says the lap arithmetic describes it better and 4Q26 should move to 3.1–3.8; below 3.6 is the mean-reversion case and retires the rule for 4Q26.
