Airbnb's revenue isn't forecast. It's converted.

Six pictures. Each one shows a piece of the mechanism, and where the Street's model looks the other way.

1A stay is paid for long before it happens

Airbnb records the booking (GBV) when the guest books. It records revenue when the guest checks in — often one or two quarters later.

2The conversion ratio barely moves

Divide each quarter's revenue by the bookings printed in the two quarters before it (⅔ last quarter, ⅓ the one before). Call it λ. Here it is for the last three years.

3Two ways to get to the same number

Side by side: how a sell-side model reaches its Q4 estimate, and how the kernel does.

4The Q4 "slowdown" is the dollar, carried through the kernel

Growth in the bookings base falls from Q3 to Q4. Here is why, in two pieces.

5So where should the forecasting effort go?

Revenue = λ × GBV. One factor is nearly constant. The other carries all the uncertainty.

6What 5 November actually resolves

Revenue is pinned by the guide and the kernel. So the printed "take rate" is just revenue ÷ GBV — and it moves with whichever GBV view is right.

Airbnb tells you more than half of next quarter's revenue every time it prints GBV; the ratio that turns bookings into revenue hasn't moved in three years; the Street forecasts the quarter as if neither fact existed. Where the work goes next: nowcast booked GBV (calendar pickup, stays index, arrivals), watch λ for drift as RNPL cancellations rise, and screen each quarter for gaps between the kernel-implied guide and consensus. Where it doesn't go: take-rate levers, seasonality models, FX as an input.
