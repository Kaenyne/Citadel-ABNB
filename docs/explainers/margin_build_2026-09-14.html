How Airbnb's margin is modelled, and what it says for 5 November

Twenty-two workstreams built and tested a forecast of Airbnb's adjusted EBITDA margin, its six cost lines, the bridge down to EPS, and the margin sentence management will put in the November letter. An external auditor then took the whole thing apart. This page is the plain-words version: what the model does, what it forecasts, what it could not forecast, and how much of it is actually evidence.

The third quarter is a small beat, and it is a revenue beat wearing a margin costume. The model puts adjusted EBITDA $38M above consensus, but at the Street's own revenue the margin view is worth only about $8M. The probability that our margin beats their margin is 0.54 — a coin flip. The probability that our dollars beat their dollars is 0.78, and that is because our revenue line is $60M higher, not because we have a better view of costs.

The fourth quarter is where we have no edge and say so. Every object in the build loses to the raw analyst consensus one quarter out, by 20–28%, so 4Q26 is quoted from the Street. That matters, because the trade into the November print is structurally a fourth-quarter position: the full-year sentence management gives lands on Q4 with a 4.5x amplifier.

2027 is the disagreement worth having. Our path says adjusted EBITDA of $5,483M against a Street of $5,766M — an incremental margin of 24.7% against the Street's implied 43.7%. The entire gap is one line, sales and marketing, at 23.5% of revenue against 19.4% two years earlier. It is labelled a spending scenario rather than a forecast, because the model fails its own test that far out. But nothing in the build supports the Street's number.

How the model works

It is a committee, not a theory. Six independent views of next quarter's margin are averaged, then capped by whatever management has already said about that quarter. Everything is refit at each historical guide date using only what was public on that day.

Six membersThe Street's own consensus; the Street plus its recency-weighted historical bias; the Street plus 0.464 times our revenue surprise; the full-year guide treated as a budget and allocated across quarters; last year's margin plus a coefficient on the direction of management's quarterly sentence; and a cost-stack family that builds margin up from five driver-based cash cost lines.
Weights that barely matterEach member is weighted by the inverse of its mean absolute error over quarters that had already printed on that vintage date, then shrunk halfway toward equal weights. The weights land between 0.146 and 0.201 at every vintage, and equal weighting scores the same. This is a pool result, not a weighting result.
A clip, not an overrideIf management has a live sentence about the quarter ("margin down slightly versus Q3 2025"), the point is capped or floored at last year's level plus that sentence. Zero estimated parameters — the sentence is already public when the forecast is made. It binds in 2 of 14 backtest quarters and helps both times. It does not bind today.
Dollars and bandsDollars are the margin times the revenue path (the H2 bridge v3 today; the frozen harness leg in the backtest). The interval is split-conformal on the object's own point-in-time errors, not the model's own quantiles — those over-cover everywhere in this build.
Below EBITDAA separate bridge supplies stock compensation, depreciation, interest income, tax rate and share count down to GAAP diluted EPS. Interest income is the one genuinely good object below the line: it is modelled as the earning cash base times the 3-month Treasury bill, and it beats the hardest baseline available.
Two parameters, fifty-two inheritedThe wrapper adds two (the shrinkage constant, fixed before any result, and a fitted residual scale). The members bring 52. The registry publishes 54. Free-parameter counts are published because this is a fourteen-observation problem.

Every historical test uses fourteen guide dates from 1Q23 (window W1) and the ten from 1Q24 (window W2). A claim has to survive both windows and both weightings before anyone quotes it. Source: analysis/src/margin_build/23_final_model/, pre-registration in prereg.json.

The shape of the year, and why Q1 is a 19% margin

Airbnb's margin swings thirty points across a calendar year. Almost none of that is a cost decision.

Adjusted EBITDA margin by quarter, 2022–2027

ReportedForecast / scenarioStreet, 3Q26 and 4Q26
Reported margins 1Q22–2Q26 and the forecast path 3Q26–4Q27. The Street's 3Q26 (49.78%) and 4Q26 (28.90%) sit as grey squares; on 4Q26 the Street is our number. Source: 23_seasonality.csv, 23_vs_consensus.csv.

The seasonality is a revenue phenomenon, not a cost one. The two mechanical cost lines — cost of revenue and operations & support — move from 20.4% of revenue in Q3 to 31.5% in Q1 almost entirely because the denominator halves. In dollars they barely move at all: across the six forecast quarters cost of revenue runs $544–688M and operations $316–378M, ranges of 27% and 20%, against revenue's range of 73%.

The discretionary block is the only thing that could smooth the year, and it does not. Product development, sales and marketing and G&A together run $1,418–1,701M every quarter regardless of season. That is the whole explanation for a 19.5% first quarter: $1,519M of discretionary spend meets $3,053M of revenue.

And 3Q26 at 49.94% is one standard deviation below its own seasonal mean of 51.76% (2022–26, sd 1.81pp). That gap is the marketing ramp, not a demand problem.

This is the whole 2027 argument. Our FY27 incremental margin is 24.7%; the Street's implied incremental margin is 43.7%. Airbnb has given no full-year 2027 margin guidance and a search of every letter and call found no hint of one. The Street's number requires the marketing ramp to stop. Ours requires it to continue at roughly the rate of the last six quarters. Both are spending assumptions; only one of them is labelled as such.

The weakest number in our own stack is G&A at +9.4% for 3Q26, when the first half of 2026 actually ran −5.4% year over year. The line stack allocates its reconciliation residual mechanically — 76.6% into sales and marketing, 9.4% into product development, 14.0% into G&A, by point-in-time error variance. A judgement version would push more into marketing, which makes the marketing call larger, not smaller.

At the horizon that matters on 5 November — the quarter being guided — yes, decisively, and against the hardest baseline available. One quarter further out, no. Two quarters out, no.

The two consensus-correction members are the accuracy; the raw Street, the guide member and the cost stack are insurance, and they cost 0.02–0.04pp of error for it. The cost-stack family is kept because it is the only member that can say why the margin moves.

There is a version that never touches consensus. Strip out every Street-anchored member and the same machinery prints 49.88% for 3Q26 — within 0.06pp of the full combination — and still runs at 0.61–0.63x the naive. That is the version to quote if anyone objects that a pitch claiming to be above consensus cannot use consensus as an input.

It survives the hard quarters. In the 1H23 regime break the combination errs 0.79pp against the Street's 1.34. Over the last four quarters it errs 0.45pp against the Street's 0.69 and the naive's 1.81. The one bad stretch is 2H23, which was hard for everything.

The caveat that is not in the p-values. The six-member pool was selected after looking at the scoreboard, and there is no untouched evaluation set anywhere in this build. Every ratio and probability on this page is retrospective and conditional on that pool. Small differences between the point-in-time and full-sample replays, and the stability of the leave-one-out table, are not evidence against selection bias — they are consistent with a pool chosen on the same fourteen quarters. The model is frozen at this vintage; 5 November is the first genuinely out-of-sample observation, and it is one observation.

The cycle: what happens if revenue disappoints

Costs respond to revenue — but slowly, asymmetrically, and by an amount we cannot pin down precisely. The honest answer is a band, and it is wide enough to matter for the full-year guide.

Margin lost per 1 point of revenue shortfall

Costs heldCosts flex at 0.364Costs flex one-for-one
Points of adjusted EBITDA margin lost per 1% shortfall in revenue, at three cost responses. The honest default sits between "held" and "flex". Source: 23_macro_sensitivity.csv.

We cannot measure a cost dial at Airbnb — but that is imprecision, not proof of rigidity. Regressing total operating expense on revenue across the peer set gives Airbnb an elasticity of 0.14 with a t-statistic of 0.47 on 18 quarters: a standard error near 0.30 and a 95% interval of roughly −0.44 to +0.72. That interval does not exclude flexibility as large as Booking's 0.61, TripAdvisor's 0.63 or Expedia's 0.44. An earlier draft of this work said "Airbnb has no cost dial"; that sentence has been withdrawn.

What the model actually uses is a different, better-identified quantity: total cash costs flex at 0.364 of a revenue move (t 6.58, n 18). Cost of revenue is the one cost line anywhere in this build that beats a drift baseline — elasticity 0.56, better in 13 of 14 and 9 of 10 quarters.

And the response is asymmetric. Costs fall more slowly than they rise for operations (p 0.0002), sales and marketing (p 0.0030) and total cash costs (p 0.018). Flexing recovers only 30–40% of a revenue miss. Use 0.35–0.6 points of FY27 margin per point of revenue.

Built from the evidence alone, the third quarter does not decline. Costs grow 11.4% against revenue of +17%, which puts 3Q26 at 52.4%, three points above management's "down slightly". Landing the sentence at the guide midpoint needs about $97M more cost in the quarter than the filings support, and the only lines management has flagged for a step are marketing and AI hosting. The base case takes management at its word: $67M into 3Q26 marketing as a timing step and $29M a quarter into hosting as a run-rate step. That gives 3Q26 $2,420M at 50.4%, a revenue beat on management's own cost budget, within $21M of the combination.

The step cannot recur in Q4. Carrying the same cost into 4Q26 would put the full year below the 35.5% floor at guide revenue, and the floor has been beaten every year. So 4Q26 is 28.3% and FY26 lands at 35.7%, the same number the combination reached by a different route.

2027 is one point higher than the combination, all of it marketing. With the 10-K split and management's stated deceleration, FY27 S&M is 21.9% of revenue rather than the allocated 23.5%, and FY27 margin is 35.7% ($5,644M) against the combination's 34.6% and the Street's 36.5%. The incremental margin is 34.8%, between the run's 24.7% and the Street's 43.7%.

The budget identity is how to trade the guide. With the first half actual and full-year revenue of $14,268M, 1 point of the FY26 margin sentence equals 4.49 points of fourth-quarter margin, and 1 point of the third quarter equals −1.51 points of the fourth. So a sentence of "approximately 36%" at our third quarter requires a fourth quarter of 30.1% against a Street of 28.9% — a 1.2-point, roughly $39M gap, and the single most contestable number in the guide.

Read the floor case as an inequality. If management simply leaves the sentence at "at least 35.5%", the arithmetic says only that the fourth quarter must be at least about 27.6%. Our own 28.90% already satisfies it, as does the Street's, as does the guide path's 29.90%. An unchanged floor is the absence of a raise, not a guide-down — and whether the absence of a raise is a sell is a price-reaction question this build did not test.

What the print settles. First, the 10-Q's split of sales and marketing into brand and performance — our $781M is the whole call, and the split says whether the ramp is a fixed commitment or a dial. Second, whether "down slightly" was a ceiling or a floor: the quarterly sentence has been missed slightly more often than beaten (mean −0.19pp, above in 4 of 10), which is why the model clips rather than overrides. Third, the full-year sentence itself, amplified 4.49 times into the fourth quarter.

What was tried and failed

Seven methods, four pre-registered tests in the final model, two of them failed and are reported. A build this size with no failures would be a build that never tested anything.

Inside the final model

  • One quarter out, it loses to the raw Street by 19–28%. The pre-registered consequence was applied: 4Q26 is quoted from consensus.
  • Two quarters out it fails outright in the short window (1.026x the naive). 2027 is a labelled scenario.
  • The weights do almost nothing. Equal weighting scores 1.123 / 0.802 against the fitted 1.126 / 0.788.
  • The intervals still over-cover — 93% and 100% against a nominal 80% — even as conformal bands. At ten to fourteen observations an interval cannot be tuned finer than the rank grid.

The seven methods

  • Driver-based cost lines: both pass lines failed. Building margin from the cost stack is a tie with last year's margin. Only cost of revenue survives against a drift baseline.
  • Time series on Airbnb's own history: nothing beats last year's margin by a distinguishable amount. The best object has t = −0.22.
  • Guide policy: the main spec failed; the full-year cushion over-predicts by 1.2–3.2 points and cannot be estimated on more than four fiscal years. What survives is the allocation rule and the budget identity.
  • Alt data: 50 pre-registered tests, one survivor, and its own placebo says that pass line fires on noise 5.5–22.5% of the time. See below.
  • Street bias: the margin claim is withdrawn; the dollar flow-through survives and is the single strongest object in the run.
  • Cycle flex: the forecaster is a tie and fails beyond h=0. What survives is the measurement — the elasticities and the peer table.
  • Below EBITDA: quarterly free cash flow fails and is not quoted. Interest income is the one genuine edge below the line.

The alt-data verdict, in one line: there is no external dataset within our reach that improves the margin call. Fifty-eight series over 1,146 screening tests produced two candidates — short rates against interest income, and a search-category share against marketing per night. Only the first survives a strict "was this knowable at the time" gate. Fifty pre-registered point-in-time tests then produced one survivor, and across fifty tests the null expects exactly one.

Two by-products are worth more than any of the signals. A calibrated false-positive rate for this name — a line-level pass line of the kind used here fires on pure noise 2.5% to 22.5% of the time, and 18 of 50 real tests beat the baseline in both windows, which is 36%, the coin-flip rate. And a mechanism worth putting in the memo: good cost lines do not make a good margin. The object with the best cash lines in the build is one of the worst on margin, because marketing errors are negatively correlated with the rest of the stack, so "improving" marketing in isolation removes a hedge.

The critical finding was a timing leak, and it is fixed. The calibration pools that set the weights and the interval were selecting earlier quarters by quarter order rather than by publication date, so a forecast made on 9 May was being weighted on a quarter that did not report until 3 August. Both pools are now gated on the print date. The quarter-being-guided results cannot be affected by this and did not move at all; the one- and two-quarter-out numbers moved by up to a quarter of a point. Every pass/fail verdict survived the correction.

Three numbers on the card changed and the old ones are withdrawn. The 3Q26 dollar band is $2,337–2,462M (it was $2,299–2,499M); the beat probability is 0.78 (the published 0.77 mixed a band from one object with a standard deviation from another); and 3Q26 marketing is $781M / +33.5% (it was $790M / +35%) once one add-back schedule was imposed on both the cost stack and the GAAP bridge.

Four claims were rewritten rather than recomputed: the results are retrospective and conditional on a post-hoc pool; "attained coverage 82–91%" was never a coverage measurement and is withdrawn; the full-year floor is an inequality, not a point forecast or a sell signal; and "Airbnb has no cost dial" confused imprecision with zero.

Nothing was rejected. One half of one minor finding is deferred: making the workbook a live input-driven model rather than a frozen report is a rebuild, not an audit fix. The build now asserts eight accounting identities and exits with an error if any of them breaks, and a no-write verification mode lets the next auditor replay everything without touching the outputs.

Provenance. Every number on this page is read from a CSV under data/processed/margin_build/, named beside the table that uses it. The model rebuilds end to end with py -3.13 analysis/src/margin_build/23_final_model/run.py (exit 0). Consensus is LSEG at 11 September 2026 with Bloomberg BEST at 5 September as a cross-check; the revenue path is the H2 bridge v3. Registry objects: final-margin__combined and final-margin__combined_dollar_from_margin.

What this page deliberately does not contain. Several numbers produced during this build were withdrawn and must not be quoted anywhere: the 77% beat probability and the $2,299–2,499M band; "attained coverage 82–91%"; "Airbnb has no cost dial"; the earlier claim that the FY26 floor survives a 1.9–2.5% revenue shortfall; any quarterly free-cash-flow forecast; any reading of an unchanged "at least 35.5%" as implying a 27.6% fourth quarter or an automatic sell; and any margin error ratio quoted without its p-value and its quarters-better count.
