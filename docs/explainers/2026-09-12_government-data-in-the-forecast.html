How government data feeds the Q3 and Q4 forecast

Official travel-volume and travel-price statistics were pulled from eleven countries and three international agencies, tested against Airbnb's own reported numbers, and sorted into what predicts, what corroborates, and what does not. This page explains the pipeline, where each series plugs into the forecast, and what the series say right now.

The answer in three sentences

Government data is the corroboration layer, never an input to the point estimates. The nights baseline comes from Airbnb's own disclosed quarters and the product-bundle lap; the ADR card comes from a term-by-term decomposition measured on Inside Airbnb review data. Every official series was then tested against those same disclosed numbers, and the ones that passed are used to check direction, region by region, not to set the forecast.

One family passed the out-of-sample bar on every window with a pre-print reading, US inbound arrivals for nights, and it is treated as a statistical correlate rather than a mechanism. Three others pass on all windows but only against a regional bucket that management discloses as a two- or three-value step, so they fit stability rather than movement. Roughly twenty pass only on the short window, which is the signature of the post-pandemic normalisation, not of prediction. A reviewer pass audited all 73 verdicts: 58 upheld, 11 reclassified, 4 overturned, and the conclusion stood.

Right now the official readings agree with the cards: a stack of 23 observable series puts 3Q26 nights at a median +9.6% against the team's +9.9%, European accommodation prices are holding while North American lodging prices decelerate, and nothing official argues the ADR residual is about to collapse.

The pipeline: from a statistics website to a verdict

Every series goes through the same five steps. The bar is not "does it correlate" but "would it have beaten the dumbest forecast, quarter after quarter, without knowing the answer".

CollectA script pulls the official file (CSV, xlsx, SDMX or JSON), caches it, and records the URL, access date, byte size and hash in a manifest. Blocked pulls are logged with their HTTP status rather than dropped.
AlignDaily or monthly values are aggregated to calendar quarters and expressed as year-over-year growth. Each series gets two vintages: the full quarter, and the quarter-to-date version that was observable on the test date.
Test walk-forwardStarting in 1Q24, fit a one-variable regression on quarters strictly before the scored quarter, predict it, move forward, repeat. The error is compared with three naive forecasts: last quarter's value, last year's value, and a simple autoregression.
Check robustnessTwo windows, from 2023 and from 2022. A jackknife drops each scored quarter in turn. A permutation test shuffles the series a thousand times. A flag records whether the reading is knowable before the print.
Assign a verdictSurvivor, short-window survivor, corroboration, duplicate, or eliminated on one stated criterion: no coverage, no history, no access, wrong asset class. One reason per elimination, so a reviewer can check it.
ReviewA separate pass retries every blocked pull by a different route, verifies each duplicate claim by correlation, re-runs the top survivors, and looks for source classes the surveys missed.

The naive benchmark matters because Airbnb's growth rates are persistent. "Same as last quarter" has a root-mean-square error of about 2.2 points on nights and 0.9 on ex-FX ADR. A series has to do better than that out of sample, with at least six scored quarters, before it earns a place.

Where each family plugs into the forecast chain

The chain runs nights times ADR to gross booking value, then lagged GBV times a conversion rate to revenue. Government data attaches at three points and is absent at two.

Nights, total
Core: PR #32 quarterly model, +9.9% for 3Q26, 8.1% for 4Q26. Alt-data check: reviews stays index.
Government layer: a stack of observable-today survivors gives a median read (+9.2% on 12 series, +9.6% on 23). A consistency check, not a competing forecast.
Nights, by region
Core: letter regional buckets; reviews index split NA +6.9, EMEA +0.9, LatAm +27.5, APAC +10.0.
Government layer: arrivals, border crossings and accommodation nights by country, read for direction. Spain's apartment nights and EU short-stay nights side with the reviews index on EMEA.
ADR
Core: seven-term decomposition, +3.4% for 3Q26, $177.2. The pricing residual is unobserved.
Government layer: accommodation CPI, hotel PPI and PCE price indices by country, tested against the residual. None beats naive on it. Used as a direction read: Europe holding, North America decelerating.
FX and revenue
FX: fitted from euro and basket rates. Revenue: lagged GBV times conversion, $4.80bn for 3Q26.
Government layer: none. Census lodging revenue and BEA travel exports arrive after the print and serve the February FY27 view, not this cycle.

  • Never a number in the model. The cards' points do not move on any of this.

What it says about 4Q26, and when it can say more

Almost nothing yet. The 4Q26 nights case is arithmetic on the product-bundle lap dates, and the 4Q26 ADR carries the 3Q26 terms. Government data enters the fourth quarter through the score sheet, not the forecast.

The 4Q26 numbers and their basis

  • Nights +8.1%, 131.8mm, from the global lap of the October 2025 cancellation redesign and fee change. Top of the band +8.9% if the lap is North America only. sourced dates one assumed split
  • ADR +4.2%, $174.6, carrying the 3Q26 mix terms and the last-quarter residual rule. The residual band for 4Q26 is 3.1 to 5.2 from the lap scenarios.
  • Revenue $3.17bn from lagged GBV, against $3.20bn consensus and an implied November guide midpoint near $3.05 to 3.07bn.

None of these three rests on an official series. The September and October releases below can move the direction read before the 5 November score, and the 20 October re-score is the planned checkpoint.

Why none of it is an input, in four reasons

  1. Asset class. Most official series count hotel nights, airline passengers or border crossings. Airbnb's KPI is a platform night in a private home. The few same-class series, Spain's tourist apartments, EU short-stay accommodation and Hawaii rentals, are either one market or arrive after the quarter closes.
  2. Stayed versus booked. Official series measure travel that happened. Airbnb reports bookings made in the quarter, including bookings for stays months later. That lead-time gap is three to eight weeks on average and is where the product bundle acts.
  3. The short-window trap. Six of fourteen families beat naive on the window from 2023 and none on the window from 2022. Everything decelerated together as travel normalised, so a series can look predictive on fourteen observations without predicting anything. The reviewer made this concrete: on a window from 2024, the target's own expanding mean beats naive on nights at 0.62, and every short-window survivor loses to that mean.
  4. The residual is not a price index. The unobserved ADR term is Airbnb's like-for-like pricing plus sub-regional mix plus the fee reprice. Accommodation CPI tracks hotel-weighted prices. The best of 164 tests against the residual was 1.03 times naive. The one thing that would identify it is a management split of the bundle's ADR contribution by feature.

The honest sentence for a teammate: the official data tells us the direction of the quarter by region and confirms nothing is wildly off, and it cannot tighten the bands, because it is less precise than the disclosed quarters it would replace.
