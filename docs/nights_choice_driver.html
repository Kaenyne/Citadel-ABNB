Turning “hotel or Airbnb?” into the nights line

The team model already does Revenue = Nights × ADR × take rate × FX. Nights is the plug. This page replaces the plug with a choice model: how many parties are in the market, how many of them would even consider a hotel, and what fraction of those pick Airbnb — by party size, moving with relative price. Everything else in the model stays where it is.

NightsAirbnb(t) = Σg [ Ng(t) + Mg(t) × Pg(t) ]
g = party size {solo, pair, 3–4, 5+}
Ng = own-category Airbnb nights — guests who would not have used a hotel (62%, Farronato & Fradkin); grows with category adoption
Mg = hotel-contestable party-nights = hotel party-nights + the other 38% of Airbnb nights; grows with U.S. lodging demand and segment mix
Pg = probability a contestable party picks Airbnb;   logit Pg(t) = logit Pg(t−1) − β·[ln(1+ADRAirbnb) − ln(1+ADRhotel)] + product shift
Revenue = Nights × ADR × take rate × FX  (unchanged)

  • Airbnb is 10.9% of U.S. lodging party-nights (145M vs 1,185M hotel). Consistent with AirDNA’s “STRs ≈ 12–15% of lodging demand” once Vrbo and others are added.
  • Share rises fivefold with party size: 6% of solo stays → 31% of 5+ stays. That is Jessie’s framing in numbers: solo/pair is a hotel market Airbnb dips into; 5+ is a market Airbnb owns a third of.
  • Only 38% of Airbnb nights are “at risk” to hotels. The other 62% (Farronato & Fradkin’s guest survey) would have stayed with friends, booked another rental, or not travelled — they grow with the category, not with hotel pricing.

How the calibration is built

  1. Airbnb U.S. nights = North America 158M × 92% (U.S. is 39% of revenue, NA is 43%). Split into party sizes with the fitted booking distribution (16 / 36 / 32 / 16%) × relative stay length (solo 1.5×, pair 0.95×, 3–4 0.9×, 5+ 0.8×).
  2. Hotel party-nights = 1.3B room nights (STR, 2024; +1% for 2025) → 42% business (AHLA), 80% of it single-occupancy (Portuguese ledger) → 58% leisure split into parties with the Hawaii/Las Vegas mix (20 / 54 / 20 / 6%) and rooms per party (1 / 1 / 1.5 / 2.5).
  3. Contestable pool = hotel party-nights + 38% of Airbnb nights. Pg = contestable Airbnb ÷ pool.
  4. Relative price per segment from the cross-over table (Inside Airbnb medians + 14% fee vs rooms × $159 ADR).

What it produces

Base case: U.S. lodging demand per CoStar/Tourism Economics (+1.7% 2026, +1.1% 2027), hotel ADR +3.1% / +1.6%, Airbnb ADR +5% then +3% (placeholder — link to the team’s ADR line), own-category growth 4% fading to 3%, mix drift toward 3+ parties, β = 2.5.

Wiring it into the team model

  1. Open docs/nights_choice_driver.xlsx. Inputs holds every assumption (blue) with its source; the yellow cells are the levers: β, the 38% contestable share, mix drift, and the Airbnb ADR path.
  2. Link the Airbnb ADR growth row on Inputs to the ADR line in the revenue model, so the same ADR drives both price and share.
  3. Replace the North America / U.S. nights growth plug with Projection!“U.S. AIRBNB NIGHTS” (or its growth row). ADR, take rate and FX stay exactly where they are; the sheet’s revenue rows are only a check (2025 U.S. revenue reproduces at $5.0B vs ~$4.8B reported, the gap being the revenue-share proxy for U.S. nights).
  4. For EMEA / LatAm / APAC keep the team’s growth assumption for now, or repeat the calibration with that region’s hotel room nights (Eurostat has EU platform vs hotel nights — Krishang’s file).
  5. Scenario the thesis: bear = Airbnb ADR +2–4 pts above hotels with β 2.5–4.3 (nights CAGR 1–2%); bull = ADR parity or below with mix drift +2 pts and a positive product shift (nights CAGR 4–5%).

What it says for a long

Only 38% of nights are contestable, and within that pool Airbnb’s share is 2–4% in solo/pair but 11–15% in 3+ parties — the segments growing fastest. Mix and category adoption add ~3 pts a year before any share gain, so nights compound even if hotels hold price.

What it says for a short

Airbnb ADR has outgrown hotel ADR for three years. At β = 2.5 every extra point of ADR premium growth costs 0.7 pts of nights; the current +1.9-pt gap is worth −1.6 pts, i.e. nights growth in the U.S. depends on the 62% own-category pool holding up while price pushes contestable parties back to hotels.

Caveats

  • U.S. Airbnb nights are proxied from revenue share; a 5-pt error in the U.S. share moves every Pg proportionally but not the growth decomposition.
  • The 38% substitution rate is from 2014 guest surveys in large cities; at peak periods it was 13%. It is the single most important lever and is deliberately exposed on Inputs.
  • β is a share-response, not a demand elasticity; the −4.27 is an upper bound because it includes not travelling and switching cities.
  • Hotel party-nights are derived, not observed: room nights are converted with the leisure party mix and rooms-per-party assumptions. STR/Kalibri party-level data would replace this.
  • No supply side: Airbnb listing growth (+4.6% STR supply in 2026) and regulation enter only through the product-shift lever.
