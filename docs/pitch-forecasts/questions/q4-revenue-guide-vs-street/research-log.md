## 0b. Question (verbatim)
### Title
Will Airbnb's 4Q26 revenue guidance midpoint, given at the 5 Nov print, be below the Street's 4Q26 revenue consensus mean?
### Resolution Criteria
Yes if the midpoint of the 4Q26 revenue range in the 3Q26 shareholder letter is strictly below the LSEG-family 4Q26 revenue consensus mean recorded on 4 Nov 2026 (register fallback: Yahoo/LSEG mean captured that day). If only a growth-rate range is given, convert on 4Q25 revenue $2,778M. If no 4Q26 revenue guidance is given, resolves No.
### Fine Print
The consensus used is the revenue mean, not the Street's implied guide. Note in the log the separate P(midpoint below Street minus the trailing-8 cushion), which is the "guide surprise" object, but the headline resolves on the raw mean. Resolution date 5 Nov 2026.

Conventions adopted for ambiguities (registry conventions apply: "Street" = LSEG-family mean at the close of the last trading day before the print, from `L0_vintage_register.csv`, fallback the Yahoo/LSEG mean captured that day): (1) "midpoint" = arithmetic mean of the two dollar endpoints as printed ($X.XX bn, so the midpoint sits on a $5M grid); (2) if the letter prints both a dollar range and a growth range, the dollar range governs; (3) the Street mean is used unrounded to the dollar, so a tie is a measure-zero event; (4) the Zacks $3,200M panel is not the resolution source and is carried only as a sensitivity; (5) [rev 2, A01-01] the guidance outcome has three branches, not two: a dollar range (resolvable as printed), a growth-only range (resolvable after conversion on $2,778M: integer-percent endpoints, so the converted midpoint sits on a 0.5pt = $13.9M grid), and no 4Q26 revenue guidance at all (resolves No); only the third branch is excluded from the continuous object; (6) [rev 2, A01-02] the historical pre-guide Street values in the register are morning-of-print quotes and are used as proxies for C01's previous-close convention.

## 9. Audit reconciliation (Astra 0.62 vs revision 2 0.72)
Astra's analytic comparison (claim 29) and this model agree to within 0.2 points when run on the same conventions (0.624 vs 0.626), so the 10-point gap is three named choices, not arithmetic:
1. **Kernel calibration (validated, +0.06).** Astra scales the guide error by 3.03% — the COVID-inclusive `last3` RMSE 2.86% in quadrature with the cushion sd — and then adds GBV uncertainty on top. The kernel note's own conclusion is that the COVID cells are the bias and the calibration failure (KS p 0.0027 → 0.63 when dropped), that `last3_ex_covid` (RMSE 2.05%, bias +0.19%) is "the defensible PIT claim", and that the three live Q4 cells are ex-COVID. Component C keeps Astra's 2.86% so the conservative convention still carries 25% of the mixture.
2. **Q4 cushion seasonality (judgment with a check, +0.06).** Astra uses the all-quarter trailing-8 1.86%. Q4 guides have run 2.7–3.3% for three years (Welch t ≈ 2.8 against the other seasons) and the G4 backtest's own three Q4 cells show the 1.86%-cushion model over-predicting the Q4 guide by +0.53% on average — the blend's premium. n = 3 either way; hence the 50/50 blend rather than 3.04.
3. **RNPL leakage (judgment, +0.04).** Astra sets the residual mean to zero. The 10-Q says RNPL bookings cancel more and de-correlate GBV from revenue; K1 sizes it at 0.17–1.39% of the base but 1H26 does not show it. P = 0.40 at the central cell.
Decision: hold 0.72 rather than compress to Astra's 0.62. Items 1 is a repo-validated asymmetry; items 2 and 3 are judgments that together move 10 points and are each carried at half weight already. If the September dumps re-centre the nights band at or above 10.5, or the 4Q26 mean falls below $3,140M, the number falls into Astra's interval without any further judgment.
