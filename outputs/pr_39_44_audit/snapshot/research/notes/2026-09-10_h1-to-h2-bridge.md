> before using it. The later audit found that Q1's approximately 3-point growth
> contribution was RNPL plus cancellation-policy changes plus simplified fees,
> not RNPL alone. The 1.5–3-point RNPL haircuts below are scenarios, not measured
> cancellation effects; 10–12 is a mapping of qualitative guidance. The later
> calendar pilot neither validates these haircuts nor rules out the research
> hypothesis. Treat the body’s projections, causal attributions, and strong negative
> research judgments as **unvalidated exploratory interpretations**, not established
> conclusions. In particular, claims such as “never regulation or wars,” “no scenario,”

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The H1 to H2 transition is stable for the two things that matter and useless for everything else.** Nights y/y moves by -0.3 pts from the H1 average to Q3 and +0.9 pts to Q4 (2023 to 2025; ranges -1.3 to +1.1 and -2.8 to +3.3). ADR ex-FX moves -0.5 and -0.2 pts. Revenue growth, FX, take rate and margin have no usable H1 to H2 pattern: their transitions are driven by calendar (Easter, Leap Day), the dollar, fee changes and marketing timing, all of which are better forecast directly.
2. **Applied to 2026, the pattern alone puts Q3 nights at ~9.5% and Q4 at ~10.6%.** Management guided Q3 to "low double digits" (10 to 12). The gap between the pattern and the guide is the RNPL lap: management said about 3 points of 1Q26 nights growth came from RNPL and that the US comparison turns tough from Q3. If none of that laps (international adoption offsets the US anniversary), Q3 lands at 10.0, the bottom of the guide, and *decelerates* against Q2's 10.3. If half laps, 8.5. If all of it laps, 7.0. **No scenario in this bridge produces a Q3 nights acceleration.** The reaction-function base rate for "nights decelerating, margin met" is -2.1% day one (11 prints, 18% positive).
3. **Revenue dollars are pinned down by a different mechanism and look fine.** Revenue is recognised at check-in, so Q3 revenue is mostly Q1/Q2 bookings. The conversion of lagged GBV (two thirds prior quarter, one third two quarters back) to revenue has been 17.4%, 17.1%, 17.2% for the last three Q3s and 12.0%, 12.1%, 12.0% for Q4s. That gives **3Q26 revenue $4.78B to $4.85B (point $4.80B, +17%)**, above the $4.69B to $4.77B guide and the $4.74B consensus, consistent with the 19-of-19 beat record (Q3 actuals average +1.3% over the midpoint). **4Q26 revenue $3.14B to $3.19B (+14%)** against $3.20B consensus. So the Q3 print itself is not the risk. The Q4 guide and the nights line are.
4. **The Q4 revenue guide is likely to print below today's consensus by arithmetic alone.** Q4 actuals have beaten the midpoint by 3.2% on average since 2022 (3.9% including 2021). A $3.17B outcome therefore implies a November guide midpoint near $3.05B to $3.07B, 4% under the $3.20B Zacks mean (10 estimates, range $3.05B to $3.70B, so the mean is skewed by one high estimate; the median is probably nearer $3.15B). Point-in-time consensus is still the open data gap.
5. **Reported growth decelerates from Q3 to Q4 for knowable reasons.** FX adds about 3 points to Q3 revenue growth (management's number) and, at a flat dollar, roughly 1 to 3 points to Q4. FX on ADR, fitted from the broad dollar index, is +1.1 in Q3 and +2.1 in Q4 at a flat spot, against +5.0 and +1.3 in H1. Hotels and services dilute ADR by about 0.5 points. Reported ADR growth converges toward the 3 to 3.5% ex-FX run rate.
6. **On the "what breaks the pattern" question: in five years of prints, the H2 breaks that mattered were FX, laps, and product, never regulation or wars.** Every external shock in the catalogue (Delta, Omicron, Ukraine, Israel-Gaza, Israel-Iran, hurricanes, the 2025 shutdown, Local Law 18, the Middle East in 2026) either left no visible mark on nights or cost about 1 point. The only exogenous event that broke the pattern at scale was COVID itself. The breaks that moved the stock 8 to 13% were the 2022 dollar surge (-7 points of revenue growth, visible in spot FX), the Omicron lap in 2Q23 (pre-announced by management and still -11%), the 2Q24 lead-time warning (internal data, invisible from outside), and the 2025 RNPL step-up (launch public, size not). The lesson for 2H26 is to price the RNPL lap, the World Cup comp and FX, and to treat the EU housing act, the host-fee pilot, the midterms and the hurricane season as headline risks with a base rate near zero on the KPIs.

## 2. Method

For each KPI and year Y: `T3(Y) = KPI(3Q,Y) - mean(KPI(1Q,Y), KPI(2Q,Y))` and the same for Q4. The base rate is the mean transition over 2023 to 2025 (n = 3). 2022 is shown but excluded (H1 2022 y/y is Omicron-distorted: nights +59% and +25%). 2021 is reopening and dropped. The 2026 projection is the 2026 H1 mean plus the base-rate transition, then explicit overlays for known step changes. Each historical year's transition is also compared with the mean of the *other* clean years (leave-one-out), and residuals above a threshold (2 points for growth rates, 1 for ADR terms) are flagged and joined to the event catalogue.

With three clean years the base rates are descriptive. The transition ranges in the tables are the honest uncertainty.

The guide was issued on 6 August with July bookings in hand, so management's "low double digits" already contains its view of the RNPL lap and the World Cup residual. For the guide to hold, the H1 to Q3 transition has to sit at the top of its range and the lap has to cost nothing. That is possible: 2025's Q3 transition was +1.1 with RNPL launching. It is not the base case. The pre-registered card item is therefore: **Q3 nights at or above 10.5% is the surprise; 10.0 or below is the pattern.**

The FX fit reproduces 1Q26 (+5.3 fitted, +5.0 disclosed) but overshoots 2Q26 (+2.3 fitted, +1.3 disclosed), so the Q3 and Q4 ADR FX figures carry about a point of model error. Revenue FX is hedged and lags irregularly (revenue FX 0, 1, 3, 4 over the last four quarters against ADR FX 2.7, 2.9, 5.0, 1.3), which is why the Q4 revenue FX figure is a stated 1 to 3 range rather than a formula.

## 5. Where the pattern broke, and whether it was visible in advance

Flagged leave-one-out deviations in the clean years (full list in `h2_bridge_deviations.csv`; 2022 flags are reopening and omitted here):

| Quarter | KPI | Pattern implied | Actual | Deviation | Cause | Visible before the quarter? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4Q23 | Nights y/y | 17.5 | 12.0 | -5.5 | Reopening fade through 2023, Israel-Gaza cancellations from 7 Oct, APAC -15 vs pattern (China base) | Comps yes; war no |
| 4Q23 | ADR ex-FX | 3.3 | 0.5 | -2.8 | Mix to lower-ADR regions as APAC and LatAm recovered | Yes (management flagged regional mix all year) |

| 3Q25, 4Q25 | ADR ex-FX | -0.3 | 2.0, 3.0 | +2.3, +3.3 | RNPL: longer lead times, larger homes, NA ADR +5 | Launch public (May 2025); size not |

| 3Q25, 4Q25 | Adj. EBITDA margin | 54.4, 33.2 | 50.1, 28.3 | -4.3, -4.9 | Deliberate S&M and product reinvestment | Yes, pre-flagged in every 2025 letter |

Three observations. First, the sign of the nights break has been set by the *comp* twice (2Q23 Omicron lap, 4Q24 war lap) and by product once (2025 RNPL). Second, the two breaks that were entirely invisible from outside were both lead-time changes (2Q24 down, 2025 up), which is the one series Airbnb never discloses numerically. Third, the margin breaks were all pre-announced and still produced 8 to 13% down days (Q3'24) because the market priced the guide, not the letter.

## 7. What this changes for the 5 November card

- **Revenue:** expect $4.78B to $4.85B, above the top of the range. A miss here would be the first in 19 quarters and would itself be the story.
- **Nights:** the pattern says 8.5 to 10.0 depending on the RNPL lap; the guide says 10 to 12; Q2 was 10.3. The acceleration sign is the trade. Price a deceleration as the base case unless Hilton and Marriott RevPAR accelerate in late October.
- **ADR reported:** ~4% (3% ex-FX plus ~1 point FX), down from 5.3% in Q2 and 9.0% in Q1. Anyone extrapolating H1 ADR growth is wrong by a knowable amount.
- **Q4 guide:** a revenue midpoint near $3.05B to $3.10B is the arithmetic outcome, below the current $3.20B consensus mean. Whether that reads as a "light guide" depends on where consensus sits on 5 November, which needs the point-in-time Bloomberg pull.
- **Margin:** not this note. The pattern approach fails for margin; use WS31 and the predictive nowcast (49.1 to 49.8 against a 50.1 ceiling).

## 8. Caveats

- Three clean years. The ranges are the uncertainty; the means are not estimates in any statistical sense.
- The RNPL lap size is an assumption in three scenarios. Management's "~3 pts" was a 1Q26 statement about US RNPL; international rollout continues through 2026 and could offset part of the US anniversary.
- The FX-to-ADR fit is a single-factor regression on the broad dollar with about 0.6 points of leave-one-out error and a 1-point overshoot in 2Q26. The hedging program makes revenue FX less predictable than ADR FX.
- The GBV-lag revenue conversion assumes take rate flat y/y, which is what management guided. The host-fee pilot (Aug 2026) is the risk to that.
- The event catalogue is hand-coded from the letters and the catalyst calendar; effects are as management stated them, and management only quantified two (Middle East -1 pt in 1Q26, RNPL +3 pts). The 2026 shutdown risk and the Middle East air-capacity situation were not re-verified against news for this note.
