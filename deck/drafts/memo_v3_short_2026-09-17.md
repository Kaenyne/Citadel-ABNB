**Recommendation:** SHORT ABNB, target $143 (−15%) over the 5 Nov print and the 4Q26 guide, probability-weighted $148. The stock is priced for an accelerating 3Q26 nights print and a FY27 in which 2026's growth rate repeats. Our work says 2026's re-acceleration was borrowed: a three-feature product bundle (Reserve Now Pay Later, a cancellation-policy redesign, the single host fee), a 3–4 point FX tailwind and a World Cup booking pull-forward, stacked on a business that was decelerating toward 7%. The legs anniversary between 3Q26 and 2Q27, the cost base is budgeted for the growth that is lapping, and the first visible test is 5 Nov: a decelerating nights print against a Street bar that assumes acceleration, and a 4Q26 guide below the Street.

## The story in one paragraph

Airbnb was a healthy business slowing the way large platforms slow. Nights growth ran +9.5% / +8.7% / +8.5% through 1Q–3Q24 and +7.9% / +7.4% in 1H25; North America, 29% of nights, was growing +2%. In August 2025 management launched Reserve Now Pay Later (book with $0 down, pay just before the free-cancellation window closes), in October it retired the Strict cancellation policy and added 14-day free cancellation, and from October it began moving hosts to a single 15.5% fee with no guest fee. Each feature converts more browsers into bookers; management tested that the booking lift exceeds the extra cancellations by check-in and says the realised curves matched the tests. The stack worked: North America went +2% → +5% (3Q25) → +8% (1Q26), and total nights re-accelerated to +8.8% / +9.8% / +9.2% / +10.3% through 2Q26. Management put a number on it twice: "over 200 basis points of growth in nights" in 4Q25 and "approximately 3 points" in 1Q26, then gave no figure in 2Q26 and moved to quoting RNPL's share of GBV instead. A company that stops quantifying a driver at the peak of its contribution is usually telling you the number has stopped flattering. On top of the bundle, 2Q26 carried the World Cup: management called it the largest event in Airbnb's history, said event bookings cluster close to the games, never sized it, and framed the quarter as "no single product". The revenue line carried +3 and +4 points of FX in 1Q26 and 2Q26 on top of all of that. **Strip the bundle (about 3 points), the World Cup bookings and FX out of 2Q26 and the underlying business is a 6–7% grower.** From 3Q26 the pieces come off one by one: US RNPL laps in 3Q26, the global cancellation and fee legs in 4Q26 together with FX flipping from +3 to about +1 and then negative, ex-NA RNPL in 1Q27 against a +17.9% comp, and the World Cup in 2Q27. The Street runs FY27 flat at +11% every quarter.

**RNPL: eligibility widened faster than the share grew** (`overnight2/D` ledger; eligible share derived from geography × policy mix, RNPL share disclosed where marked)

| | Who was eligible | Eligible share of GBV | RNPL % of GBV | Management's growth attribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 4Q25 | same, full quarter; Oct cancellation redesign; up-funnel merchandising | 15–18% | ~9% (assumed) | **"over 200bp of nights, ~300bp of GBV"** |
| 1Q26 | global ex BRL/INR/TRY from 17 Feb–4 Mar; US cross-border | 25–35% | **~20% (disclosed)** | **"approximately 3 points of nights, 4 of GBV"** |
| 2Q26 | first full global quarter | 45–55% | **"over 20%" (disclosed)** | **none given**; share metric instead |
| 3Q26E | July: eligible booking types expanded (unnamed) | 50–60% | 21–25% (module) | 5 Nov tell: ≥2.5pt restated weakens us; none or ≤1.5 supports |

Adoption among those offered it is about 70% in the US. A pool that roughly tripled while the share moved from "roughly 20%" to "over 20%" says the product is near its ceiling. That matters because the gross booking lift scales with the *flow* of new RNPL bookings, which has flattened, while cancellations scale with the *stock* of live unpaid bookings reaching their payment deadline, which is still growing; 46–49% of a quarter's excess cancellations arrive from earlier booking cohorts. That is the configuration in which the net contribution turns, and reported Nights and Seats Booked subtracts a cancellation in the quarter it occurs, not the quarter it was booked. We size the cancellation drag honestly: the cohort engine puts it at −0.2 to −0.9 points of 3Q26 nights, and the level anniversary is two to fifteen times larger. The thesis is the lap; the cancellation tail is what keeps it from being a one-quarter event.

## Why nights and the guide, and how we built our numbers

Since 2023 ABNB has been an earnings-day stock: 12 of the last 14 moves of 7% or more were prints or macro shocks, and the prints key off the **nights guide**, not the reported quarter. 3Q22, 1Q23, 2Q24 and 2Q25 all beat on revenue and EPS and fell 8–13% on nights or lead-time commentary; 4Q24 rose 14% on a nights beat with a revenue guide below the Street. Since mid-2022, prints that paired a Q4-guide-below-Street with a downgraded nights direction fell on the day in 4 of 5 cases (median −10.9%; 1Q25 +1.0% the exception), a historical base rate; the run's joint reaction model puts the same cell at a median of −5.1% with P(≤ −8%) 0.37 (S01 rev 2). Revenue and EPS surprises carry no measurable reaction, and Airbnb has beaten its own revenue guide midpoint 19 of 19 times, so the *level* of a guided quarter is not tradeable. We therefore put the alternative-data work on the two things that move the stock: the 3Q26 nights print and the 4Q26 guide.

**Nights.** Our 3Q26 nights read is +9.5% (146.3m, band 8.5–10.0; the pre-alt-data model path of +9.9% is the top of the band), from three independent routes that agree. (1) A review-date stays index built from 363 Inside Airbnb review dumps across 123 markets (review dates 2018 to 17 Aug 2026), corrected for the 15–16% of reviews that vanish per year of dump age; it is the first team-built series that beats a naive nights forecast out of sample (walk-forward RMSE 0.68x the naive; NTTO inbound arrivals corroborate at 0.72–0.74x) and it reads +9.5 to +10.0%. Same-listing review volume is −3% to −7% y/y; all growth is from listings under a year old. (2) An external stack of 12 knowable survivors from 40 ranked sources (STR weekly hotel RevPAR, which fades from +8.2% in July to +4% by mid-August; NTTO I-94 arrivals; Eurostat, BLS, INE; EUROCONTROL daily flights for EMEA) reads +9.2%. (3) Booking-pace from 328 calendar snapshots across 34 markets runs flat-to-slightly-up on 2025 and is used as a direction check only, because it failed its backtest. Behind these sit a consumer-relative-strength index across 28 origin countries (US consumer the weakest of the large origins; backdrop, not an input), 1,159 vintage-stamped consensus rows so every backtest is point-in-time, and a catalogue of 1,773 open-source travel repositories screened for signal.

**Revenue and the guide.** Airbnb's revenue is converted, not forecast: revenue_q = λ_season × [⅔·GBV_{q−1} + ⅓·GBV_{q−2}], with λ_Q4 = 12.03% and a within-season range of 0.17pp over three years. GBV_3Q26 prints the morning management guides Q4, so the 4Q26 guide is arithmetic on our 3Q26 GBV (nights × ADR): **implied guide midpoint $3,05–3,10bn against a Street revenue mean of $3,157–3,162M**, and a nights bucket that drops from "low double digits" to "high single digits" (our 4Q26 nights +8.1–8.9% vs the Street's +9.9%). 3Q26 revenue itself is a beat on our numbers ($4,804M vs $4,744M Street) on FX and a full RNPL quarter; we say so and do not trade it.

**How we know the view is variant.** Bloomberg MODL, 12 Sep: 28 estimates for 3Q26 nights, low 147.0m (+10.0%), mean 148.9m (+11.5%), high 151m. Ours is 146.3m (+9.5%), below the lowest, while our ADR ($176.9, +3.3%) sits on the consensus mean ($177.06, +3.4%) and our take rate is inside the range. The whole disagreement is nights. The Street's bar implies acceleration for only the fourth time in 16 prints, and it is management's guide carried forward one-for-one after the 2Q26 beat (same sign as the guide in 12 of 13 guided prints). So the call is that management misses a nights guide to the downside for the first time in the sample, and we say that before the judge does. The run prices it: P(3Q26 nights ≥ +10.0%) 0.39 on the nowcast distribution (R01; 0.57–0.82 if management's delivery record is taken as the object), P(≥ +10.6%, an acceleration) 0.26 (R02), P(4Q26 guide midpoint below the Street mean) 0.72 (C01; midpoint p50 $3,100M), P(the 4Q26 descriptor drops from "low double digits") 0.61 (C02).

## Thesis

**1. Volume: the lap starts in 3Q26 and compounds through 1H27.** Bundle contribution ~3 points (management), FX +3–4 points on revenue in 1H26, World Cup bookings in 2Q26. US RNPL laps 3Q26 (partial), the global cancellation and fee legs 4Q26, ex-NA RNPL 1Q27, the World Cup 2Q27. RNPL-aware FY27 nights are +6.4% (global lap) to +8.2% (NA-only), against the +8–9% the price carries and management's delivered +10%. 1Q27 guides against a +17.9% comp; 2Q27 against +16.5%.

**2. Price: geographic mix is a structural drag and the term that has been carrying ADR is fading.** Reported ADR ran +9.0% in 1Q26 and +5.3% in 2Q26, of which FX was +3–4 points and an unobserved like-for-like pricing residual +4.4 to +4.9 points, against 2–3.5 points in 2023–25. Underneath, geographic mix is −1.4 to −1.5pp a year: North America (highest ADR) grows +6–8% while LatAm grows +16–20% and APAC +15–17% on paid growth initiatives in emerging markets (1H26 10-Q), nine new expansion markets, US inbound weakness (AirDNA US international inbound STR demand −12%, Canada −32%, July 2026, on visa fees, tariffs and the Canadian boycott) and India as the fastest origin (+60%). Hotels (growing ~3x homes) and Experiences seats dilute a further −0.5pp. Bigger homes add back +0.6–0.8pp (measured bedroom elasticity 0.23, not the 1:1 the Street uses). For 3Q26 our ADR is on consensus; the price leg lands from 4Q26, when FX turns (ADR FX −0.4 to +0.2, revenue FX +3 → +1 → −0.6), hotel ADR growth has already collapsed to +0.6% (STR, mid-August; CPI lodging 4.9% → 3.1%), and the residual has to persist at 1H26 levels for the Street's ADR.

**3. Costs: budgeted for the growth that is lapping, and management cuts less readily than it spends.** Built from 10-Q component disclosure alone, 3Q26 costs grow 11.5% and the quarter prints 52.4%; management's "margin down slightly vs Q3 2025" means it has chosen to spend about $97M more, in brand marketing and AI hosting (`40_line_build.md`). S&M goes from 19.4% of revenue (FY25) to 21.3% (FY26E) to 21.9–23.5% (FY27E); the Street's FY27 margin of 36.5% implies a 43.7% incremental margin that needs the ramp to stop, against our 24.7–34.8%. Cash-cost elasticity to revenue is 0.36 and asymmetric (k_down < k_up, p < 0.02): a revenue miss costs 0.35–0.6pp of FY27 margin per point, and the FY26 "at least 35.5%" floor breaks on a 2H26 revenue shortfall of 0.6–0.9% ($50–75M). Adjusted EBITDA excludes $1.8bn of FY26 SBC (35% of EBITDA); on SBC-adjusted FCF the stock yields ~3% and the price needs 16% FCF growth on a reverse DCF.
