Quarterly levels don't correlate at all (r = –0.09): TSA peaks in Q3 when people fly; Airbnb nights booked peak in Q1 when people book.

What it means

The strong full-period correlation is an artifact of 2023: everything travel-related was decelerating from post-COVID reopening at once. Strip that out and air traffic has no explanatory power for Airbnb since 2024. US screenings have been flat-to-down for six quarters, international air demand turned negative in Q2 2026 on the Middle East war, US inbound arrivals have fallen for most of 2025–26 — and Airbnb nights growth held 7–10% and GBV accelerated to +16–19%.

So Airbnb is not an air-travel beta. Its growth is share gain and mix (first-time bookers, hotels, expansion markets, longer/large-group stays), not more people flying. For the pitch: don't use flight data as a demand signal — use it to show decoupling. The risk framing flips too: if air travel recovers in 2027, Airbnb doesn't necessarily get a tailwind; if it weakens further, the last ten quarters say Airbnb keeps growing anyway.
