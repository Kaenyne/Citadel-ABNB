## Economic identities fixed before review

Define A[b,t] as recognized Airbnb fee dollars in recognition quarter t attributable to a consistently defined original booking cohort b. Backward composition is A[b,t] / R[t] when the fee matrix reconciles to corporate revenue. An explicit unallocated bridge U[t] must satisfy R[t] = sum_b A[b,t] + U[t]. Row-wise forward conversion is A[b,t] / V[b], where V[b] is a compatible original-cohort denominator. Its sum over t is neither a probability nor necessarily a mature full-cohort rate: future quarters may be censored and the denominator may represent monetary value rather than reservations. Booking, check-in and recognition dates can differ, particularly for long stays.

If only reported net quarterly GBV B[b] and corporate revenue R[t] are available, A[b,t] = beta[season(t),t-b] * B[b] defines an effective accounting allocation under assumptions. This does not make A observed or beta a physical completion/survival rate. Corporate B includes modifications/cancellations recorded in the reporting period, including effects from old booking cohorts; it is not the clean original V. Do not subtract a generic cancellation haircut a second time.

Column-normalized backward shares and booking-row forward rates must derive from the same dollar matrix. Normalized regression coefficients alone are not backward shares; unequal booking-quarter levels matter. Finite lag support is an assumption. Keep the 3+ tail, pre-sample bookings, future recognition, incomplete rows and any residual bridge visible. Renormalizing the observed 0/1/2 lags to 100% hides the tail.

Low variance of an aggregate conversion ratio can coexist with offsetting and poorly identified cohort allocations. For jointly varying components X, total variance is the sum of component variances plus twice each pair covariance. Conditional fit dispersion, sensitivity envelopes, sampling uncertainty, model-selection uncertainty, and actual reservation survival variation are different objects. A bootstrap of aggregate quarters cannot turn latent allocations into observed physical cohorts.

K2 describes historical reconstructed Melbourne accommodation value by check-in period. It is stale, one-city evidence with failed truncation-recovery diagnostics and no newly available raw reservation reconstruction. It cannot be called current corporate fee-revenue shares, RNPL-specific behavior, or a time series measuring current quarter-to-quarter cohort variance. Any K2 sensitivity must restrict the appropriate backward matrix shares, with original local month labels, rather than directly substitute shares for forward beta coefficients. Its bounds are assumption/sensitivity bounds, not confidence intervals.

## Gates and test plan

1. **Accounting integrity:** independent matrix arithmetic must reconcile column revenue plus the residual bridge; backward and forward normalization must agree with their distinct denominators. Test unequal cohort sizes, nonzero tails, missing future outcomes, zeros and invalid negative/non-finite inputs. Full-share claims require visible coverage rather than conditional renormalization.
2. **Identification:** inspect parameter count, design rank/conditioning and multiple materially different feasible allocations. Quant's preregistered 10-percentage-point share-envelope and 5-point deletion-stability limits govern its proposed precision claim; a good revenue fit alone does not pass them. Any fitted bound conditional on beta positivity, lag support, error tolerance or K2 remains explicitly conditional.
3. **Chronology:** target-quarter and current as-yet-unpublished GBV must not enter pre-event forecasts. Training cutoff, source publication dates and recursively forecast lag inputs must be inspectable per origin. Poison unpublished GBV/revenue and verify historical pre-event forecasts do not change; oracle inputs must affect only separately labelled diagnostics. Matching baselines must use identical forecast origins, training eligibility and outcomes; abstentions remain visible.
4. **Variance:** independently recompute component covariance and the total-variance identity. Test perfectly offsetting components, where nonzero component variances sum to zero total variance after covariance. Inspect any bootstrap/resampling for whole-row/quarter coherence; never treat independently sampled components as the observed joint distribution. Report actual independent quarterly n, not reservation count as quarterly n.
5. **Cancellations/fees/tail:** reject claims of physical survival based on reported net GBV or accommodation value; distinguish empirical data absence from a failed predictive test. Tail-cut and cancellation assumptions must remain explicit sensitivity dimensions, not discovered physical parameters. Missing direct fees cannot be replaced with a precise observed fee-share label.
6. **Alternative-data eligibility:** independently bind PR60 commit ancestry and the data agent's field/vintage inventory. Calendar availability is not observed booking; reviews/stays do not reveal original booking time. Stale or revised snapshots cannot become PIT predictors merely because a file exists now. Excluded features retain source/reason labels. No pre-event feature admitted without a defensible as-of rule.
7. **Predictive validation:** apply the quant preregistration exactly on common W1/W2 rows, including MAE/RMSE/bias and every year deletion. Separate research fit, forecast accuracy and economic identification. A failing promotion rule remains FAIL; absence of necessary data is UNAVAILABLE; a completed bounded audit can PASS engineering while neither promotion nor physical identification passes.
8. **Pitch eligibility:** observed aggregate financial ratios may be used with season/sample/date labels. Effective-cohort model diagrams and assumption-conditioned envelopes may be used as explanatory research only if their labels survive review. Actual current corporate booking shares, physical survival/cancellation rates, RNPL causality and new forecast/trading edge require their own evidence; no such claim is inferred from model fit or low variance.
